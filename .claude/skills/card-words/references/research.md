# Where the principles come from

Brief notes, gathered Oct 24, 2026. Our own playtests outrank all of this.

- **Same function, same words.** Magic's templating keeps identical effects in identical words, so a different wording tells players the effect differs. Clear, understandable sentences outrank brevity, and strict templating can make simple cards wordy, so weigh it card by card. (RulesFest, "Templating and Why it's Useful", slugfestgames.com/?p=1469; goodgamery.com/?p=3635)
- **Retire a confusing term everywhere at once.** Magic replaced "converted mana cost" with "mana value" across every card, keeping the meaning. (belloflostsouls.net/?p=368324) We did the same with "get rid of" → "cast out".
- **Test readability on its own.** Gut Check's designers playtested mechanics, then card readability, then errors, as separate passes. Proofreaders stop rechecking a card that hasn't changed, so errors hide in old cards. (entrogames.substack.com, "Can your game pass these six design tests?") Our /review-text exists for the second point.
- **Icons only for a small vocabulary.** About five actions and seven objects at most; a look-alike icon gets misread every time. (bgdf.com, "iconography vs quick reference vs text") Our taste rule: words beat symbols, except the resource dot and the follower mark.
- **Punctuation parses.** Effects stacked with no breaks read as one run-on sentence. (forums.greaterthangames.com) Our hovers put each effect on its own line.
- **Worked examples and a fresh reader.** The best rulebooks teach through one full example turn, and the test is whether a new player can learn without the designer present. (entrogames.substack.com, "How to Write a Rulebook…"; meeplemountain.com, "Top six rules for writing rulebooks")
- **Plain, direct language.** No flowery wording, no humor in rules, no repetition that adds bulk without understanding. (herotime1.com, "How to Write a Rulebook for Your Board Game")
- **Skills.** A skill's description carries all of "when to use"; the body stays lean and points to references read on demand; examples and the why beat rigid MUSTs. (anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills; the skill-creator guide)
