# The design core: how Cults plays, and what makes a space land

Read once per thread, with the cheatsheet. This is the positive half: what the game is about, the arcs every space sits in, what each color's player is trying to do, and why the spaces Cory loved worked. Use it to *generate*; use the cheatsheet's kill list to *cut*. Facts are from the live rules and decisions.md; readings are marked as Claude's.

## 1. The game in one breath

You lead a cult for three rounds. Each turn you put a minion somewhere others now can't, take what the space gives, and turn some of what you hold into members or followers. Every turn asks three questions at once:

- **Where?** Spaces are shared and snake-drafted. "Will my spot still be open?" is the heartbeat (Cory). Blocking is real play.
- **Hold or spend?** Each color's contest pays whoever holds the most of it at round end (+2 / +4 / +6). A resource held wins contests; a resource spent becomes a member or followers. Theft and gifts move piles, so a big pile is both a lead and a target.
- **What do I turn it into?** One recruit a turn: a member (an ability, worth followers, a bet that should return about 1.5× its cost) or one follower card (1, 5 or 10 at 1 for 1).

## 2. The arcs a space sits in

**Economy arc.** Round I is poor: 1 to 4 resources, members cost 2 to 10, the recruit is often wasted. Round II is the engine: members start paying, piles grow, the middle ring opens. Round III is the burst: piles are big, 5 minions, and the recruit is the bottleneck, because 10 resources need a whole recruit to become 10 followers. The most exciting round III spaces break that bottleneck: Spree (recruit 3 times), Recruit a Member (free), Poach (take one), White's cash-in (all your white into followers, no cap, no recruit), Greatest Hits (copy every action with your minion). Earlier spaces should feel like they're building toward the burst: collecting fuel, setting up a member engine, or positioning for round III.

**Timing arc.** Snake order means the ends take two turns in a row; from round II the player with the fewest followers goes first, so falling behind buys tempo. Round I fills every space; rounds II and III each have exactly one spare space per color, so late placements choose among scraps. Order is a resource: going early grabs the contested space, going last can be paid for (Last Call), and making someone wait (Hold Up, Sleep In) is a sting everyone understands. Minions left in your column is a visible count everyone can read.

**Contest arc.** Every contest is visible state: anyone can see who leads each rim. Rounds I and II contests are cheap for whoever isn't spending; round III is a cliff, since cashing out empties your pile right before the 6-follower prize. A space that pushes a player across that cliff (spend everything now, or hold to win) creates the best round III decisions.

**Interaction arc.** Most interaction is through the board: taking a space, copying it, buying the member someone wanted. Direct harm (steals, swaps, skips, Poach) is fine when it's mild early and big only in round III, and when the victim keeps a choice or a defense (Shake Down, Lie Low, Bodyguard, Loyalists, Snitch).

## 3. Each color: the player's fantasy, how they win, who they rub against

Write a space so that the player taking it feels like this sentence.

- **Red, the recruiter.** "I get members without spending my recruit, and I trade up." Wins on free recruits (Open Door, Recruit a Member) that dodge the one-recruit limit, and on swapping up the cost ladder (Promote). Rubs against Blue and Magenta: all three treat the shop and members as the board. A Red player hates Loyalists and loves a deep, rich shop row.
- **Green, the echo.** "Whatever the best play was, I get it too." Wins by riding the strongest spaces of the round, with chains paying green at every link. Lives off everyone else's spaces: every big space you design is also a Green space. Agents and Bait are its brakes.
- **Magenta, the thief.** "Your pile is my pile." Wins by taking half a hoard (Crime Wave) and members (Shake Down, Poach). The natural predator of any color whose plan is to hold a big pile (White, Gold), and the reason hoarding has a cost. Lie Low and Bodyguard are the walls.
- **Blue, the shopper.** "I shape the row, then I buy a lot of it." Wins on extra recruits late (Recruit Twice, Spree) and on churning the row until the right members show (Churn, Out of Style, Peek, Bury). Competes with Red for the shop's best cards.
- **Purple, the clock.** "I act when it matters most." Wins by bending when turns happen: two turns now (Crunch Time), a turn right after this one (Right Away), going last for followers (Last Call), making the next turn get skipped (Hold Up), placing on taken spaces in round III (Crowd In). Everyone at the table feels Purple, because it changes the order they play in.
- **White, the exchange.** "I bank white and cash it in past the limit." Wins by building a white pile and converting it in round III with no cap and no recruit, plus selling a spent member for white equal to its cost. Its tension: the white contest rewards holding, the cash-in rewards emptying, and Magenta wants the pile.
- **Yellow, the gatherer.** "I have every color." Wins by holding many colors, which pay any member's colored part and reach every contest. Feeds off the table's variety; strongest when the shop is mixed.
- **Gold, the doubler.** "My pile grows on its own." Double Down in round III; a visible gold bank is Cory's lean. Shares the hoard tension with White, and Magenta preys on it.
- **Silver, the host.** "I give, and the giving comes back." Rising Tide is the model: hand rivals a little, then take a share of the biggest pile. Its tension is that gifts feed rivals' contests.

## 4. What made his favorite spaces land

Each line names the card, his words, and the reason it worked. Generate new ideas by reusing the *reason*, never the card.

- **Echo**, "Copy the previous action": "absolutely perfect". *Its value comes from the table*, not from a number on the card, so it scales on its own and makes you watch what others do.
- **Shake Down**, "That rival pays you its cost, or you steal it": *the other player decides*. Both outcomes are fine for you, and the victim feels in control of their loss. That makes table talk.
- **Promote**, "Swap a member with a shop member costing up to 2 more": *visible progress*. A free upgrade you can point at, and it can target a rival's member.
- **Last Call**, "+2 followers per minion you have left. Skip your turns until everyone else is out of minions": "YES now you're cooking". *It trades one scarce thing for another.* You give up position, so your last minions take whatever scraps are left, and in return you get followers by a count everyone can see. It's a gamble with clear math, it's worth more the earlier you take it, and rivals get something from your choice too: the spaces you're giving up.
- **Crunch Time**, "Take two turns after this one": "Crunch Time is great". *A tempo burst.* You grab two contested spaces before anyone can answer.
- **Crowd In**, "This round you can place on taken spaces" (his own idea): *it suspends the one rule everyone feels*, at the round III moment when the good spaces are gone.
- **White's cash-in**, "Turn all your white into followers": *it breaks a limit everyone feels*, the 10-per-card cap and one recruit a turn, but only for the player who built a pile for it.
- **Sending your own member to the bottom of the pile for white equal to its cost**: "improves on mine". *A sacrifice that turns a spent asset into fuel* for the color's burst.
- **Rising Tide**, "Each rival +2 silver, then gain half the silver of the rival with the most": "I like this one a lot". *Generosity that sets up your grab.* The gift makes your take bigger.
- **Recruit Twice / Spree**: *more of the scarcest thing late.*

The shapes underneath, to generate from:
1. **Trade a scarce thing for a different scarce thing**: position for followers, a member for fuel, a turn now for a turn later at a price. Write down what's given up; if nothing is, it's a number.
2. **Break a limit everyone feels, for one player, once**: the recruit, the 10-card cap, taken spaces, turn order. Best in round III.
3. **Read the table**: value from what others did, hold or placed (the previous action, the biggest pile, minions left). It scales without "× round".
4. **Give another player the choice**: they pay or lose, they get a gift or a turn. Choices make table talk.
5. **One visible count**: minions left, members held, the biggest pile. Players can do the math at a glance and plan around it.
6. **One sentence, done when taken.** Lasting effects belong on members.

## 5. Balance anchors (for your own sanity check; numbers go to Cory as fill-ins)

- A resource is about a follower at par (follower cards are 1 for 1), but a follower that skips the recruit is worth more late, and one that skips the 10 cap more still.
- Typical space totals, counting the +1 of its color, by round: round I about 2 to 4 (Gather +2/+3/+4; Help Yourself "steal 1 and +1 follower" 3/5/7 counting both), round II about 4 to 6 or a real ownership change (Promote, Shake Down), round III a burst (Spree, Poach, Double Down, Cash Out). Cory leans conservative on resources.
- Price a space by when it will really be taken, and compare it with the best other space a player could take with that minion that turn.
- A member returns about 1.5× its cost over the game; costs never go below 2.
- Buy-followers-only should win about 25% of 4-player games. A space that mostly helps a player who only recruits follower cards pushes the wrong way.

## 6. Prompts that start good ideas

- What limit does every player at the table feel in this round, and what would this color pay to break it?
- What does this color's player give up to take this space? (Nothing? Then it's a number.)
- What's lying on the table this round that the space could read: piles, minions left, members, the previous action, the shop row?
- Who groans when it's taken, and why are they still okay?
- Finish the color's sentence from section 3 for this exact space. If it doesn't fit, it's another color's space.
