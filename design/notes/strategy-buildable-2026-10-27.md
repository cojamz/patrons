# Why every turn is "biggest value right now"

Claude's read, Oct 27, on Cory's playtest note: "it doesnt feel like you can really cobble together a strategy. You're always just going for biggest value turn." Read against the live rules, decisions.md through Oct 27, the member decks (Oct 25 review) and the Oct 25-26 bot reads. Nothing here is a design call or built.

## What pulls every turn toward the biggest number
1. One exchange rate for everything. Any resource buys a follower 1 for 1, any time you have a recruit. So every space, steal and member can be priced on the same scale in your head, and the right turn is whichever number is biggest. A plan has to beat that number every turn to survive.
2. Spaces pay everyone the same. A space gives the same thing whoever takes it (mostly its color, often 3/4/5 by round). What you already own rarely makes a space better for you than for a rival, so the choice is "what's biggest" rather than "what's mine".
3. Members win by count, not by shape. Hard bots: cults ending with 10 or more members win 42 to 50%, cults with 1 to 9 win about 20%. Most cards that count, count members of any kind (Patriarch, Host, Mob). So the best member is the best value on the row, not the one that fits.
4. You can't steer toward a card. The row turns over before your turn comes back, round decks shuffle new cards in, and nothing lets you hold one. You chose that on Oct 23 ("players having to go with the flow and not getting to plan everything"), which is good for surprise but leaves the artifact as the only thing that points you anywhere.
5. Round III swamps the build. Five minions, big piles and cash-ins (Greatest Hits, follower buys of 10) are worth more than everything before, and they reward a big pile more than a built cult. Testers said rounds I and II get erased (Oct 26).
6. Artifacts point but don't grow. Each is +1 when something happens, all game. It tilts you toward a color but never rewards leaning in harder.

## What already pulls the other way
- Copying (Green) and Red's moves read the board, so they reward watching what rivals built.
- Cory's Oct 24 call: members should make two-color plans work. Few cards do it yet (Courier, Connector, Impostor, Extortionist and a handful more).
- The round I rework thread (Rework round I actions) is making round I's draft feel strategic. It fixes the first turns, not points 1, 3, 5 and 6.

## Cory's direction (Oct 27, 8:34pm Eastern)
"players should have fewer members but more strategy", "by end of game". So the fix aims at a smaller, weightier cult at game end, not more cards.

## Broad directions, shaped by that (Claude's, for Cory to pick)
1. Fewer, weightier members: each member is a bigger piece of a plan, so a game ends with a handful that work together.
2. Stop paying for member count: counting cards and multi-body members push toward quantity; let what a member does matter more than how many you hold.
3. A reason to stay small: each member beyond a few costs more, or a cult has room for only so many, so every recruit is a commitment.
4. Members that pay more the longer you keep them or the more you build around them, so keeping four good ones beats buying ten.
5. Artifacts that grow as you lean into them, so the opening pick becomes a game-long direction.
6. Round III cashes in the engine, not the pile: its big spaces pay by what you built in rounds I and II.
