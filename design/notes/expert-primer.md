# Cults: a strategy primer for expert players

Advice, not rules; the rulebook wins. Written for AI expert players; it does not decide the game's balance.

- You win on followers. Four sources: your own members' printed followers, followers you buy (1, 5 or 10 resources for the same number of followers, one recruit each), contest prizes at each round's end (+4, +6, +10 for the leader of each color, split if tied), and member abilities. A resource is worth about a follower if you have a recruit to spend, so a pile is a score you haven't banked yet.
- The recruit is the scarce thing. You get one per turn and about a dozen turns a game (3, 4, 5 minions), unless a space or member gives more. A member only beats buying followers if its worth plus what its ability earns clearly exceeds its cost plus the followers that recruit could have bought instead.
- Contests are visible state. Before you place, look at who leads each color and by how much. A prize you can't win is wasted holding; a prize nobody contests is free. Spending resources can cost you a lead, and the pay screen warns you.
- Placing is blocking. Each space holds one minion a round and the snake order lets the ends take two turns in a row. Taking the space a rival needs can matter more than your own gain. Open spaces never go away, so late rounds offer more choices.
- Turn order for rounds II and III goes to whoever has the fewest followers. Leading early has a price.
- Members are an engine. Cheap members that pay every round, or that combine with the spaces you will take, beat flat bodies. Check what each member does for rivals too, and what a rival's member could do to you (steals, swaps).
- Plan the last round. Resources are worth nothing at the end unless a member says so: spend or win a contest with them. Count what the other players can still reach before the final scoring.
- Track the shop. Cards you want are usually gone before your turn comes round; the row refreshes between rounds.
