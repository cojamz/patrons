# The rework list: actions and members (Oct 28)

One ordered list to work down, a step at a time. Each step names the real card, what's wrong and who said so, and my recommendation with the strategy behind it. Answer a step by number; I bring about 5 options for any step you want to open, and nothing changes in the game until you pick. Your standing calls: no member is swapped or removed yet; decks grow to about 16 per color with a real spread of cost, power, timing and member/follower counts; "Once per round" goes away.

The six colors here are the 2.0 ones (Red, Green, Magenta, Blue, Purple, White) plus the guests. Gold, Silver and Yellow come last.

How I judged every step: the game is engines and synergy, not good picks in a vacuum. Round I is short on resources, round II should change what you own or how much you recruit (the bar is Promote and Shake Down), round III is the burst where the recruit is the bottleneck. A card is a problem when it multiplies past that arc, when it does nothing at the moment it's taken, or when nobody builds around it.

## Part 1. Flagged by you, open now

### 1. Crowd In with Greatest Hits copies too much
- Crowd In (Purple, round III): "+2 purple. This round you can place on taken spaces." Greatest Hits (Green, round III): "Copy every action with your minion."
- Your read: "the purple Round III space is definitely too strong. It lets you do too much copying with greatest hits." Parked at 4:07pm for this rework.
- What's actually happening: Crowd In lets your remaining round III minions (up to 4) land on the best spaces of the game, Spree, Poach, Recruit a Member, Cash In, even if a rival is there. Greatest Hits then repeats every one of them. Each card is a fair burst alone; together they're the two biggest bursts multiplied.
- Even without Greatest Hits, Crowd In is the strongest round III space: every other round III space is one burst, Crowd In is access to all of them.
- Options:
  a. Crowd In: "Your next minion can go on a taken space." One burst, the same feeling of breaking the rule, sized like the other round III spaces.
  b. Crowd In: "This round you can place on spaces a rival took" (not your own), so you can't pile onto your own best space twice.
  c. Leave Crowd In; Greatest Hits copies "up to 3 actions with your minion". Caps the multiplier everywhere, including Squatter and Night Owl feeding it.
  d. Greatest Hits can't copy a space with a rival's minion on it too. Fixes the combo exactly, but it's a rule players must remember.
- [My pick: a.] Crowd In is your idea and its joy is "the space I wanted is gone, but I go anyway"; one minion keeps that and stops it being four bursts. If it feels too small after, add purple (+4) rather than a second minion.

### 2. Encore, Green's round II copy, becomes something else
- Encore: "Copy an action with a rival minion. +1 green." Your call: one Green copy goes (Encore); Cover ("Copy an action with no minion on it") stays.
- Two lists missed: (Kick Out, Any Color, Backup Singers, Second Wind) "i don't like these", and the first list (followers per your minion on a Green space; move a minion and do its space; green per your minions on Green spaces).
- What a Green round II space should do, as I read the color now: Green's player lives off what the table built. Round II should change what Green owns, like every round II space does, without adding a copy of a space.
- Directions, not cards, so you can steer first:
  a. Copy a member, not a space: a rival's member's ability works for you once (its On Recruit or One Time Use). Overlaps Red's Tryout (a shop member's On Recruit); it differs by reading the table, not the shop.
  b. Copy a pile: you match one rival's color, a bounded "Mime". You disliked Mime as a flat refund, so only if capped and visible.
  c. Copy a recruit: you recruit a member a rival also has (from the shop or pile, if one is there). Very Green, but needs two copies of a card, which the deck doesn't have.
  d. Feed the copies: a big green gain that pays off Green's members (Impressionist, Muse, Chronicler) without copying, for the green contest.
- [My pick: a, as a direction.] It's still the echo fantasy ("whatever the best play was, I get it too"), but on the member layer, which no Green space touches and which the member rework is about to make richer. If yes, I bring 5 cards.

### 3. "Once per round" goes away
Members printed "Once per round" today (live): Night Owl and Hot Streak (Purple), and the guests Squatter, Straggler and Big Spender. Taskmaster, Lookout and Pawnbroker carry it too, in decks outside the 2.0 colors. Your 3:33pm call holds: none of them changes until you pick.

Where each could go, my defaults:
- Night Owl, "after you place your last minion, take an extra turn" → stays every round, reworded to its moment: "Round End: before the round ends, take one more turn." Its strength is that it's every round; making it one time guts a 9-cost card.
- Straggler, "after the last minion is placed: you may move your last minion to an open space and take it" → same, "Round End:" wording. Every round is the card.
- Squatter, "place a minion on a space a rival already took" → One Time Use, cheaper. Every round it's too strong (your Oct 15 notes), and it's half of the Crowd In problem.
- Hot Streak, "+2 followers per turn in a row you've taken just before this one" → One Time Use, +3 per turn. One big planned payoff after Crunch Time or Right Away, instead of a small one each round.
- Big Spender, "recruit twice in one turn" → Round Start: "Your first turn each round, recruit twice." Same power, a fixed moment, nothing to track.
- Taskmaster, Lookout, Pawnbroker → when their colors come up.
- [My pick: the defaults above.] The rule I'd use: a card whose point is "every round" moves to Round Start or Round End; a card that's a burst becomes One Time Use.

### 5. Shake Down feels too bad for the player shaken down
- Shake Down (Magenta, round II): "You choose a rival's member costing 5 or less. That rival pays you its cost, or you steal it." Your note, Oct 24: "just feels too bad". Open since.
- Why it stings: the victim's choice is between two losses, and the cheap members it reaches (5 or less) are often the engine pieces they built around.
- Options: a. they pay half its cost, or you steal it; b. they choose which of their members is at stake; c. if you steal it, they take your cheapest member back; d. they pay its cost to you, or give it to the bottom of the pile (no one gets it).
- [My pick: b.] It keeps "the victim decides", which is why you liked it, and turns it from a mugging into a negotiation: they give up their least loved card or pay for a good one.

### 6. Extortionist gets reworked
- "Whenever a rival buys 5 or more followers, steal 2 resources from that rival." Your yes: it stops taxing follower buys. No shape yet.
- Why: buying followers is the way to score without adding members, so taxing it pushes every cult bigger, the opposite of your fewer-members direction. It's also passive (owners won 55% in Hard bot games at the old price).
- Directions: tax a big member haul ("Whenever a rival recruits 2 or more members in a turn, steal 2 from them"), tax the leader ("Round Start: steal 2 from the player with the most followers"), or a One Time Use shake-down of the biggest pile.
- [My pick: Round Start, from the leader.] A fixed moment, aimed at the right player, pairs with Robin Hood.
