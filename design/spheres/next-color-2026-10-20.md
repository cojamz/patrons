# Which color after Magenta — pitch, October 20

Claude's brainstorm for Cory. Broad themes and play only; cards, numbers and the economy are his. Built on design/strategy.md. Nothing here is approved.

## 1. Blue, the shop [Claude's pick]

Why now:
- It's on every Classic table (Gold, Red, Blue, Green at 4; Gold, Red, Blue at 3). Red and Green are done, so Blue is the weak half of the game friends actually play. Magenta isn't on Classic at all.
- The new economy moves followers into members. That makes the shop the main stage and the one-buy-per-turn the scarce thing late. Blue is the color that should own it, and today it's the one Cory called "needs a complete rework".
- It carries the soul of the game onto the second board: "will my member still be there?" is the same tension as "will my spot still be open?". Cory's own Oct 14 complaint ("a member you like is usually bought before your turn comes back") is unanswered.

Themes, without overlapping Red: Red gets members free and moves them between cults. Blue decides **what's for sale, who sees it first, and how many times you buy**. Not discounts: Cory dropped every cost reduction.
- Claiming: hold a face-up member so only you can buy it later (Splendor's reserve).
- The pile: look into it, pull from it, set what comes out next (Cory wanted more interaction with the pile).
- The row: clear part of it at the right moment (Cory on New Faces: a whole-row sweep is too disruptive).
- The buy itself: more than one buy, or a buy at an unusual moment.

Working back from round III: Spree ("This turn you buy 3 times") already reads as the shopping-day burst once members are cheap, because three buys in one turn is three members a rival can't contest. Alternative anchors worth a look: recruit anything from the pile, or claim several cards at once.

The play inside it: hate-buying and claim-denial; timing a row clear just before a rival's turn; reading a rival's plan from what they claim; and the shopper's dilemma under a held-blue contest (sit on blue for the rim, or spend it at the counter).

Risks: Blue's spaces may give access without the money to use it (the old Snap Up/Spree problem, Normal-bot bench). Under +1-per-action resources that gap gets wider unless the access itself is the value.

## 2. Purple, turn order

Why: the purest lever on blocking. Work back from Crunch Time ("Take your next 2 turns right away", Cory: keep as is): two placements before anyone can answer. Purple would own when you place: jump the snake, go first next round, take the end seat's double turn.

Play inside: denying the one space a rival needs, holding your placement until the last useful moment, buying position instead of resources.

Risks: extra turns need a spare space and round I at 4 players has none; Cory ruled out placing out of turn; the color's history is bugs and fiddly timing. Not on Classic.

## 3. Gold, a light pass

Why: on every Classic table, Cory's own design, and the most out of step with the new economy (+3 Kickback, 2/4/6 Golden Years, +1 gold per member, a cost-reduction space). Keep the hoard, gold as money and Double Down; mostly bring its gains and members onto the new economy.

Play inside: unchanged, spend-or-sit. Its flat spot (Double Down only matters to the gold leader) stays unless he wants to touch it.

Risks: little new play; it's an economy pass more than a design.

## 4. Silver, the counterweight

Why: Red, Green and Magenta all act at rivals' stuff. A giving color that buys influence would be the one thing on the table that rewards helping. Cory already asked for new Hold Court, Sweet Talk and Goody Bags, so round III is a blank slate.

Play inside: aimed gifts that come back to you, gifts of silver that feed a rival's silver contest (a tension nobody plays on purpose yet), soft deals with the trailing player.

Risks: gifts are hard to make matter (AI tester: "most gifting is token"); Rising Tide-style swings feel unfair at scale. Not on Classic.

## Not pitched now

- Yellow: color conversion gained value since stolen colors keep and five contests count held color, but its contest (full sets) needs a rethink first; better after Blue or Gold, when the colors it converts are settled.
- White: follower trading competes with the core follower buy. Under the new economy, where followers come from members, it needs a new job more than a rework; worth a broad brainstorm on its own.
