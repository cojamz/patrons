# Blue, the shop — first ideas, October 20

Cory picked Blue next (decisions.md, Oct 20). This is Claude's brainstorm: broad shapes only, no cards or numbers, nothing approved. Grounded in design/strategy.md.

## The line Blue must not cross

Red already trades members with the shop (Promote/Demote swap a member for a shop card; Open Door and Recruit a Member take shop cards free). Magenta moves members between cults. Both are about **which member a cult has**. Blue should be about **the row itself**: what's in it, how long it stays, who gets to it, and how often you buy. Not discounts: Cory dropped every cost reduction.

## Five shapes

1. **Claim.** Put a marker on a face-up member: only you can recruit it, until you do or the round ends. Rivals see what you're building, so a claim is a tell and a block at once. "Will my member still be there?" gets an answer you pay a space for. The worker-placement soul, moved onto the shop.
2. **Sweeten the row.** Put your own resources on a face-up member; whoever recruits it takes them. A lure you set for yourself, which a rival can grab first. The row starts holding value, like Small World's coins on unpopular races. Pairs with "a card nobody buys gets better".
3. **The pile.** Look at the top few and put them back in any order, or pull one up into the row. You decide what comes out next, including what the whole row refreshes into between rounds. Quiet planning power, and denial by burying a card a rival wants.
4. **Churn.** Send chosen face-up cards to the bottom of the pile, refill from the top. Not the whole row (Cory: too disruptive). A hate-draft without spending your buy: kill the card a rival is saving up for, right before their turn.
5. **The buy.** More buys in one turn, or a buy at an odd moment (before you place). Late in the game the buy is the bottleneck (strategy.md), so this is where Blue's burst lives.

## Round III anchors, working backward

- Spree as it stands ("This turn you buy 3 times"): three members in one turn that nobody can contest. Fits shape 5, and with cheaper members it's the shopping-day burst.
- Recruit from anywhere: any card in the pile, not just the row. Fits shape 3; the one member you've been waiting for.
- Market day: everyone may recruit once from the row, you first and you twice. Fits a gentler, table-wide Blue; spills a little onto others.

## How it plays under the new economy

- Contest on held blue (the Oct 20 default) gives the shopper's dilemma: sit on blue for the rim, or spend it at the counter.
- With +1 blue per action and cheap members, Blue's spaces need the access itself to be the value. A claim, a sweetened card or a buried rival target is worth a space; "you may buy" with no money behind it isn't (the old Snap Up/Spree problem).
- Members that fit: ones that care about the row (claimed cards, sweetened cards, the pile), not "when you recruit a blue member" (that hung on luck of the draw, Cory's main complaint).

## Claude's lean

Shape 1 (claim) as Blue's core, with 3 or 4 as its quieter round I neighbors, and Spree or recruit-from-the-pile as round III. Claim is the only one that adds a new kind of "will it still be there?" tension rather than more shopping.
