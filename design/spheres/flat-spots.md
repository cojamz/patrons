# Where each sphere goes flat (groundwork for the sphere rework)

2026-10-15. Cory: "the next step is to start slowly reworking all the spheres to be more strategically interesting." This is the groundwork: where each sphere's choices go flat today, and one broad direction for each. Cards and numbers stay Cory's. Nothing here is built or measured fresh; every piece of evidence names where it came from. Directions are Claude's fill-ins until Cory says yes.

"Flat" means one of three things: an obvious best play, a choice with no tension, or no reason to care what rivals do.

## The one thing every sphere shares

Six of the nine spheres score the same way: most of their color held when the round ends (Gold, Blue, Green, Purple, Silver, White; the code's `scoring` in each content file). So every sphere asks the same question, spend or sit, and the spheres feel alike at the moment that decides games.

- In round III everyone's best move is to spend everything, so the one player who sits on a pile wins uncontested. An AI tester saw a bot take Most White, Most Blue and Most Silver with 2, 2 and 9 while everyone else had cashed out (boardgame-enthusiast-w3.md §2).
- The bench agrees (Normal bots, 336 games, bench/economy-claims.md): 21–24% of round-III prizes go to a player nobody else scored against, and removing the round-III prizes changes the winner in 37–39% of games.
- Gold earns its "hold the most" rule: hoarding is its mechanic, and spend-or-sit is its tension. Blue (a shopping sphere told not to spend), Purple (extra turns paid in purple) and White (followers bought with white) all argue with their own prize.

Direction: each sphere's prize measures what its own mechanic builds, still as visible state you can read off a seat (principle 9 of the sphere brief). Gold keeps "most gold". The others get a shape of their own.

## Sphere by sphere

**Gold** (Cory's, locked). The reference that works: spend or sit is real, and Underdog, Penny Pincher and Hustler are the members testers loved (member-feel.md). The flat spot is Double Down, a no-brainer for whoever leads gold and a dead space for everyone else. Direction: leave Gold as the ruler.

**Red** (Cory's, locked). Most members, cheap bodies. The free recruits are the autopilot: an AI tester called Hand-pick and Open House "obvious" (boardgame-enthusiast-w3.md), because a free member costs nothing, so the only question is which one. Direction: a free recruit carries a visible price (in members, position or what rivals get), so taking it is a trade, not a gift. Only if Cory wants Red touched at all.

**Blue** (market; Cory: "needs a complete rework"). The prize tells a shopping sphere not to shop. Its spaces open the shop without the money to use it: Snap Up reads about 2 resources, Spree in round III 0.9 against a band of 5–9 (bench-618a8f5-provisional.md, Normal bots). Its members hang on whether blue cards happen to show up in the row. Direction options are below; Blue is the proposed first sphere.

**Green** (echo). Copying a rival's space is the best "watch the table" idea in the deck: you care what rivals took. The flat parts are the two plain gains in ring I (Hum, Crescendo) and a "most green held" prize that copying doesn't build. Direction: every Green space reads the table, and the prize measures what copying builds.

**Purple** (extra turns). At 4 players round I never has a space to spare, so Purple's ring I is three ways to gain purple, and Errand Runner and Night Owl do nothing there (rework-candidates.md 7 and 13). Roll Call is dominated by Clock In on the same ring. Rounds II and III are good: spend purple on turns, or hold it for the prize. Direction: Purple owns turn order (go first, go last, jump the snake), which matters from the first placement, instead of extra turns that need a spare space.

**Silver** (giving). Most of its gifts are "everyone else +1 silver": kind, but with no choice and no reason to care who gets it (AI tester: "most gifting is token", boardgame-enthusiast-round2.md). Rising Tide is the one that bites, and it swings hard. Direction: gifts you aim, which buy you something back from the player you helped, so kindness is a play.

**Yellow** (full sets). Opaque: nobody, bot or tester, chased "most full sets", and a set count is hard to read off a rival's seat (boardgame-enthusiast-w3.md §8). Its spaces mostly top up colors. Direction: a prize built by Yellow's own spaces and readable at a glance (the colors you hold, the gaps you have), with spaces that reshape your mix.

**White** (follower trading). It is the follower buy in another coat: All In is the 10-for-10 rate without spending a buy (why Cash In was cut), and Cheap Seats reads weak. It competes with the core buy instead of bending it. Direction: White is about when to buy followers (sell high, buy low, against the leader), not how many.

**Magenta** (thieves). Steals aimed at the rich and the leader already feel fair (AI testers). The flat part is the gap between stealing and the prize: loot is spent at once, so "most Magenta members" isn't built by stealing, and Kingpin pays nothing in 64–77% of games (member-feel.md §5). Direction: the prize rewards what a thief builds or keeps, so stealing and winning point the same way.

## Blue, the first calibration (pick one)

1. Bargain hunter [Claude's pick]: Blue's spaces give blue and bend prices in relative terms (half, per round, per member), round III is a shopping day, and the prize is your priciest member. Red wins on many cheap members, Blue on one grand one.
2. Tastemaker: Blue controls the row (sweep it, peek the pile, hold a card for later), and the prize is most blue members.
3. Collector: Blue rewards a spread of member colors, and the prize is the most different member colors.

Order after Blue (Claude's default): Purple, Yellow, White, Silver, Green, Magenta. Red and Gold only if Cory asks. One sphere at a time, with his yes before each.
