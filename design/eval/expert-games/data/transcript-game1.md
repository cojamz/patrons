After that I was a member-less player buying followers while Dee, who led all four colors, sat behind Lie Low. The deciding moment was round III when I stripped Dee's magenta and white with Help Yourself, then Ben copied that same Help Yourself with Echo and took my blue; later Dee's last-minion Fixer plus Accomplice, and Cal's Bargain Bin giving him +1 blue off my own Bury, broke my last contests. Too strong: Exchange at 2 followers per resource in rounds I and II (copyable by Echo and Reprise for 8 followers a turn), Lie Low as a first pick (it made Cal's 12 blue and Dee's piles untouchable), and Echo or Cover copying Help Yourself or Shake Down. Weak and unfun: early members like Busker and Plus-One feel like bait for Shake Down when you are spent, and the cheap-member engine plan barely pays against pure follower buying, since almost nobody else recruited members at all.

**Ben:** I lost because I thought my last green was untouchable once every steal space was taken, and I forgot that a last-turn member recruit can steal too. Dee's Spree bought Fixer, took Ava's Accomplice for 2 magenta, and its On Recruit stole my one green, flipping the green contest from me to her for a 20 point swing; I had even left myself at 1 green to buy one more follower. Before that I played well: Echo copying Help Yourself and Exchange early, Poach on Dee's Accomplice, and Greatest Hits into Allowance kept me within reach. Exchange (2 followers a resource) in rounds I and II and the round III copy chain (Echo, Reprise, Greatest Hits, Cover into Spree) feel too strong, since one good copy is worth a whole turn. Lie Low also felt too strong for a leader because it makes a big pile totally safe, and Accomplice-style On Recruit steals on cheap members decide the last turn of the game in a way that is hard to defend against. Money Changer was weaker than it looked: the white refund on buying followers did not seem to come back.

**Cal:** I finished last at 53, one behind Ava and Ben, because I lost my whole round I blue pile to Accomplice plus Help Yourself in one Ava turn and then fell 13 behind Dee in round II when Lie Low plus a final placement let her sweep three contests untouched. Ben's Exchange (2 followers per resource in rounds I and II) with Echo and Reprise copies was the real engine; it turned a few resources into 25 followers by round II and felt far too strong compared with anything else on the wheel. Lie Low also felt too strong: whoever holds it can't be punished, and in round III I got 12 blue with Peek plus Reprise and nobody could touch it, which made the blue contest a lock after two placements. The deciding moment was my first-pick Lie Low in round III, which kept my blue safe but left me placing last-tier spaces while Dee took Build Up and Allowance and Ben took Greatest Hits into Allowance. Accomplice and Shake Down on an emptied player are pure steals with no counterplay, and Greatest Hits plus Echo copying Allowance for +3 followers each felt like the best round III line. Members were nearly irrelevant: buying followers at 1:1 or 10-for-10 always beat 3 to 5 cost one-follower bodies.

**Dee:** I won from 18 behind on the last turn. Ben thought his single green was a lock, but my last minion was Spree for three recruits, then Fixer took Ava's Accomplice, whose On Recruit stole Ben's only green and two of Ava's white, which gave me green for +10 and a white tie while Ben lost his +10. Earlier, Cover into Shake Down on an emptied Ava and Lie Low on my first pick of round II let me hold all four colors in round II. Fixer plus Accomplice re-fired as a theft chain felt too strong, because nobody could have defended against it and a one-resource lock is not a lock. Exchange in rounds I and II (2 followers per resource, copyable by Echo/Reprise) was the clear runaway: Cal and Ben went from 1 to 25 on it. Lie Low plus Peek and Reprise gave Cal an untouchable 12 blue in round III, which felt unfun. Greatest Hits looked weak on the wheel but is the best space in round III, and Impressionist was fine but never a big factor. Allowance and Shake Down on a player with no resources felt a bit too strong, and the contest tiebreak/zero-holder rule produced the finish.
