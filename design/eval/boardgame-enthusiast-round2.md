**Cults: board-game enthusiast review (blind, laptop 1270×700)**

**Coverage:** I played one full game and two partial ones. Game 1 (Classic: Gold/Red/Blue plain/Green plain) is complete, though it ended after round II, which is issue #1 below. Game 2 (Purple/Silver/Yellow/Magenta) reached round III with me leading 40–37 when the browser tab crashed. The box was at 15 of 16 GB RAM, so I think this was the environment, not the game. Game 3 (Red/Blue badged/White/Green) went through round II, and then I stopped because you asked me to. Move log is in the appendix and at /tmp/bg-enthusiast/movelog.md. Screenshots are in /tmp/patrons-shots/enthusiast/ (s0xx = games 1–2, g3_xxx = game 3; s001–s002 were overwritten).

**Verdict:** The drafting is lovely and the round-III "chain turn" is a real highlight. But the scoring is broken in three places: a 1:1 follower buy, contests where ties all score, and a clock that can delete round III.

**Grades**
- Decisions: **B**. About 17 of 27 placements were real choices. The rest were leftovers or "take the 10→10 because I can afford it".
- Tension: **B+**. Losing Tide to P3 ruined my Blue Pillar plan. Squatter, steals and Lookout made me watch the table.
- Strategy/arc: **C**. Game 1 never reached round III. Getting the round-III centre space comes down to turn order, not planning.
- Interaction: **B**. Steals, gifts, Echo and Lookout all bite. Most gifting is token.
- Balance: **D+**. The 10→10 buy dominates, contests hand out free points, round-I Turnout is an outlier, and Front-runner is cheap.
- Clarity: **C**. There are hidden tiebreaks, stolen goods silently change colour, and nothing says extra turns add minions.
- Fun: **B−**. My best moments were great; the endings felt arbitrary.
- Overall: **C+**.

**Top 10 design issues, worst first**

1. **The clock can delete round III.** In game 1, I bought two 10→10s (one per round) and P3 and P4 bought the last two in round II. The game ended after round II, so the inner ring never opened (s032, s035). The game is sold as a three-round build to a burst, and here the payoff was cancelled by an ordinary buy. **Fix:** the clock can only shorten round III, never skip it, or make the 10-buy one per player per game. **Model:** Agricola and Lorenzo use fixed round counts; Dominion's Province pile is the right kind of clock.

2. **The 10→10 buy is always the right move.** In game 1 I won 30–27 with 24 of my 30 followers bought, and the recap says so. No member converts at 1:1, so engines lose to just buying followers. In game 2, too, my biggest single gain was a 10→10. **Fix:** make it 12→10, or keep 1:1 only on the centre spaces (Boom, Jubilee). **Model:** Dominion, where Gold and Province compete with engine pieces.

3. **Contests are participation trophies.** In game 2 round I, all four players tied for silver at 1, so everyone got +2 (s051). In game 1 round I, P2 swept all four contests (+8) by holding two of each resource. P2 then finished with 12 from contests and 0 bought; in game 2, P2 took +12 in round II alone (s063). Contests are the meal, not the seasoning. **Fix:** the minimum to score rises by round (2/4/6), and tied leaders split the points rounded down. **Model:** El Grande's majorities, where a tie drops everyone to the next tier.

4. **Tiebreaks are hidden.** "Most full sets: yellow breaks ties" and "badged: blue breaks ties" appear only in the round-end summary, not on the rim, the setup screen or the rulebook. In game 2 I held a full set and scored nothing. **Fix:** print every tiebreak on the rim.

5. **Getting the round-III centre is a lottery.** Each centre space holds one minion, and the fewest-followers player picks first. My game-3 plan was to hoard white and then Boom, but it died because P3 ended up behind me (g3_023). The summary also said "P3 picks first" in round II when P2 had fewer followers (3 vs 7), and I couldn't see why. Meanwhile Front-runner (2 purple: "always pick first") buys that lottery outright. **Fix:** reveal turn order as something you can contest, or price Front-runner at 6 or more. **Model:** the Lords of Waterdeep and Agricola start-player space.

6. **Extra turns add minions, and the rules never say so.** In game 2 round III I took Hourly, used Taskmaster, then Rally, Toast and Jubilee: four placements before anyone moved, plus an 8th buy (s066–s070). Thrilling, but uncapped, and Front-runner guaranteed it. **Fix:** cap Rally at 2, and state that "extra turn = extra minion". **Model:** Viticulture's grande worker is a single, visible extra.

7. **Stolen resources silently turn magenta.** In game 2 I took 2 silver to dent P3's silver lead and received magenta; the preview hinted at it, but neither the rulebook nor the space text says it. **Fix:** add "(arrives as magenta)" to Pickpocket, Rob and the other steal spaces.

8. **Last-moment contest swings are invisible.** In game 1, P2's Squatter joined me on Rush after my final pick and took "most gold" 4 to 3. The rim shows current standings, not who can still move. **Fix:** a rim marker for "can still be overtaken by X".

9. **Turnout is an outlier.** Late in round I it gave me +8 red, worth 10 followers once spent. **Fix:** cap it at 2× the round number.

10. **Restock swept away my own target.** Its row sweep removed Recluse, the card I was about to buy (g3_011). **Fix:** let the player buy before the sweep, or have the preview list what leaves.

**Members and spaces**
- Front-runner: nerf (cost 6+). A permanent first pick for 2 purple is too cheap.
- Shift Crew / Crew: nerf. 2 resources for 2 followers plus a member beats 2→1 outright.
- Turnout: nerf in round I.
- Rally: cap at 2 extra turns.
- Speculator, Gala, Jackpot (once-per-game doublers): traps. Buys are the bottleneck, so doubling resources you can't spend does little.
- Patience, Magnate (end-of-game "+1 per 3"): weak; buff to "+1 per 2".
- Merchant, Prospector: too small to matter; buff.
- Rising Tide: a trap. Giving every rival +1 to get half the richest pile rarely pays.
- Reserve ("No effect"): fine as Hire fodder.
- Exciting ones: Hustler (scales nicely), Taskmaster plus Rally, Jubilee, Boom, Proselytizer, Squatter, Lookout, Discount, Planner (its discount stacks, which surprised and delighted me), Lender, Dowager, Blue Pillar.

**Felt unfair or unfun**
- The game ending after round II. I only learned it at the reckoning banner; the "2 left" counter was the only clue.
- Squatter stealing "most gold" after my last move. Foreseeable in principle, but not on screen.
- Losing the full-sets tie to a yellow tiebreak I couldn't have known about.
- Stolen silver arriving as magenta.
- The last pick of a round, which is often dead leftovers with no contest in reach (game 2 round II, pick 4).
- Bots getting +8 for holding one of everything.
- From the other side: stripping P4's purple lead with Pickpocket felt great, and Lookout's skim back at me felt fair and funny.

**Table talk**
1. "Wait, the game's over? We never even opened the middle!"
2. "Just buy the 10-for-10 every round, it's the best deal on the board."
3. "Why did I get points? I had one silver." "Everyone had one silver."
4. "Four turns in a row? That purple chain was filthy, do it again."
5. "Who knew yellow breaks ties?"

**Vision: what a great Cults looks like in a year (ranked by impact on fun)**
1. **Round III is sacred.** Three fixed rounds; the follower stock only speeds scoring up. Every game ends in the centre-ring burst I felt in game 2.
2. **Contests become races.** Rising minimums, ties split, tiebreaks printed on the rim. They stay seasoning, and holding versus spending becomes a sharp choice instead of free points.
3. **The follower ladder gets priced so engines win.** 12→10 at the top, cheap members reworked, and 1:1 conversion only through the centre spaces (Boom, Jubilee). Recruiting is the game; buying followers is the fallback.
4. **Every quadrant gets a signature round-III chain**, like Taskmaster, Rally, Toast and Jubilee. A "chain preview" on hover sells the dream from round I.
5. **First pick in round III becomes a visible, contestable prize**, so a round-I plan can actually aim at it.

**What genuinely works**
- The spinning wheel plus snake draft is elegant, and the "you'd get" previews with live contest standings are excellent.
- Steals, Lookout, Echo and Squatter make you watch the whole table.
- Resume after a crash worked perfectly.

One-line UI notes: a large pixel follower sprite sat over the Pawn space for whole rounds. Extra turns are labelled "your turn again (snake order)". My minion was sometimes not drawn on the space it occupied.
