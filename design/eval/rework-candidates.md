# Rework candidates (tentative, 2026-10-14)

Built only from evidence already in the repo; nothing here was measured fresh. Available: design/decisions.md (Cory's calls, and what's already fixed and left off this list: Linger, Rally, Overtime, Shift Crew, Clockmaker, Feast Hall, Cash In, Barter, Relay, Pawn, Broker, Dealer, Echo, Recall, Choirmaster, Fan, Keepsake, Social Climber, Bloom, Silversmith, Heir, the "now" flags and the Purple reprices); the dead-window audit in cory-notes-2026-10-14.md §4 (a scan with mixed Normal/Easy bots over 360 + 100 games, whose "units" are resources + 1.5 per follower + 0.8 per point of discount); the fire-audit gate's exemptions (ALLOW: Late Riser, Harmonist; EXEMPT: Bodyguard, Social Climber, Magnate); card ledgers A and B; top-tier.md; purple-members.md (member-value sim, Easy and Normal bots, n=320); the member-value reference runs in design/rule-benders.md and the sphere docs; and design/eval/bots.md on the unmerged claude/cults-bots branch. Not available: the fair-price screen is still running (claude/cults-fair-price has pushed only its scripts, last commit "--from-seed"; no fair-price.md or reprice proposal yet), the all-Hard tables are pending in bots.md, and no blind panel or enthusiast has judged the current cards. Most member values predate the retuned Normal bot, and many predate the ×2 costs; each line says which. Per Cory's ruling, bot offer statistics ("dead in X% of offers") and floors are left out, so Branch Out [Prism], Redistribute [Tip-off], Bandwagon [Turnout], Bumper Crop [Flourish], Echo, Encore, Greatest Hits [Eternity], Sell High, Grifter and Extra Hand stay as he kept them. Names follow the content files as they are now, mid-rename; the old name is in brackets where the eval docs use it. "Classic" marks cards in the friends build's default table (Gold, Red, Blue, Green).

1. Sponsor (Red, 6+4, Classic): 'Your members cost 2 less.' Why: the hottest member still in the game. Handed over at round II it gained 8.3 followers and won 62% (member-value sim, old Normal bots, doubled costs, Release off; rule-benders.md), above Sprinter's 60%, which was cut as too strong (purple-members.md). Before the doubling it also topped the same-table anchors at 9.7 (greedy bots, market.md). It stacks with Intro Offer, Apprentice and Planner. Direction: reprice to 8+4.

2. Reseller (Blue, 8+4, Classic): 'Whenever you recruit, +1 of that member’s color.' Why: one template at three prices in the same shop: Middleman (Gold, 2+2, '+1 gold' on a non-gold recruit), Recruiter (Red, 4+4, '+1 red') and Reseller at 12. By the round it was recruited it paid 6.0/3.9/2.0 units, against Recruiter 6.8/4.8/2.0 and Middleman 4.9/3.1/0.7 (dead-window audit scan), so the dearest pays no more than the 8-cost one. Go-Between (Yellow, 4+4) is the same card again at 2.5/2.7/1.8. Direction: reprice to 4+2, and give the family one price (the audit's proposal: 2+2 each).

3. Tycoon (Gold, 8+4, Classic): 'Game end: +1 follower per 3 gold.' Why: the priciest Gold card measured at a third of its price: 3.4 followers when handed over at round II, against Banker (4) 4.0 and Proselytizer (12) 5.3 in the same run (member-value sim, old Normal bots, doubled costs). Its old 7.5–13.6 readings came from greedy bots that hoarded gold, and hoarding is gone at every bot level (bots.md). With the 10-follower buy unlimited at 1 for 1, it only pays on gold that one buy a turn can't absorb. Magnate and Timekeeper print the same rate at 8. Direction: reprice to 4+4.

4. Snap Up [Bargain] (Blue, ring I, Classic): '+2 blue, then you may recruit a face-up member now, in any colors.' Why: Cory's own anchor lists "an extra recruit plus +2" as too strong (value-model.md), and the Blue designer flagged it for him as an unjudged fill-in (market.md). The one measurement disagrees: forced as a round's first pick it scored −1.0 followers against the bot's own choice (greedy bots, pre-doubling), because the recruit is paid. Cory's anchor wins under his rules. Direction: reword to 'Recruit a face-up member now, in any colors.' (drop the +2 blue).

5. Groupie (Green, 4+4, Classic): 'Whenever anyone recruits a green member, +1 green.' Why: the same card as Stylist (Blue, 2+2) and Merchant (Gold, 4+2), all three in the Classic shop at 4, 6 and 8. By recruit round they paid Stylist 6.3/3.8/1.5 units, Merchant 4.5/3.7/1.4 and Groupie 4.8/2.4/1.1 (dead-window audit scan): the dearest pays least. Direction: reprice to 2+2, Merchant too.

6. Mime [Mirror] (Green, 4+2, Classic): 'When recruited: gain as much green as the rival with the most green.' Why: the same one-shot as Upstart [Silver Spoon] (Silver, 4+2: 'When recruited, gain silver equal to the most silver any rival has.'). Both are weak early and huge late: Mime 2.3/5.0/7.2 units by recruit round, Upstart 1.5–2.7/5.8/10–16 (dead-window audit scan). Recruited in round III, either one instantly ties whoever held that color all round for the +6 ring. Direction: halve it ('half the most any rival has, rounded up', Rising Tide's rule), and make Upstart match.

7. Errand Runner (Purple, 4+4): 'When recruited: take an extra turn right after this one.' Why: dominated at the same price by Go-Getter [Second Wind] (6+2: 'Once per game: take an extra turn right after this one.'), which picks its moment and gets its use back when no space is spare (purple.js). Errand Runner's turn is simply lost then, and at 4 players round I never has a space to spare: 4 of 4 round-I recruits got nothing (dead-window audit). The member-value sim can't read it, since it skips "when recruited" (2.1 Normal, 1.2 Easy). Direction: reprice to 2+2.

8. Accomplice (Magenta, 4+4): 'When recruited: take 1 resource from each rival.' Why: 3 resources once at 4 players (1 at 2 players) for a cost of 8, measured 2.7–2.8 units (dead-window audit scan). Cutpurse (2+2: 'Now and at round start, take 1 resource from the rival holding the most.') takes 1 now and 1 at each later round start for half the price, and Pilgrim (Yellow, 2+2) gives 4 resources for 4. Direction: reprice to 2+2.

9. Exporter (White, 4+4): 'Whenever you recruit a non-white member, +1 white.' Why: word for word Middleman (Gold, 2+2) in white, at twice the price, and the weakest of its family at 1.7/2.1/1.5 units by recruit round (dead-window audit scan). Its old 7.3 was the drip artefact the White designer warned about: greedy bots spent all their white, so any drip won the contest (trading.md). Direction: reprice to 2+2.

10. Tipster (White, 4+2): 'Whenever a rival buys followers, +1 white.' Why: word for word Tax Collector (Gold, 2+2) in white, at 6 instead of 4 (dead-window audit, "twins"). Its higher greedy-bot reading (6.2 vs 4.4) is the same white-drip artefact (trading.md). Direction: reprice to 2+2.

11. Roll Call [Wind-up] (Purple, ring I): '+1 purple per minion you have on the board, this one included.' Why: dominated on its own ring by Clock In [Hourly] ('+4 / +5 / +6 purple, by round.'). Roll Call tops out at the minions you get that round (3/4/5), so it only catches up after extra turns, Extra Hand or a Squatter (card text, purple.js). Bots can't tell them apart (takers won 27% vs 26%, Easy bots), which is why it never showed. Cory welcomes weak twins across rings (Open Door and Open House), but this one shares a ring and never wins on its own. Direction: reword so it outgrows Clock In somewhere (Cory's number), or cut.

12. Harmonist (Yellow, 6+2): 'Game end: +1 follower per full set you hold.' Why: one of only two cards the fire-audit gate lets stay silent (ALLOW: "pays only with a full set at game end"). It scored 0 in 50–80% of games and 1.5–1.8 on average (dead-window audit scan). A set is 4 resources for 1 follower, while the unlimited 10-follower buy pays 1 for 1, so it only pays on what a Yellow player still holds for the round-III ring. Direction: reprice to 2+2 (the audit's proposal).

13. Night Owl (Purple, 8+2): 'Each round, after your last minion, place one more and buy again.' Why: Cory found its first wording "extremely confusing" (decisions 345 and 355). The new one says "each round", but at 4 players round I never has a space to spare, so it does nothing there (decision 345, purple.js). Because the text avoids the words "extra turn", the glossary's "only while the board has a space to spare" never attaches to it either. Direction: reword so the spare-space condition shows on the card.

14. Hustler (Gold, 2+0, Classic): 'When recruited, pick a color. Actions there give you +1 gold per round number.' Why: Cory asked for the weak Gold cards to be buffed, and after the buff Hustler still measured 1.3 followers, "still weak" (decision 179, member-value sim of that day). "Actions there" points at a color as if it were a place; the code means that color's quadrant (gold.js). Direction: reword ('actions in that color's quadrant'), with a stronger payout of Cory's choosing.

15. Safecracker [Big Score] (Magenta, 2+2): 'Once per game: take half (rounded down) of the magenta held by the rival with the most magenta.' Why: magenta comes only from stealing and is spent at once (Cory's loot rule), so the pile it halves is thin: about 2 units when used (dead-window audit scan). Cutpurse, at the same price, takes 1 now and 1 at every later round start. The audit's "unused in 15 of 25 holdings" is bots forgetting once-per-game cards, not the card. Direction: reword (the audit's 'Once per game: take 3 resources from the rival holding the most').

16. Farmer (Yellow, 4+2): 'Whenever an action gains you yellow, +1 more.' Why: word for word Prospector (Gold, 2+2) in yellow, at 6 instead of 4. The design critic flagged the pair, along with Go-Between ≈ Middleman (yellow.md); Cory passed the Yellow members as "OK" on first read (decision 193). Direction: reprice to 2+2.

17. Benchwarmer [Reserve] (Red, 1+0, Classic): 'No effect.' Why: identical to Intern (Red, 1+0, 'No effect.'). It's a placeholder waiting on Cory's open Reserve question (decision 253; member-names.md says it becomes Seat Saver if he picks the Splendor-style hold). Direction: give it the hold once Cory rules, or cut.

## Watch list
1. Investor (Gold, 4+2, Classic): its 1.9 followers when it cost 3 (decision 179) comes from bots that set aside half; a person who sets aside everything doubles it each round. Judge in play.
2. Sower (Yellow, 2+2): "near-dead" for bots (zero 65–100% of the time, dead-window audit) because bots never bank yellow (yellow.md), yet planting 5 yellow returns 15 a round; the critic called it fiddly and Investor's clone. Judge in play.
3. Dividend (White, ring II, '+1 white per loose follower you have.'): not measured (decision 360); by arithmetic, a heavy follower buyer with 10–15 loose followers in round II takes 10–15 white against a ring-II budget of about 4.
4. Revelers (Silver, 4+2, 'Counts as 3 members.'): not measured (decision 315); for 6 it counts 3 for Red's ring, Patriarch, Tithe, Heir and Grifter.
5. Silversmith ('Whenever a rival takes a Silver space, +1 silver.') and Ensemble ('Whenever you copy a rival’s space, +1 follower.'): today's new texts, neither measured (decisions 318 and 332).
6. Magnate (White, 4+4): fire-audit exempt (about 1 fire in 13 holds) and 0 in 70–73% of games (dead-window audit), yet 5.0 followers at 8 (member-value sim, Normal bots, after the White rework); the instruments disagree.
7. All In [Boom] (White, ring III): its takers won 39%, the best round-III space (Easy bots of the day, 300 random tables, confounded by who can pay); it has the unlimited 10-follower buy's 1-for-1 rate without using a buy, which is the reason Cash In was cut.
8. Proselytizer (Red, 6+6): 5.3 followers at 12 on the old Normal bot (rule-benders.md), which under-recruited (bots.md); recheck on the retuned Normal before repricing.
9. Choir (Green, 4+2) and Impressionist (Green, 2+2): same trigger, same +1 per copy, and Choir costs 2 more (card text).
10. Speculator and Lottery Winner with Underdog: once-per-game gold doublers leave 60-gold piles that one buy a turn can't absorb (bots.md), and Underdog makes Speculator ×4 (Claude's gut call, decision 252); never measured as a combo.
