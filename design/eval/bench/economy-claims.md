# Five economy claims from the blind game-feel reviewer, measured

2026-10-15. Instrument: `scripts/v4-economy-claims.mjs` on claude/cults-bench 3b04cc2 (the rules of 618a8f5 plus the Hard rollout fix cac604b). 336 bot games, seeded: 240 with 4 Normal seats (120 on Classic, 120 on random 4-sphere tables from makeTables) and 96 with Hard-20 in one rotating seat and 3 Normal (48 Classic, 48 random). Intervals are 90% Wilson. These are bot games: they say what the rules allow bots to do, not what people will do.

**1. "10→10 at 1:1 makes members a losing trade."** Not supported. On Classic with 4 Normal seats, the quarter of players who recruit most (13–20 members) win 44.2% [36.9–51.7] against 15.8% for the quarter that recruits least (2–8); with a Hard-20 seat the same shape holds (37.5% vs 16.7%). Players who recruit 14 or more win 47.2% (n 89) and finish last 20.2%: a 16-member engine coming last happens, but about one game in five, not as the rule. On random tables the slope flattens to nothing (22.5% for the fewest recruits, 25.0% for the most), so members neither win nor lose there on average. Winners do take about half their followers from buys (Classic: 34.8 of 69.7, 49.9%), but so does everyone else (55.9%); what separates winners is rims (20.6 vs 12.7) and members' own worth (9.5 vs 7.4). The reviewer's "winners bought ~43" is in range for one game; the bots' winners average 35.

**2. "Hoarders win round-III rims uncontested while everyone cashes out."** Partly supported. Of round-III rims that paid, 21–24% were won with nobody else scoring at all, and another 35–46% were won at twice the runner-up or more (Classic Normal 35.4%, random Normal 43.5%, Hard-20 tables 42–46%). So a round-III rim is usually not close. It decides games: removing every player's round-III rim followers changes the winner in 36.7% of Classic games and 39.2% of random ones (Normal; 27–35% with a Hard-20 seat). The winner takes 10–11 followers from round-III rims out of 64–70. The claim's picture (one hoarder, everyone else cashing out) matches about one rim in four; the rest are lopsided but contested.

**3. "Round-I rims are won at a 1-1 tie, so several players score."** Weakly supported. Ties are common: 28.8% of round-I rims that paid went to 2 or more tied leaders on Classic (33.1% on random tables). But ties at a score of exactly 1 are rare on Classic (4.0%) and occasional on random tables (10.6%). Most round-I ties are at 2 or more of the counted thing.

**4. "New Faces is taken before a player's first placement in round I."** Supported as an occasional event. New Faces (+2 blue and a fresh face-up row) is taken in round I in every game (every round-I space fills), and within round I's first four placements, so before at least one player has placed, in 15.0% of Classic Normal games (18.8% with a Hard-20 seat) and 25% on random tables with Blue (n 36 and 12, wide intervals). When it happens, the player still to place sees a row they had no chance to buy from.

**5. "Rising Tide gives +10 in round II often."** Not supported for round II. Rising Tide is a ring-II space: its gain in round II has a median of 5, a 90th percentile of 6–7, and never reached 10 (0 of 65 takes, Normal and Hard-20 tables together). In round III it is a different space: median 9, 90th percentile 11–14, and 10 or more in 49.0% of Normal takes (27.3% with a Hard-20 seat). The reviewer's +10 is a round-III number.
