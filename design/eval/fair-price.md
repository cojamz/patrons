# Fair price of every member (2026-10-15)

## Summary (≤15 lines)
- Every member on all 11 spheres plus the 7 guests (157 cards) priced at today's rules. Instrument: a paired forced-recruit panel, 4 normal bots, random real tables, 230k probes over 4,795 seeds, plus a 500-seed confirmation run for the 14 biggest jumps. Each card's round I/II/III prices are weighted by when players actually recruit it (14k-game jitter screen).
- Verdicts: 81 fair as printed, 38 underpriced, 38 overpriced. 23 need an ability change, not a price (they're in the proposal with the price unchanged).
- Raise the most: Tax Collector 4→10, Tipster 6→10, Tinker 6→12, Silver Spoon 6→12, Peddler 4→10, Lender 2→8; and at 4→8 Planner, Recluse, Cutpurse, Mole, Robin Hood. Banker 4→6.

## Method (one paragraph)
Three instruments, one funnel. **Screen** (scripts/v4-fair-price.mjs screen): 14,000 games, a random 4-sphere table each (makeTables), every member's price jittered ×U[0.6,1.4] independently (colored/any split kept, rounded to even), 4 normal bots with one Hard seat in 10% of games. It logs every offered-and-affordable recruit and what each player ends with. It gives demand (recruit rate) and when cards really get recruited. Its ownership-vs-margin regression is biased (only rich players afford late, pricey cards; the fitted price slope came out the wrong sign), so it ranks and is never used for a price. **Panel** (… panel), the price: each seed plays a base game; in each paired copy, every seat gets its own private copy of a different card, at printed −4 / printed / +4. It recruits that copy at its first affordable buy step from round I, II or III (games forked at that round's start, so the prefix is shared), instead of its normal buy. Δ follower margin vs the base is fitted jointly over all cards (a seat's margin also moves with its rivals' probes: −⅓ each), with per-round intercepts and slopes. Fair price in round r = printed + net margin at printed ÷ slope (slope held to 0.4–1.5 followers per resource). The card's price is the per-round prices weighted by its natural recruit-round mix from the screen. 90% CIs bootstrap over seeds. **Confirmation** (… panel --center): the 14 cards whose proposal moved more than 4 were re-run on a grid around the proposed price, so no proposal rests on extrapolation; it also reports what share of seats could ever pay that price. **Single-card sweeps** (… sweep), the original one-probe design, validated the panel on the anchors and ran a Hard (thinker, budget 60) test seat on 8 cards. Paired games decorrelate fast (paired-margin SD ≈ 12 followers, pair correlation ≈ 0.1), so precision comes from volume. All runs are in design/eval/runs/fair-price/.

## How to read a line
printed (colored+any) → fair ~Y (90% CI, panel n = probes; by round recruited I / II / III, then the share actually recruited in each round); screen recruit rate; verdict; flags. Proposed prices are even, keeping the printed colored/any share. "Bots under-recruit it" = worth far more than printed, yet bots take it in <10% of affordable offers: a bot-valuation gap as well as a price.

## Anchors (validate the method first)
- Big Family (2+2, worth 3): panel ~4 (2.5–5), best recruited late (III ~4). Hard seat, forked at round III: ~4 (0–6.5, 119 seeds). It matches the 4→3 follower buy, as it should. Forced into round I it's worth only ~3, since 3 early followers push you later in the pick order.
- Volunteer (free, 1 follower): panel ~2.5 (1.5–3); single-card sweep (600 seeds) ~2 (1.5–3). A free member is a free follower (2→1 rate) plus member count. Printed 0 stays (a deliberate lure; it's an anchor, not proposed).
- Banker (2+2, "now and at round start +3 gold"): round I ~7, II ~5.5, III ~4 → ~5.5 (5–6.5) at the natural mix. Single-card sweep, forced from round I: 7.3 (6.3–8.3, 600 seeds).
- Panel vs single-card sweep, same anchors, forced from round I: Volunteer 2.6 vs 2.1, Big Family 2.8 vs 2.4, Banker 7.0 vs 7.3. The 4-at-once design holds.
- Hard seat (thinker, budget 60, 120 seeds), forked at round I: Banker ~7.7 (6.1–10.3), Volunteer ~2.6 (0.9–4.3), Revelers ~1.2 (−3.3–3.8), Lender ~5.3 (2.9–11.7). It agrees with the normal-bot panel's round-I numbers (Banker 7, Volunteer 2.5, Revelers 5 with a wide CI) except Lender, where Hard reads lower than 12. Lender's proposal of 8 sits inside both CIs. Forked at round III: Big Family ~4, Proselytizer ~8.6, Shift Crew ~2, Magnate ~2 (wide CIs; directions agree with the panel).

## Caveats (name the instrument)
- Normal bots everywhere except the Hard checks. Turn order, denial and long plans are what bots value worst (Front-runner, Squatter, Straggler read low partly for that reason).
- "Forced at first affordable buy step" prices the card against the bot's normal buy at that moment, which itself often recruits underpriced cards. Prices are relative to the current economy.
- The probe is a private copy with its own id: Translator (checks itself by id) is unmeasured, and Hustler's and Doppelganger's bot hints don't see the copy (minor).
- Prices above about printed+4 lose affordability: the confirmation run's recruited share is printed in the line. Under ~30% means the price can't hold it.
- Stand-in Blue/Green plain members and other plain members are yardsticks (Cory's rule: old price). They're listed, never proposed.

## Findings for the foreman
1. Engine crash, reproducible: `node scripts/v4-fair-price.mjs screen --games=1 --from-seed=1823 --hard=0.1` → "Card 40 is not in the shop" from red.takeFree via echo.js ctx.recruit (a copy of Take, or Take on a stale choice: the chosen card has left the shop). It happened in 3 of about 76k games (screen g=1823, panel g=901 and g=3539). Same class as the Straggler stale-choice fix: re-check the shop when the choice resolves. Also, 3 panel games (g=3555, 3609, 3946; `panel --games=1 --from-seed=3555 --rounds=2,3`) hit the harness's 6,000-move cap without ending, which may be a loop. Unchecked.
2. Lender ("+5 white; game end: lose 2 followers"): Release from the next round refunds its 2 white and dodges the −2, so the loan is never repaid. A rule fix (releasing it costs the 2 followers), not a price.
3. Bots under-recruit these (under 10% of affordable offers) though they're worth well above printed: Thrift, Tinker, Errand Runner, Dyer, Weaver, Feastmaker, Double Agent, Tipster, Déjà Vu. Each is a bot-valuation fix as much as a price.
