# Table feel critique (rework panel, phase 3)

Seat: how each concept plays with friends online, each at a laptop. Read: brief, strategy-read, member-shape, pricing, deck-visibility, cory-judge (rubric v3), strategy.md, taste.md, decisions.md (ties, #99, the Oct 15 "not CRAZY bad" line, the pacing numbers), every open pitch. No sims, no games, no code. All card texts quoted are the designers' fill-ins.

## What I score against

- **Table talk.** Does it make someone say something out loud: a warning, a groan, a "nice"? A concept whose whole round can be played without looking at a rival scores low.
- **The victim.** Cory's line (Oct 15): a sting is fine, "just not CRAZY bad". What a rival loses has to be visible and explainable in one sentence.
- **The newcomer at a glance.** taste.md: words beat symbols, one hover sentence, every choice clicked on the board. If a rule needs the player to work out "second-largest", "whoever the leader is, then their biggest color", or "which pile counts where", it fails here.
- **Waiting.** A rival turn takes about 1.6s to animate, and your wait between turns is about 6.3s (decisions, pacing). Anything that hands one human three or four decisions in a row, or stops everyone at round start or round end while one player chooses, adds dead time that friends at laptops feel at once.
- **Hidden state.** Anything that has to be remembered, or that one player sees and others don't.
- **Interesting but not complicated.** Cory's principle for tonight.

Scores are 1–5 from this seat only.

## Gold

### gold-anchor A ("Gold is the money for buying members"): 3
Clean and readable. It's also the quietest concept on the panel. Apart from Kickback's gift, nothing a gold player does makes a rival look up. At 2 players, where gold is half the board, that's a long solo game. "Double one of your colors" (the widening) is the best moment in it: "she doubled red to 14" is table talk, and it reads at once.
- Fixes: (1) Take the widening as the default; it's the only shared moment. (2) Cut Socialite ("When you pay 5 or more gold for a member, +2 followers"). A threshold the buyer has to check against how the payment split is a hidden sum on the pay sheet. (3) Splurge ("gold you spend on a member counts double this turn") needs the shop to show doubled buying power while you shop, or players will misread what they can afford. Flag it as a screen cost.
- Cards: Midas ("Your gains from actions come as gold") is fine to own but confusing to watch: a copy's gain or a Potluck payout flies in as gold, and rivals can't tell why. It needs a hover line on every flight. Miser ("Ties for the most gold go to you") is the panel's fourth "you win ties" card (see collisions).

### gold-anchor B ("Gold multiplies whatever you hold"): 2.5
"Double", "half", "match" are each one word. But with Gold Standard, Half Again and Double Down all reading "one of your colors", every gold turn is a quiet sum on your own pile. Rivals feel it only as "her contest lead flipped again". The designer is right that it crowds Yellow.
- Fixes: cut, keeping only the widening (which A already does).
- Cards: Notary ("You win contest ties") is a near-copy of Tiebreaker. Interest ("At round start, +1 gold per 3 gold you hold") is fine.

### gold-outsider A (the Jackpot): 4, the best table feel in Gold
A coin pile with a number on a wheel space is the most legible shared object anyone pitched. It's the oldest worker-placement table talk there is: "don't Stake, you're feeding the trailer", "someone Skim before she gets it". The forced round I fill turns every Gold placement into a public choice. Motion explains it for free: a coin flies to the pot on every Gold placement. The victim never loses anything they owned.
- Fixes: (1) Cut Dividend ("Gain gold equal to the gold on the Jackpot"). It's a second "how big is the pot" read that dies once the pot is taken, and it makes the newcomer ask "wait, does it empty?". Put Collect Debts or a plain scaling space there. (2) Merge Stake and Skim into one round I space: "+1 gold × round, then put 1 on the Jackpot or take 1 from it". You click the pot, or you don't. That gives three round I spaces that differ in kind, not three "+1 gold × round" with different side coins (the designer's own template worry). (3) Watch 2 players. The pot plus the trailer's first pick plus the snake's double turn is a rubber band you can see coming. A cap ("at most 10") makes it read as a prize, not a lottery.
- Cards: Bagman ("take half the Jackpot") and High Roller ("Once per game: take the Jackpot") are the same verb at two sizes. Keep High Roller (the timed shot). Heirs ("When recruited: put 1 gold on the Jackpot") is a dull card that also feeds a rival. Launderer is Cory's guest idea, so it doesn't belong in Gold.

### gold-outsider B (Compound): 2.5
Same feel as gold-anchor B: solo arithmetic on your own pile. Tribute ("Take 1 gold × round from the rival with the most gold, if they have more than you") is a conditional you have to check before you click, and it's Magenta's verb.
- Fixes: cut. It survives only as the round III fallback inside A.
- Cards: Tax Collector, kept here, fires on every rival's follower buy, about 15 times a game at 4 players. It's a drip on other people's turns, which Cory dislikes.

## Purple

### purple-anchor A ("Skip ahead"): 2.5
Fast Forward and Wake Up are lovely to watch: each member pays its round-start income again, and the coins fly from the cards (taste rule 11, scoring shows its sources). The problem is the pile of back-to-back turns:
- Crunch Time ("Take your next 2 turns right away")
- Head Start ("then take your next turn right away")
- Errand Runner ("When recruited: take your next turn right away")
- Double Shift ("Your first turn each round: take your next turn right away")
- Late Riser and Night Owl (extra turns)
- Sprinter, a payoff for doing it

At a 4-player online table, one human making three to five placements, each with a buy, back to back, is 20–40 seconds of three friends watching. The designer says so ("dead air for rivals"). It's also #99 ("non-intuitive turns in snake draft") multiplied by four.
- Fixes: (1) One turn-mover in the deck, not three. Keep Crunch Time on the board, cut Errand Runner and Double Shift, and keep Night Owl as the capstone. (2) Head Start stays only if the turn rail shows "Ann moves up" before it happens. Otherwise drop its "take your next turn right away" and keep the "+1 per minion left" gain. (3) Fast Forward collides with the strategist's White lean. From this seat it's the best idea in Purple, so it should own it.
- Cards: Sprinter ("Whenever you take two turns in a row, +1 follower") fires on every snake end, so it pays at 2 players without any decision. Punch Clock ("+1 purple per rival with more followers than you") is fine.

### purple-anchor B ("Own the order"): 1.5
Hold Up ("a rival you choose takes their next turn last this round") is the worst feel on the panel. The victim's turn vanishes from where they expected it. Online, nobody is looking at the turn rail until it's their turn. The talk will be "why is it not my turn?", not "nice play". Time Thief puts the same shove on a card. Night Watch ("Rivals can't move your turns") is a defense against your own deck.
- Fixes: cut.

### purple-outsider 1 (The Count): 3
The biggest single moment pitched tonight. "Every contest but purple's pays out now" plays the round-end scoring animation mid-round, and the table already knows how to read that. The problem is who it pays. Most of the followers go to rivals who happen to lead, so the taker is kingmaking: "you just handed Ben 6 for nothing" is the talk, and at a friends' table that sours fast. Contests then pay again at round end, so a newcomer sees a contest pay twice in one round and asks which one was real. Early Count is a calmer version: one color, you pick it.
- Fixes: (1) If it survives, make it "Contests you lead pay out now (they still pay at round end)", so the taker isn't feeding rivals. That makes it White-anchor A's verb, so one of the two goes (collisions). (2) Cut Head Count ("+1 purple per contest you lead right now"): it pays the leader twice and needs a rim scan before the click.
- Cards: Tiebreaker (another "you win ties"). Snap Poll and Election Night are the verb on cards and read fine.

### purple-outsider 2 (Put It Off): 3.5, top Purple
The most readable purple: "+1 per minion you placed before this one" is a number the player can see on their own seat. Patience ("+2 followers per minion you placed before this one") creates exactly the snipe talk Cory likes ("take Patience before she gets there on her last minion") without Bandwagon's seat bias. Only Crunch Time moves turns. Night Shift's "at round end, +1 purple × round" sits on the board under your minion, so nothing is remembered.
- Fixes: (1) Cut Rush Job ("Recruit: take your next 2 turns right away"). It's a second Crunch Time and the same dead air. (2) Replace Long Shot ("+2 purple per rival with more followers than you"), which is off-theme and is White's catch-up. Fast Forward from purple-anchor A fits that slot, if White doesn't take it. (3) Patience reaches +9 followers with no buy in round III. Cap it, or make it purple instead of followers in rounds I–II.
- Cards: Night Watch ("Game end: +2 followers per purple space your minions are on") is fine if minions stay on the board at game end. Check that rule. Go-Getter is a third extra-turn card. Fine to own, but count it toward the dead-air budget.

## Silver

### silver-anchor A (The Tide): 4, top Silver
The only positive-sum color on the panel, and it feels like hosting a party. Coins land on everyone's seat, and the talk is "thanks for the silver", then "oh no, she's taking my pile". High Tide reads in one pass. Regift ("Give a rival one of your members. Gain silver equal to its cost") is two clicks on the board (the member, then the seat) and a great moment: "you gave me WHAT?". The victim of a Tide loses nothing.
- Fixes: (1) Attention. Toast, Rising Tide, High Tide, Retinue and Benefactor each send coins flying to three seats. On a Silver-heavy turn that's taste rule 10 broken ("rivals' turns are summarized"). Batch the gifts into one flight per seat. (2) Warm Welcome ("A rival you choose gains 1 follower") is pure kingmaking on a space at a friends' table; even a small gift reads as a political pick. Swap it for a plain follower space, or make it "the player with the fewest followers". (3) Cut Caterer ("Whenever you give a rival something, +1 follower (once per space)"). "Once per space" is a small rule you have to keep in your head.
- Cards: Chaperone ("Your gifts to everyone else skip the player with the most followers") reads well and gives the table a reason to talk about the leader. Guest at 1+4 is the right counter.

### silver-anchor B (the Pot): 2.5
Fine to look at, but it's the Jackpot again, and Gold's version is on more tables. Free Lunch ("You may pay for members with silver from the pot") adds a second payment source to the pay sheet. Steward is a drip on rivals' turns.
- Fixes: cut in favor of gold-outsider A.

### silver-outsider A (calls the contests early): 2.5
The same verb as white-anchor A, with more small rules: Toast "Score half of a contest you lead now; it's over this round", Hecklers "Rivals can't score early a contest you hold any of", Runner-up "+1 follower per contest where you're second". The rims show the leader, not second place, so Runner-up is a hidden count. Two players: Silver calls red, the other player sees a greyed rim and can't do much.
- Fixes: cut. The contest-timing verb goes to White (collisions).

### silver-outsider B (lift, then match the top): 3
Pleasant (nobody loses anything) and quiet. "Gain enough to tie the rival with the most" is a sum the screen does for you, so it reads fine. Four of the six spaces are match or half-match, so a newcomer can't tell them apart from the panel text. It's the giving color without the party feel of anchor A.
- Fixes: cut as a whole. Keep Rising Tide's gift-then-harvest, which anchor A already has.

## Yellow

### yellow-anchor A (Your cult is your harvest): 3.5, top Yellow
Bumper Crop ("Each of your members brings you 2 of its color") is the best "members feel good to own" moment in the yellow pitches. Every card in your cult flies its coins in, and the source shows itself. It plays the same at 2 and 4 players. It's a solo engine: rivals mainly feel it as "she takes Bumper Crop first, again".
- Fixes: (1) Cut Gossip ("+2 of the color the follower leader holds most of"). Two lookups, ties in both, and a newcomer can't predict it. (2) Reword Feast: "+1 follower per member you have of another color" reads "other than what?". It should say "per non-yellow member". (3) Granary ("+1 follower per resource in your second-largest color") is a sum at game end that players can't track while they play. Swap it for Melting Pot from yellow-outsider ("+1 follower per non-yellow member you have"), which you can count by looking.
- Cards: Field Hands (a plain 3-body) and Sprouts are fine. Fieldworker is the template the designer already flagged.

### yellow-anchor B (Even Out): 3
"Raise one of your colors to match your largest" is one click on your own dot, and a big moment. Branch Out ("Gain half your largest color in any other colors") needs a split picker every time it's taken, which is a lot of clicks for a round II space. If Gold takes "Double one of your colors", Even Out is the same moment in a different color: two near-identical round III spaces on one table.
- Fixes: (1) Only if Gold doesn't widen. (2) Branch Out becomes one color: "half your largest, in one other color".
- Cards: Scarecrow ("Your largest color can't be stolen") flickers: which stack counts as largest changes as you play, and the protection moves with it. The victim (the thief) can't tell before clicking.

### yellow-outsider A (Dye): 2.5
Pour is a fine round III, and repainting cubes is easy to show on a real table. Fade ("Up to 3 of a rival's resources of one color turn yellow; gain that many of that color") is confusing to be on the receiving end of. Your red turns yellow, you lost nothing, and you just fell out of a race. The victim's first question will be "did she steal from me?", and the answer takes a paragraph. Pour wants a multi-select of your pile ("any of your resources").
- Fixes: (1) Fade becomes a member at most, never a space. (2) Pour: "Turn all of one color into another, then +1 per 2 turned". It's one click on your dot, then one on the target. (3) Chameleon ("At round end, your yellow also counts toward one other contest") needs a choice at round end, which pauses everyone, or a standing pick, which is hidden. Make it "toward the contest you're closest to leading" or cut it.
- Cards: Tiebreaker (duplicate). Colorfast only defends against this deck's own two cards.

### yellow-outsider B (Spotlight): 2
A sun on a rim is easy to show. But Spotlight does nothing on the turn you take it, and Headliner's "your yellow counts toward it" is the which-pile-counts-where problem. The rival's view ("my contest suddenly matters double and I did nothing") makes for some talk, but the leader gets richer: win-more by default, as the designer says.
- Fixes: cut.

## White

### white-anchor A (take the prize early): 4, top White, and the home for contest timing
One new rule, shown where players already look: the rim greys out with the taker's face. The two round II spaces make a visible two-turn plan with risk in between: Take the lead, then Cash a lead. That's the best rival talk on the panel at 4 players: "she'll cash red next turn, someone top up red". The victim loses a race they hoped to win at round end, which is mild, and they can see it coming. No followers enter the game, so it doesn't inflate.
- Fixes: (1) Cut Arbiter ("At round end, add your white to one other contest"). It's a round-end prompt that stops the scoring beat for one player's choice, and "which pile counts where" on the rims. Clerks or a second body takes the slot. (2) "Even odds" ("This round, contests you tie for the lead pay you in full") is a this-round rule shown only by a minion; the rim needs a small mark, or cut it for a third plain gain. (3) Heckler (rival cashes: they lose 1, you gain 1) and Holdout (the defense) do the same job. Keep Holdout.
- Cards: Magistrate and Crown (the verb on arrival) read cleanly. Bailiff ("+1 white per contest you lead") is fine.

### white-anchor B (followers on your members): 2
A follower number on each member card in every seat is the badge soup taste.md bans. Theft also gets out of hand: a Poach or Shake Down that takes a member carrying 6 followers is the "CRAZY bad" loss Cory drew the line at, and Shake Down does it in round II.
- Fixes: cut.

### white-outsider A (White catches up): 3
The table talk is real ("don't get too far ahead, White pays on your lead"), and Recluse finally has a home. But it adds a new number to every seat (how far you trail the leader), and it stacks a third catch-up on top of the snake and the trailer's first pick. A leader at a friends' table can feel that nothing they do is safe. Last Laugh ("Your follower buy this turn gives that many again, up to how many you trail the leader by") needs the gap read and the pack size together. At 2 players it's a pure rubber band. Hermit ("Worth 3 followers. Counts as no members") and Untouchable ("You never count as the leader") ask a newcomer to believe something the screen contradicts.
- Fixes: (1) Cut Tall Poppy ("the leader loses 2 followers, and you gain 2"). It's a follower swing on a 4-cost card, aimed at whoever is ahead. (2) Cut Untouchable and Hermit for plain bodies. (3) Lean Years ("+1 follower per member you have fewer than the rival with the most") is the clean core, readable from the seats. Build out from it, not from the gap number.
- Cards: Tiebreaker (duplicate). Nest Egg is Penny Pincher pointed at followers.

### white-outsider B (tie the leader): 2
"I was winning that, then White tied it" at every round end wears thin fast; the designer says so. Photo Finish ("raise every color to tie its leader") is a whole-table spoil in one click.
- Fixes: cut.

## Blue members

### blue-members deck A ("Every extra buy pays"): 3.5, top Blue
Plus-One, Shopaholic, Allowance and Entourage are one-line cards that feel good to own. Cory asked for Shopaholic by name. Two feel costs:
- Turns get long. Spree plus Shopaholic is 4 buys in one turn, and with Scalper ("When you buy a member, you may send a shop member to the bottom") each buy adds a yes/no bury prompt: up to 8 decisions while three friends wait.
- Insider ("The top card of the pile is face up; only you may buy it") puts an eighth card next to the row that only one player can touch. Rivals will try to click it.
- Fixes: (1) Cut Doorbuster ("Round start: you may buy once before anyone places"). It's a buy outside your turn, and it stops the whole table at every round start. The guest designer cut its cousin for the same reason. (2) Scalper's bury goes on arrival only ("When recruited: send up to 2 shop members to the bottom"), not on every buy. The hate-draft talk stays and the prompt fires once. (3) Insider needs a locked look and a hover saying who it's reserved for. If that can't be made plain, it goes.
- Cards: Bulk Buyer ("+1 follower per buy after your first") is fine for feel: it's counted automatically and paid on your own turn.

### blue-members deck B ("You set the row"): 2.5
Tastemaker ("Round start: look at the top 10 of the pile; choose the 7 that come out") is a private 7-of-10 choice made while everyone waits, at the moment the table most wants to start. It's the worst pause pitched tonight, and rivals feel controlled without seeing how. Talent Scout ("buy one of the top 5 cards of the pile") is another private look. Gatekeeper and Window Dresser are Blue's round I spaces on cards, near-copies of the board.
- Fixes: cut as a deck. Window Dresser is the one card worth trying in A.

## Collisions, from the feel seat

- **Contest timing.** One home: White (white-anchor A). It has the cleanest one-rule form ("take the prize, the rim greys with your face"), the best two-turn talk, and it adds no followers. The Count pays rivals and pays twice in a round, so it's confusing. Silver-outsider's version has halves and a hidden second place. Is it right at all? From this seat yes, in one color, because it moves contest talk from round end into the middle of the round. More than one color doing it makes the rims unreadable.
- **The shared pot.** Gold (gold-outsider A). It's on every Classic table, and the pot is the most legible new object pitched. Silver B goes.
- **"You win ties."** Feel-wise it's an invisible rule until it fires, then the rim has to explain why the tie went one way. One card in the game at most, the guest that's already with Cory. Cut Miser, Notary, Diplomat, Tiebreaker (Purple, White, Yellow) and Referee.
- **Round-start effects fire now.** Purple (Fast Forward), because the strategist's White lean is now taken by contest timing. It's the most watchable payout on the panel.
- **Doubling and pouring a stack.** One big "a color jumps" moment on the table: Gold's widened Double Down. Yellow's Even Out and Pour then look the same to a player watching.
- **Catch-up on the follower gap.** The snake and the trailer's first pick are already two layers. A whole color of it (white-outsider A) is one too many for the leader's fun. Keep it on members (Recluse, Lean Years style).
- **Launderer** stays a guest, not Gold or Yellow.

## Top concept per scope (feel)
- Gold: gold-outsider A, the Jackpot (4)
- Purple: purple-outsider 2, Put It Off (3.5), with Fast Forward brought in
- Silver: silver-anchor A, the Tide (4)
- Yellow: yellow-anchor A, members bring their colors (3.5)
- White: white-anchor A, take the prize early (4)
- Blue members: deck A (3.5), without Doorbuster
- Magenta members and guests: already with Cory; context only

## The one thing not to let through
**Chained turns and pauses where one player acts while the table waits.** Purple-anchor A stacks six turn-movers (Crunch Time, Head Start, Errand Runner, Double Shift, Late Riser, Night Owl), Rush Job adds a seventh, and Blue adds Doorbuster and Tastemaker as round-start stops. Each looks fine on paper. Together at a 4-player online table they turn friends into spectators. Budget: at most one back-to-back-turn source on the board (Crunch Time) and one extra-turn capstone per deck. No member acts at round start or round end in a way that needs a human choice.
