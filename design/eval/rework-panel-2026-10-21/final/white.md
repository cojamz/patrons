White is a genuine split: four critics put the early prize first, and the judge put catch-up first because it is Cory's own note. So both directions are written out in full below, and the first pick is the direction.

## What goes in both directions

Today's White trades followers (Sell High, Dividend, All In, Cheap Seats, the "loose follower" rule). Cory: "what's a loose follower?", and selling then buying back is the follower buy in another coat. All of it goes, in either direction: Sell High, Dividend, All In, Cheap Seats, the loose-follower rule, Rich Uncle, Heiress, Bulk Buyer (the name frees up for Blue), Broker, Exporter, Reinvestor (a cost cut), Raider. Kept: Shareholder ("Worth 3 followers", 2+1) and Magnate ("Game end: +1 follower per 3 white", 2+2), both live. Contest in both: **most white held**, the Oct 20 frame (live is still "most white members").

Both decks are short on purpose. Each card had to be one only White could have; where nothing good enough turned up, the slot stays open for Cory rather than padded.

# Direction 1: White takes the prize early

## Identity

White lets you take a contest's prize the moment you lead it ("it won't score at round end"), then spend the pile you were holding for it. Every other color makes you choose between holding to win a contest and spending on members; White lets a leader have both, paid for with a minion on a contested space. At the table, a narrow lead is no longer safe until round end: "she'll cash red next turn, someone top up red" is the new talk, and the rims get read all round instead of only at the end. Nothing is ever taken from anyone, and no followers are added to the game. An early prize replaces that contest's round-end prize, so the contests still hand out the same 2/4/6.

**One new rule (shown on the rim):** *Take a prize early*: you take that contest's prize now (2 / 4 / 6 by round). That contest is closed for the rest of the round: it won't score at round end, and nobody leads it. Next round it's a normal contest again. On screen the rim greys out with the taker's face and "taken early"; on a table, a token on the rim. You can only take a contest you lead outright: a tie isn't a lead, and holding none never leads (both live rules).

## Actions (every space ends +1 white)

### Round III: Settle Up
"Take now the prizes of up to two contests you lead. They won't score at round end. +1 white."
- Walk-away: leading 1–2 contests at your first round III turn: **7–13** (6 or 12 followers, +1 white). The bigger gain is that the piles you held for those contests are free to spend. In round III a held pile dies at game end, so the gain over "hold to the end" is about the smaller of the prize and the pile, per contest (guess, **8–13 all in**).
- 4 players: four contests, about one lead a player (contests per player stay constant because colors = players), the specialist usually two (white plus one). 2 players: two contests, so "up to two" is every contest, and after it nobody else has a contest left to play for that round. Swingy, but plain to read.
- Why "up to two": a hoarder leading three contests would take 18 plus three freed piles. The cap is rule 4's round III cap in prize terms.
- Wanted beyond the specialist: anyone leading their own color can cash it and spend that color on its members. The holder usually trails on followers and picks first in round III, so the anchor goes to whoever held, which is the core tension paying off rather than a fourth catch-up.
- Copy: flagged, the copier cashes their own leads. Unflagged, a copy would mint the taker's prizes from the bank and grow the pool.

### Round II (×2)
1. **Call It**: "Take now the prize of one contest you lead. It won't score at round end. +1 white."
   - Walk-away: round II **5** (4 followers + 1 white), plus the freed pile spent this round on members while they are in the row. Beside Shake Down (about 5) and above Promote (about 3.5). Round III **7**, plus a pile that would otherwise die: still the second-best space for the second leader once Settle Up is gone. Same at 2 and 4 players.
   - Honest marginal (guess): in round II a leader who would have held on anyway gains about 3–5 (the prize locked, plus spending sooner). Resources carry over, so the freed pile is worth tempo in round II and its full value only in round III.
   - Copy: flagged, the copier cashes their own lead.
2. **Draw Level**: "+1 white. Then choose a color you don't lead: gain enough of it to tie its leader (at most 5)."
   - It ties, it doesn't lead (the skeptic's fix): a tie can't be cashed, so the leader can still answer, and the leader can no longer cash it either. It's the counter to Call It as much as a setup for it. The +1 white comes first, so choosing white can't slip you one ahead.
   - Walk-away: round II leaders hold 3–5, you 0–2: **3–5**, plus blocking a cash, plus half the prize moving from them to you if the tie holds (about +2/−2). Round III **3–6**, plus a split 6. Same at 2 and 4 players.
   - The two round II spaces make a visible two-turn plan with risk in between: tie a lead now, get one more of the color somewhere, cash it next turn. Rivals can top up past you or take Call It first.
   - Copy: flagged (it reads the copier's own piles).

### Round I (×3, equal-ish gain, different side effects, all still worth taking in round III)
1. **Pick a Fight**: "Gain 1 × round of one color. +1 white." **2 / 3 / 4.** The flexible one: builds a lead anywhere, or pays a member's colored part. Copy: flagged, so the copier picks their own color.
2. **Sit Tight**: "+1 white per 3 resources you hold (at most 5). +1 white." Holding 3–5 / 6–8 / 9–15: **2 / 3 / 4–6.** Pays the holder before the cash-in. The cap stops a gold hoard (pricing finding 0.1). Flag: Gold's "half your gold" also pays holding; this counts every color, in white. If Cory reads them as twins, Sit Tight gives way. Copy: flagged.

Taking a prize early closes that contest, so it stops counting here: take Top Dog before you cash, which is a small sequencing puzzle and not a rule to remember. Bounded by the number of contests. Copy: flagged.

## Contest

## What changes at the table (and the risks)
- **For every color:** a leader can lock their own contest and spend down the same round, so White's round II and III spaces are wanted beyond the White player.
- **For rivals:** a narrow lead can be closed out from under you. You see it coming (the rim, the open Call It space) and can answer by topping up or with Draw Level.
- **Abuse checked:** the old "take the lead by 1, then cash" let a player strip a rival's prize in two placements, Crunch Time in between. With Draw Level tying instead, the cash needs a lead you built yourself. Cashing white every round is the White player leading their own color, which is what every specialist does.
- **Buy followers only:** gets the +1s, Pick a Fight and Draw Level's resources, nothing more. The early prize pays holders, then frees their spending.
- **Engine and screen (for the foreman):** one "taken early" flag per contest per round, a rim state for it, a button on led rims for Magistrate. Nothing else changes in the base rules.

# Direction 2: White catches up

## Identity

White pays you for being behind the leader on followers, up to drawing level and never past, and it pays only through the members you buy. It's Cory's own note ("Likes fewer followers as a catch-up"), with Recluse finally at home ("Recluse not in blue"). At the table, the follower leader becomes everyone's reference point: a big lead funds whoever is behind, and White's middle spaces aim at the leader's contests. Two guards answer the critics. First, every payoff is capped at your gap, so White alone can never put you ahead. Second, the big ones ride on a member buy ("if your buy this turn is a member…"), so they push the trailer toward members, the Oct 20 frame, and never multiply a follower pack, which keeps buy followers only out of it.

**One new number on each seat:** how many followers you're behind the leader (the leader's followers minus yours; 0 if you lead or tie). It's visible, it changes only when followers do, and nothing carries between rounds. "The leader" means the player with the most followers.

## Actions (every space ends +1 white)

### Round III: Last Laugh
"If your buy this turn is a member, also draw level with the leader: gain followers equal to how many you're behind, counted after the buy (at most 10). +1 white."
- Walk-away: 4 players, the far trailer (about 10–15 behind, 8–14 after a worth-1–3 member): **9–11 plus the member**. A middle player 5 behind: about 5. The leader: 1. 2 players: the trailer is about 4–10 behind and often draws exactly level: **4–10**.
- Why it's not a rubber band: the leader takes another color's round III space (Recruit a Member, Poach, about 10), so both players get about an anchor's worth. White just makes the trailer's anchor worth exactly the gap. Counting after the buy means the member's own followers are part of drawing level, so you can never pass the leader.
- Why it's off follower packs: the outsider's draft ("your follower buy gives that many again") paid the hoarder twice for a 10-pack. Here the buy must be a member. The sandbagger who held all game still has to turn the hoard into followers with the round III buys that remain.
- Copy: flagged (it reads the copier's gap and pays only followers; unflagged it would record nothing).
- Engine note: the bonus resolves after the buy, a "this turn" effect like Spree.

### Round II (×2)
1. **Fresh Blood**: "If your buy this turn is a member, also gain followers to halve how many you're behind the leader, counted after the buy (at most 5). +1 white."
   - The weak twin of Last Laugh: half the gap, not all of it (Encore beside Greatest Hits).
   - Walk-away: round II, 4 behind after the buy: **3 plus the member**; 10 behind: **6**. Round III 4–6. Leader 1. 2 players: round II 2–4.
   - Changes what you buy: a member instead of a pack, which is the round II bar.
   - Copy: flagged.
2. **Upset**: "+1 white. Then choose a contest the leader leads: gain enough of its color to tie them (at most 5)."
   - Catch-up through the board instead of a gift: it splits the leader's prize (about +2 for you, −2 for them in round II if the tie holds). It aims only at the follower leader, so it reads the leader's face on the rims, one read. The leader can't use it on themselves (+1 white).
   - Walk-away: round II **3–5 plus a split 4**, round III **3–6 plus a split 6**. Same at 2 and 4 players.
   - Copy: flagged.

2. **Small Wins** (replaces Your Call): "+1 follower × round. +1 white." **2 / 3 / 4.** Followers now shrink your own gap. Taking them is the White question: score now, or stay behind and let White pay you later. It differs from Save Up in kind (followers against white for the contest), so neither is an upgrade of the other. Copy: flagged (followers only).
3. **Sob Story** (live name, reworked): "+1 white per 2 followers you're behind the leader (at most 4). +1 white." Far trailer, 4 players: **1–2 / 3–5 / 5**; middle 1 / 2–3 / 3–4; leader 1. 2 players: 1–2 / 2–4 / 3–5. It grows by round because gaps do, and it works at 2 players (the live "per rival" version tops out at +1 there). Copy: flagged.

## What changes at the table (and the risks)
- **A fourth catch-up?** The snake, the trailer's first pick in round III, and Purple's turn tricks are already there, and Silver's final aims its gifts at the fewest followers too. The answer is that this one is capped at the gap and paid through members, and its middle spaces fight the leader's contests instead of gifting. The feel critic's worry stands: a leader at a friends' table may feel nothing is safe. Watch it at 2 players first.
- **Sandbagging:** hold everything, stay "behind", cash in late. Limits: Last Laugh and Fresh Blood need a member buy; contest prizes are followers and close your own gap; the hoard still needs round III buys; Magenta's half-a-pile steal. A real strategy with real risk, unproven.
- **Buy followers only:** usually leads on followers, so the gap spaces pay it nothing; it gets Save Up, Small Wins and Recluse.
- **If this direction wins:** the other "fewest followers" members (Desperado, Late Bloomers, Late Riser) should move or change, so catch-up has one home.

# The judge's objection to direction 1, weighed

His Oct 15 ❌ was on contests that count something other than held color ("own scoring shapes"), and his reason was "Scoring defaults to most of your resource held: it makes you weigh holding against spending." Direction 1 keeps what's counted and keeps the prize pool, but it goes straight at the reason: the whole point is to let a leader stop holding and spend, which softens the very weighing he wanted. The defense is that the weighing moves rather than disappears. You still have to hold enough to lead before you can cash, it costs a minion on a contested ring II or III space, and every rival now has to ask how big a margin is safe, so the hold-or-spend question gets asked mid-round by the whole table instead of only at round end. Two things still count against it. It changes how a base rule works (a "taken early" state on every rim), the kind of change he called "too much redesign" on Oct 9. And it makes contests the meal for one color when the principles call them seasoning. I'd put his yes on direction 1 near a coin flip, not the judge's 60% no, and direction 2 a little higher, because it's his own words.

**My pick: direction 1.** It's the only White that lives on the Oct 20 core tension instead of beside it. It plays with every color through the rims without taking anything, adds no followers, scales through the 2/4/6 prize itself, and its deck came out fuller and more White-only. Direction 2 is the honest second: his note, Recluse's home, and safe once it pays through members, but its verb is narrow (thin round II, a 6-card deck), and it stacks on the catch-up the board already has.
