# Blue members, final (rework panel, 2026-10-21)

Gate fixes applied (see gate.md).

**Every card text, cost and follower count here is Claude's fill-in, not approved.** Members only: Blue's actions are settled elsewhere (round I churn 3 / flip out a color / peek the top 4 and reorder; round II Buy twice; round III Spree, buy 3 times, +2 blue; every space +1 blue). Built from pitches/blue-members.md deck A, with the fixes all five critics asked for.

## Identity, and what changes at the table
Blue sells buys. Its spaces give almost no money (a Blue specialist makes about **7–8 blue a game** from spaces), so its members don't pay in blue; they make a turn with two, three or four buys the best turn on the board, and make every extra buy land on something. Nothing in the deck depends on which colors show up in the row. At the table: the Blue player's big turn is visible from their seat (Shopaholic, Personal Shopper), they reshape the row in the middle of their own buy step (Window Dresser, then buy into the refill), and every blue spent on a member is blue not held for the contest. Blue is only at 3- and 4-player tables (37 and 49 cards; about 1.9 and 1.4 blue cards per row), so every card stands alone.

## Contest (recommendation, flagged; asked once across colors, not as a Blue pick)
**Most blue held at round end**, like every other reworked color, instead of "most blue members". Why: "most members" pays the shopper twice for one buy and rides on what the row shows; held blue makes every Blue member's colored part (1–3) a real choice between the card and the contest. Risk: Blue's held numbers are small (2–5 at a round end), so ties will be common (halved prize). Allowance is what keeps the race from being a coin flip.

### Why each card, and its price
- **Plus-One** 1+1, worth 2. Buy twice on a card, the "one-time activate, elegant" shape. Round I it's a 2-body for 2, at par like Twins (the extra buy is near-free while money binds). Round III it's an extra buy worth about +1.5, and on a Spree it makes 4. It fires again if a rival's swap or free recruit moves it (Oct 18 rule): bounded, fine at 2.
- **Entourage** 1+2, worth 3. The cheap body that makes buy 3 and 4 of a Spree land on modest money. At par (f = C); a Shake Down target at 3, which is its safety valve. Flag: it's the one card here any color could have; kept because members are where followers come from and this deck is otherwise thin on bodies.
- **Window Dresser** 1+2, worth 2 (from deck B). The churn on a card with bodies (Panel's shape). Bought as your first buy, you then spend your second, third or fourth buy on the 3 fresh cards: "rearrange the shop and then buy a bunch" inside one buy step. It's also the deck's sting: you bury what the next players were waiting for. 2 bodies (about 2.6) plus a round I space's job (about 2) for 3. It replaces Scalper: Scalper's bury moved to arrival is this card, so one stays.
- **Allowance** 2+3. 1/2/3 blue by round, now and at round start: 6 bought in round I, 5 in round II, never less than 3. The money Blue lacks, and steady income is the kind of member Cory said feels right. By the 2X rule about 1.3 bought in round I, 1.1 in round II. It feeds the held-blue contest, so it sharpens hold or spend.
- **Personal Shopper** 2+3 (was Bulk Buyer, a live White name). Counts members you pay for only, so follower packs and free recruits (Open Door, Recruit a Member) never trigger it, and the buy-followers-only seat gets nothing. For a Blue specialist: two Buy twice turns +2, a Spree of 3 members +2, Plus-One +1, so about 4–5; with Shopaholic about 6–8. Near 0 for others. Value about 6 for 5 plus 2 blue of contest tax: about 1.0–1.15 alone, 1.4 with Shopaholic. Both together is a pair that sings (one player owns both maybe 15–25% of 4p games), each fine alone. Risk: Cory may read "+1 follower per…" as a drip; it is on your own turn, on the color's verb, and counted for you.
- **Snob** 3+3 (live 3+2). Kept text, one more any. The engine counts a worth-N member as N, so a committed Blue cult ends with about 6–8 blue bodies (Plus-One 2, Entourage 3, Window Dresser 2). Par for the committed, a loss for the dabbler. The "up to 10" cap is a guard; a realistic owner never reaches it. It's the deck's only scorer, and "go wide in your own color" is the plan Blue's extra buys make possible.
- **Shopaholic** 3+6. Cory's "you can always buy twice", which also turns Buy twice into 3 and Spree into 4. Worth about one follower a turn in round III plus the last turn of the game (up to 10 resources that would otherwise die): about 9–12 bought early in round II. It fits any build, and every price note he gave on universal cards said "too cheap", so 9. Colored 3 keeps it out of round I for anyone but Blue; left in the row in round III it is the best free pick for whoever takes "Recruit a Member". Flag: it also lets a hoarder buy two 10-follower packs a turn in round III. They're his words, so flagged, not restricted.

## Cut from the pitch, and why
- **Doorbuster** ("Round start: you may buy once before anyone places"): a buy outside your turn, and it stops the table at every round start. Out (no out-of-turn buys).
- **Insider** ("The top card of the pile is face up; only you may buy it"): a standing Claim, and Claim was ❌ ("promote and ransom are just straight up way better"). The likeness to know: it is also a narrow copy of Cory's own guest Insider ("You may also recruit from the top 3 of the pile"), which he okayed Oct 14 ("he wants more interaction with the pile") and then cut on Oct 15. Out; the slot stays open.
- **Scalper** ("When you buy a member, you may send a shop member to the bottom"): a prompt on every buy, up to 8 decisions in a Spree turn. Moved to arrival it becomes Window Dresser, so it's cut.
- **Recluse** ("+2 followers per rival with more members than you"): Cory's #17, "Recluse not in blue", and equal counts per color. It leaves Blue unchanged for whichever color takes it (the White and Silver revisers both have a claim). Nothing replaces it one-for-one: the stand-ins considered (Last Call, "buy twice while you have the fewest members") are variations of Spree and Shopaholic, so its slot stays open.
- From the live ten, cut as before: Stylist, Connoisseur, Patroness (pay on blue showing up, Stylist on rivals' turns), Planner, Underbidder, Sommelier (cost cuts), Social Climber (Release), Minimalist (Recluse's condition again).

## Skeleton check
One plain body (Entourage, flagged), one scorer (Snob), no defense card, no "+1 when you take this color's space", no round III capstone burst (Spree is already that). The three buy-adders of the pitch are down to two that differ in kind: once on arrival (Plus-One) and always (Shopaholic).

## The alternative: deck B, "you set the row"
- Blue as the color that picks what comes up, then collects its own: Tastemaker (choose the round's 7 from the top 10), Gatekeeper (flip on a card), Talent Scout, Curator (+1 blue per other blue member).
- Ranked second by every critic (2–3 of 5): Tastemaker is a private pause at round start and reads as Dig ("so weak"), and Gatekeeper and Window Dresser are Blue's round I spaces copied onto cards.
- Two of its cards pay on blue members showing up, the old Blue's luck problem, and it carried only 11 followers on 10 cards.
- Its best card, Window Dresser, is in the deck above.
