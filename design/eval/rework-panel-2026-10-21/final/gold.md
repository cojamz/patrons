## Identity

Gold is the money. Your members pay you gold (Tithe, Treasurer), gold goes further than any other resource when you spend it on members (Penny Pincher), and in round III whatever you held doubles. Every coin poses the core tension by itself: spend it on a member now, or keep it for the gold contest and Double Down. What changes at the table: Gold's spaces stop being the big outlier (2 to 4 a placement instead of Golden Years' +6), Double Down is no longer the gold leader's leftover in round III because anyone can double their best stack, and the gold spaces grow as the whole table piles onto them (Boomtown). Collect Debts stays as the one check on a hoard on the Classic table.

This is mostly Cory's Gold, made to fit the Oct 20 economy: 4 of 6 spaces and 6 of 7 members are his. The judge predicts "this is basically the same Gold". That's a fair read. The new idea is the alternative (the Jackpot), which is why it's the first pick.

## Actions

Every space ends "+1 gold". Caps follow brief rule 4, and a cap limits what you gain from the space **after** any member changes it (Underdog), so nothing multiplies past the printed ceiling. Build flag: today caps sit before Underdog's doubling in the engine, so this order is engine work.

### Round III: Double Down (widened, capped)
"Double one of your colors (at most +10). +1 gold."
- Why widen: live, "Double your gold" is worth 7 to 12 to the gold leader and 1 to 5 to anyone else, and at 4 players the leader picks last in round III, so it's the anchor left over for the one player it suits (win-more by default). Widened, the trailer picking first doubles their best stack. It's still Cory's verb and nearly his text, and it's his "double, half, match" principle. All five critics agree.
- Why cap at 10: Gold sits on every Classic table; uncapped, a 15-gold hoard turns one placement into +15. 10 is the member ceiling and the top follower pack.
- Walk-away: gold specialist holding 6 to 12 gold: **7 to 11**. Anyone else doubling a 5 to 8 stack: **6 to 9**, and that color's round III contest (6) often flips. Same at 2 and 4 players.
- Copy: flagged so the copier doubles one of their own colors. Greatest Hits on a turn you also hold Double Down redoes it (a second +10): accepted, it's the same as Greatest Hits redoing Recruit a Member, and it costs Green its anchor pick.

### Round II (×2)
1. **Tithe** (kept, capped): "+1 gold per member you have (at most 5). +1 gold."
   - Walk-away: round II **3 to 6** (2 to 5 members, multi-body members count), round III **6**. Same at 2 and 4 players. At 2 players the Red player, with Twins, Panel and Mob bodies, reaches the cap sooner, so both seats want it.
   - Why kept (the architect would take it off for Yellow's Potluck): Tithe is the board half of "members pay you gold", and Treasurer, the card Cory called "really good", is Tithe on arrival: the color's verb on a card. If Yellow's final makes Potluck its identity, the two are siblings in different currencies (one color vs each member's color); flagged for the map, not cut here.
   - Cap: counts bodies, so it prints 5 (rule 4).
   - Copy: replays the taker's gain. No flag needed.
2. **Collect Debts** (kept): "Take up to 3 gold from rivals, split however you like. +1 gold."
   - Walk-away: 4 players **4** in rounds II and III, and rivals lose up to 3 (theft counts twice: about +6 against the table, beside Shake Down). 2 players: the Red player holds 1 to 4 gold from forced round I picks and Kickback, so **2 to 4** in round II, **4** in round III.
   - Why kept (anchor and architect cut it as Magenta's verb): without it, on the Classic table (Gold, Red, Blue, Green) nothing touches piles at all, and the widened Double Down makes held stacks bigger. It's Cory's card, it only takes gold (Magenta takes half of any stack), and at 2 players it's the Red player's main way to fight the gold contest: 3 off the leader swings round II's 4 followers. If Cory sees it as Magenta's, cutting it leaves the Classic table with no pile check, and round II needs a new space (pick 2).
   - Copy: already redone by the copier (live); keep the flag.

Splurge ("This turn, gold you spend on a member counts double") is cut. Judge and feel: on the board it reads as the cost cut he dropped (Oct 18), it needs the shop to show doubled buying power, and it stacks or goes dead next to Penny Pincher. Penny Pincher, the card he liked, keeps the rule as a card. Half Again ("Gain half your gold") is cut too: it's Double Down's little brother and pays the gold leader in round I (judge, skeptic).

### Round I (×3): three kinds, by round, by gift, by the board
1. **Golden Years** (rescaled): "+1 gold × round. +1 gold." **2 / 3 / 4.** The plain one that scales. Today's +2 / +4 / +6 is above the economy. Copy: replay.
2. **Kickback** (kept, gift dropped): "+2 gold. +1 gold." **3 / 3 / 3.** The gift is gone: Cory said it was "too close to Silver's theme" (Oct 15) and Silver's final is all gifts (gate conflict 4). Front-loaded against Golden Years' back-loaded: the best first pick in round I, the weakest in round III. A scaling version ("+1 gold × round, +1 more in round I") was tried and dropped: it adds a rule to remember and lands at 3 / 3 / 4, nearly Golden Years. **Flag: this is the weak twin** of Golden Years (flat vs scaling, same verb); it holds because round I fills by rule, but it's the space most likely to want a new idea. Copy: replay.
3. **Boomtown** (new, in Intro Offer's place): "+1 gold per other minion on Gold spaces. +1 gold."
   - Walk-away: round I **1 to 3** (only 3 Gold spaces are open), round II **1 to 5**, round III **1 to 6**. Typical 2 / 3 / 4.
   - It grows during the round, the shape Cory said yes to twice (Bandwagon "dynamic as the round goes on", Catch Up 1/2/3 "tension between taking 2 now and hoping it's still there for 3"). It counts Gold placements, not players, so it's the same size at 2 and 4 players, the one good property of the Jackpot without a new object on the board. Every rival's Gold placement grows it: you read the wheel, not a pile.
   - Its sibling check: Golden Years is better early in a round, Boomtown better late; neither is a strict upgrade.
   - Purple's final cuts Rush Hour, and its Catch Up and Plan Ahead count your own minions, not a color's spaces: no overlap (gate conflict 5).
   - Copy: replay.

Cut from live: Intro Offer ("Your next member costs 4 less": the cost cut he dropped).

## Contest
Most gold held (unchanged). Tithe, Boomtown and Double Down push gold in; members, Penny Pincher and Collect Debts pull it out. Widening Double Down doesn't weaken the contest: the gold specialist still doubles gold, and a rival who doubles red has left the gold race alone.

## 2 players, Gold + Red (every Classic table starts here)
- Round I: 6 placements fill the 6 ring I spaces, so the Red player takes at least one Gold space and ends with gold. The gold contest (2) is real from the start.
- Round II: Collect Debts is the Red player's lever on the gold leader, and the gold player's way to keep him out of the race. Tithe is wanted by both; Red's multi-body cult hits the cap first.
- Round III: the anchors are Double Down and Recruit a Member ("Recruit a shop member", up to 10). The Red player holding 5 to 8 red wants Double Down; the Gold player wants a free 8 to 10 Gold capstone. **Both anchors are wanted by both seats**, so the trailer's first pick is a real choice, and the snake's double turn gives the other player the second anchor, not both.
- Underdog ("While anyone has more gold than you, your gold gains are doubled") is the Red player's best splash buy at 2 players: his forced Gold picks double until he passes the gold player, then it switches off. That's the interaction Cory liked.
- Buy-followers-only: gets 2 to 4 on every Gold space and spends it; Double Down doubles almost nothing for them; Collect Debts hurts them least (they hold little). I'd expect below 25% (a guess, not a measurement).
- The line to watch: hold all game, double in round III, buy 10-packs. The cap of 10 and round III's 5 buys limit it.

Fixes decided:
- **Speculator stays, capped; it may fire on a Double Down turn.** The skeptic's 10 → 21 → 42 dies with the caps: the same turn is at most +20 for an 8-cost capstone, the size of Tribute Band's 15 to 20. A "not on a Double Down turn" rule is one more thing to remember.
- **Midas cut** ("Your gains from actions come as gold"; live with a switch). With every action now paying +1 of its color, it turns all your verbs into gold contest stake and locks the gold contest by round II (skeptic), with Underdog it doubles every action you take (judge), and rivals can't tell why gold flies in (feel). It's his card from Oct 12, so it's pick 3.
- **Cut from live:** Prospector ("When an action gains you gold, +1 gold": now fires on every Gold placement, the "+1 when you take this color's space" slot every pitch had), Tax Collector (fires on rivals' turns about 15 times a game at 4 players, about 6 at 2), Merchant (rival-turn drip, needs gold members to show), Alchemist (fires on nearly every non-gold placement). Socialite and Tellers (pitched) are cut: a threshold "+2 when…" and a card-for-card copy of White's Shareholder.

Three slots open for Cory. Candidates, not padding: Midas without the switch if he wants the all-gold cult back; "Your gold pays any member's colored part" (his outsider-era "one member, not the anchor", but it's a subset of Freelancer, a guest).

## The alternative: the Jackpot (gold-outsider A)
- What: every Gold space taken puts 1 gold on the round III space from the bank; round III "Take all the gold here". The pot is about 3 / 7 / 8–11 at every table, since it counts Gold placements. Pricing (4/5) and feel (4/5) rated it highest: a coin pile with a number is the most readable shared object anyone pitched, and every Gold placement becomes a public "feed it or starve it" choice.
- Must-fix if chosen: copies must take the now-empty pot (Echo or Encore right after the Jackpot would mint 8 to 11 from nothing); Underdog doubles the pot for the trailer to about 20 (cap the pot at 10, or drop Underdog); Dividend becomes "half the Jackpot"; the round I trio becomes one Stake space plus two that differ in kind; High Roller (the leader takes the pot before the trailer's pick) is cut.
- Why second: it's a new object on the board, and Cory's new base rules come from him; the pot goes to whoever picks first in round III, not to the Gold player; and it risks his Lottery Winner "doesn't like it". The judge predicts "interesting" or "it's a lottery", about 40/35.
