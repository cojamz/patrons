## Identity

Silver is the generous host. Every Silver space hands the table something first, then pays you off what your rivals hold, and Silver is the one color that lets you give your own members away for value. Nothing is ever taken from anyone. At the table: you get coins on your seat whenever the Silver player moves, and the richest of you is the measuring stick, so the hoard you build to win the silver contest is what the Silver player cashes. Every gift Silver aims goes to the rival with the fewest followers, so the color props up whoever is behind instead of letting the Silver player pick a winner. Ties go to whoever picks earlier this round, the game's own order (in round I everyone is tied at 0, so this is what gives the first turns a target). The counterweight to "steroids" is built in: you can't run away on the Tides, because they read a rival's pile, and the round III one hands the silver contest to the rival it reads.

Built on Cory's own words: Rising Tide "I like this one a lot" (kept), "Silver members play on everyone having lots of silver… Silver's round III action is bad; tie it to that theme", "silver rounds II–III reward getting RID of silver members", Patron Saint "1 follower per silver member in play (max 10)", Retinue "more members, more followers", the Gossips worth-2 idea.

## Actions

### Round III: High Tide
"Everyone else +3 silver. Then gain 1 follower per silver the rival with the most holds, up to 10. +1 silver."
- Why it isn't "Rising Tide bigger": Rising Tide pays silver you then hold or spend; High Tide is the payout. It reads the whole pile, not half, and it pays followers straight off the space, skipping the buy in the round where the buy binds. The ladder is the one Cory approved for Red (Open Door's "the cheapest" to Recruit a Member's "any"), but the currency changes, so the two play differently: Rising Tide feeds your contest, High Tide feeds your score and usually hands the round III silver prize (6) to the rival it read. Take it and you trade the contest for the followers.
- Walk-away: 4 players, the richest rival holds about 3–7 before, 6–10 after the gift, so **6–10 followers + 1 silver**. 2 players: one rival at 2–5, so **5–8 followers + 1**. Cap 10 (rule 4).
- Wanted beyond the specialist: a trailer picking first in round III reads the Silver player's own hoard.
- Copy: flagged so the copier does it from their own seat (gives the +3s, reads their own richest rival). Without the flag a copy records nothing (followers only) or skips the gift.

### Round II (×2)
1. **Rising Tide** (kept, plus the +1): "Everyone else +2 silver. Then gain half the silver of the rival with the most (rounded up). +1 silver."
   - Walk-away: 4 players, round II 3–5, round III 4–6. 2 players, round II 3–4, round III 4–5. Half of a pile is self-capped near 5 in round II.
   - Copy: flagged (the copier gives the +2s). Today a copy skips the gift and beats the taker (skeptic, engine fact 1).
2. **Regift**: "Give one of your members to the rival with the fewest followers (ties: whoever picks earlier this round). Gain silver equal to its cost, up to 6. +1 silver."
   - Cory's "reward getting RID of members", as a gift, not a discard. The member stays in play (it still counts for Patron Saint), the trailer gets a body and its ongoing effect, and its On Recruit does not fire for them (live rule: a gift into a rival's cult is an arrival, not a Recruit).
   - Changes what you own and what a rival owns, so it passes the round II bar beside Promote and Shake Down.
   - Walk-away: a typical member held in round II costs 3–5 under Pricing v2 and is worth 1: **net +3 to +5** (cost + 1, minus the follower you hand over). Same at 2 and 4 players. A plain body worth its cost nets only the +1, so you give away members whose job is done: a spent On Recruit, an income card late in the game, a Fruitcake. Cap 6 because Cory called a cash-in "+10 blue in round 2".
   - Why the trailer, not "a rival you choose": the skeptic and feel seats both flagged chosen gifts as kingmaking at a friends' table. A fixed target keeps the choice on *which* member, which is the interesting one.
   - Give only, no discard: Cory's discard note was Oct 16; on Oct 17 he said "Release needs to go entirely", and "put it under the pile for its cost" is Release with a bigger refund. Giving keeps it Silver's verb ("you gave me WHAT?").
   - Abuse checked: a free recruit (Open Door, Recruit a Member) Regifted next turn for up to 6 also hands the trailer that member, so it's self-limiting (skeptic).
   - Copy: a player loses a member, so the engine already makes the copier do it themselves; flag it anyway.

### Round I (×3): the taker gets about the same, the table gets something different
1. **Toast**: "+1 silver × round. Everyone else +1 silver. +1 silver." You 2 / 3 / 4, each rival +1. Spill: broad silver that seeds the Tides and the contest. Cory's "gift grows in proportion with its own gain" was set when the gain was 3/6/9; at 2/3/4 the gift in proportion is about 1, so it stays flat (flagged). Copy: flagged, so the copier pays the gift.
2. **Good Deed**: "+1 silver × round. The rival with the fewest followers (ties: whoever picks earlier this round) gains 2 of a color you choose. +1 silver." You 2 / 3 / 4. Spill: aimed at the trailer, in a color you pick, so you can push them into a contest. Skeptic's kingmaking fix: the target is fixed (the trailer), the color is yours. Copy: flagged.
3. **Care Package** (new, trimmed): "Give the rival with the fewest followers (ties: whoever picks earlier this round) a shop member costing up to 2 / 3 / 4 by round. Gain silver equal to its cost. +1 silver." You 2–3 / 3–4 / 4–5. The cost cap came down one step so round I sits with Toast and Good Deed (2–3), not above them, and the free member to a rival in round I is a cheap body (Gossips, Shift Crew). Spill: a whole member, from the shop, to the trailer. It differs from Toast and Good Deed in kind (a member, not resources) and scales by round through the cost cap. The trailer gets about what you gain, so your edge on them is the +1; your edge on everyone else is the whole gain. Copy: takes from the shared shop, so flagged.

Cut: Hold Court, Sweet Talk, Goody Bags (Cory asked), Toast's 3/6/9 (the steroids), and the anchor's Warm Welcome ("Gain 1 follower × round; a rival you choose gains 1 follower": kingmaking, a buy-followers space, and close to White's Your Call).

## Contest
Most silver held (unchanged). Every space puts silver in rivals' hands, and rivals who hold it to fight you for the contest are exactly what the Tides read; rivals who spend it hand you the contest. High Tide gives each rival 3 and you only 1, so taking it usually concedes round III's prize: the burst and the prize pull against each other.

A Silver specialist earns about **18–22 silver a game** (Toast/Good Deed/Care Package 2–5 a placement, Rising Tide 3–6, Regift 3–6; High Tide pays followers, not silver), plus Benefactor or Upstart. The deck's colored parts sum to 15, so 4–5 Silver members cost a specialist about 9 silver: affordable without giving up the contest every round. ## Risks, flagged
- Table injection: one High Tide hands 9 silver to a 4-player table. Dial if it still feels pumped: High Tide's gift 3 → 2.
- 2 players: Care Package and Regift always feed your only rival, so they're close to even trades there; the Tides run 1–2 lower. Silver isn't on the Classic tables.
- Screen: Toast, both Tides and Retinue send coins to three seats on one turn. Batch the gifts into one flight per seat (feel seat, taste rule 10).
- Care Package is the weakest card on the board here: it can whiff when the row has nothing cheap enough (round I cap 2), which is on the player.

## The alternative: silver-outsider B, lift the table then match the top
- Every Silver gain matches or half-matches the best-off rival after you've handed the table something: High Tide (gain the richest rival's silver), Rising Tide, Keep Up ("gain enough of a color to tie the rival with the most"), Climb ("half the gap").
- Same giving heart as the Tide, and it keeps Rising Tide.
- Ranked second: four of six spaces are matches ("slight variations"), Keep Up is White outsider's Keep Pace word for word, and relative "tie the top" gains run 10–12 in round II uncapped.
- Its deck was mostly repriced live cards, with no member-giving and no Regift, so it misses his "get rid of members" note.
- Not the contest-calling concept (contest timing goes to White) and not the pot (Gold's alternative).
