# Cory judge: predicting his verdict on a pitch (2026-10-21)

Built from his verdicts and calls up to Oct 18 only (judge-calibration/ledger-to-oct18.md, decisions-to-oct18.md, member-feel.md, neutral-members.md, live-cards.txt), then tested on 15 pitches from Oct 19–20.

**Contamination warning.** The task brief that launched this judge listed the Oct 19–20 answers in short, so the "blind" predictions below were written by a judge that had already seen them. I wrote each prediction from the v1 rubric and say where the rubric alone would have pointed, but the score is an upper bound. A truly blind run on a fresh set (for example the Oct 21 rework proposals once he answers them) is the real test.

## Step 1. Rubric v1 (from material to Oct 18)

1. **Round band.** Each slot has a strength band; he says no to both sides. Round I: "each sphere has a round-I action giving up to 3 resources with a condition or flavor attached" (Oct 15). Too strong: "Bloom (+6 early) is crazy" (Oct 14); "a free swap plus +3 red; an extra recruit plus +2: too strong" (Oct 10). Too weak: Red III Rally/Encore/Payroll "weren't strong enough" (Oct 13). Round III "just needs to be powerful in general" (Oct 9).
2. **Simple, trackable, shows on screen and on a real table.** "Cards should carry over well into a physical board game" (Oct 15); Big Spender "hard to make obvious in the UI", Sleepwalker "too hard to track", Lender "a little too complicated" (Oct 15); "We're getting too complicated. Start basic" (Oct 12).
3. **Not a variation of what exists.** "Copy your last action… that's basically Reprise" (Oct 18); "just variations on what exists" (Oct 15); no "members within a sphere that are slight variations of each other" (Oct 15). Cash In and Magnate "redundant" (Oct 14).
4. **Think broadly about how the color plays with the others.** "Not thinking broadly enough… think about how the quadrant plays with the others" (Oct 18); "need better pitches… deeper options grounded in the game" (Oct 18).
5. **No board action that charges to use it.** "overtime fucking SUCKS"; "no board action should require spending" (Oct 15).
6. **No out-of-turn minions; no extra placement in round I.** "I don't like your neutrals. Esp ones where players interject out of order" (Oct 14); "no extra minion placement in round I" (Oct 15).
7. **No rescue clauses.** "taking a space or card you can't use is on the player" (Oct 14).
8. **Relative, open amounts that grow.** "Everything should feel vaguely powerful. Open, relative amounts (double, half and so on) beat small fixed numbers" (Oct 9). Echo is "absolutely perfect… scales because it copies better actions later" (Oct 18). Top Up "too bad in Round 3" → "scales by round" (Oct 17–18).
9. **He sets the numbers.** He replaces pitched numbers with his own (his-call tag 0✅ 3❌): Bribe "any player, the gold goes to the bank" (Oct 10), Extra Hand "make it cost 9". Pitch the shape; expect him to retune.
10. **Interaction is wanted, mild sting fine; crazy-bad only in round III.** "Stuff can feel bad… just not CRAZY bad, like straight up losing a member (unless it's a round-III action)" (Oct 15). He overrides harm-points-up freely (Collect Debts, Bribe, Poach). Member movement "is wanted" (Oct 14).
11. **Every action feels GOOD to take, and distinct** (Oct 15). Weak twins are fine for draft texture: "Weaker versions of other actions are good" (Oct 13), Open Door beside Open House.
12. **Members: feel good to own.** "Members should feel good and fun to have in your cult" (Oct 15, principle zero). The weak feel is the "+1 when…" triggers; scorers and round-start income feel right (Oct 15). Rule-benders are loved (Squatter "really great", Recluse "I love this one a lot", Doppelganger "really good").
13. **A card lives where its idea lives; the name says what it does.** "copying is Green, buying more than once Red or Blue" (Oct 15). Neutrals stay neutral (Oct 18).
14. **Blue's brief is his.** Blue "needs a complete rework"; "rearrange the shop and then buy a bunch" (Oct 17); Snap Up "is good", Show Off "is trash" (Oct 17).
15. **Core loop and base rules stay.** "too much redesign" (Oct 9); Barter "no, I don't like that" (Oct 14). New base rules come from him, often parked: the discard pile was his own "idea, not now" (Oct 15).

Procedure: (a) Is it in the right color and round? (b) Hard nos first: spends to use, out-of-turn, rescue clause, round-I extra placement, too hard to track. (c) Put it in its round band: what does the taker walk away with this turn? (d) Is it a copy of a sibling or another color's card? (e) Does it grow with the round or the table? (f) Expect him to keep the shape and change the number.

### Misses, and the rule that would have caught each
- **9 Dig (miss, his strongest words).** A round II space whose whole effect is rearranging the shop gives the taker nothing this turn: no resources, no member, no followers. The rubric saw "shop-mixing he asked for" and stopped. Rule: **the walk-away test**: count what the taker gains this turn and set it next to the strongest action in that ring on the live board (Promote, Shake Down). A setup-only action loses that comparison in rounds II–III. His "rearrange the shop and then buy a bunch" means rearranging comes with the buy, not instead of it.
- **13 Peek (miss).** I applied the walk-away test too hard in round I: a cheap, simple, on-theme round I space that steers your own next buy is fine to him at round I. Then he flagged "don't scale". Rule: **round I can be light if it is simple and in the color's brief, but it must grow by round** (Echo is his bar). Fixed shop effects (peek 3, churn 3) don't grow, so they draw a yes now and a scaling note later.
- **2 Exchange (reason half).** Rule: **redundant with a choice the card already gives**: a steal already lets you pick which colors to take, so converting them adds nothing.
- **3 Lie Low (half).** His fuller note (decisions, Oct 19): "Actions must remain worthwhile later and avoid a dominant first move." Rule: **check against its siblings in the same ring.** A new space that does the sibling's job plus a bonus outclasses it; he keeps the shape and trims it to the sibling's number. Weak/strong twins should differ in kind, not as a strict upgrade.
- **4 Steal 2/4/6 (half).** Rule: **theft counts twice** (you gain, a rival loses), so steal numbers sit near half of the plain gains for the same round: 1/2/3 beside +1-per-action colors. His own Oct 18 "2/4/6" call was superseded once every action gained its +1 inherent.
- **7 Churn (reason half).** Same scaling rule as 13.
- **8 Claim (reason half).** The anchor he used was the strongest action in the same ring on the live board (Red's Promote, Magenta's Shake Down), not Red's free recruits in general. Same rule as 9.
- **1 Extra follower buy.** Hit, but the rule should be explicit: **an extra buy is worth what you can pay for**: little in round I, a lot in round III (the extra-buy note of Oct 15 says one buy now soaks up 10 resources).
- **15 Between-round trigger.** Hit. Rule made explicit: **count the triggers**: the shop refreshes every round, so "whenever cards leave the shop" fires on the whole row.

## Rubric v2 (use this one)

What predicts his verdict, in order of weight:
1. Walk-away test: what does the taker gain this turn (resources, a member, followers)? Set it beside the strongest action in the same ring on the live board (Promote, Shake Down ransom, Red's recruits). Setup-only effects lose in rounds II–III: Dig, "so weak… you dont understand the game at all" (Oct 20).
2. Round band: round I is about +3 with flavor, round II a clear step up, round III powerful. Too strong is as dead as too weak: "+10 blue in round 2" (Oct 20), "Bloom (+6 early) is crazy" (Oct 14).
3. Theft counts twice: steals sit near half of a plain gain (1/2/3 by round), since a rival loses what you gain.
4. Extra buys and conversions are worth what you can pay: poor in round I ("poor in Round I", Oct 19), strong in round III (Spree, Buy twice).
5. Scales by round: open, relative or growing amounts beat fixed ones. Echo is the bar (Oct 18); fixed effects get "kinda don't scale" (Oct 20).
6. Sibling check: no strict upgrade of a sibling in the same ring, and no dominant first move; it must stay worthwhile later. He keeps the shape and trims the number (Lie Low, Oct 19).
7. Not redundant with a choice players already have, inside the card or in another color (a steal already picks its colors, Oct 19).
8. Simple, trackable on a real table, easy to show: "too hard to track", "a little too complicated" (Oct 15). Interesting but not complicated.
9. Count the triggers: between-round refreshes and "anyone does X" fire far more than they look (Oct 20, "too strong between rounds").
10. Hard nos: a board action you pay to use ("overtime fucking SUCKS"), out-of-turn minions, extra placement in round I, rescue clauses, wholesale base-rule changes ("not for now").
11. Lives in its idea's color; buying more than once is Red or Blue; Blue is "rearrange the shop and then buy a bunch", the rearranging riding on a buy or a gain.
12. Interaction is welcome and aggression in Magenta is intentional; a mild sting anywhere, losing a member only in round III.
13. Members: fun to own, relative or growing payoffs on your turn or at round start, rule-benders priced for round-III power; "+1 when a rival…" drips feel weak.
14. He sets the numbers: pitch the shape with a defended number, and expect "should just be 3".
15. Breadth: show how the card plays with the other colors, not three variations of one verb ("not thinking broadly enough", Oct 18).

Procedure: hard nos (10) → color fit (11) → walk-away and band (1, 2) with theft and buy adjustments (3, 4) → sibling and redundancy (6, 7) → scaling and triggers (5, 9) → simplicity (8). Predict "changed" when the shape passes but the number breaks the band or beats a sibling.

## Blind test 2 (members, fresh agent given only rubric v2) — scored by the foreman
The first test leaked its answers in the brief (the foreman's error), so a fresh agent with only rubric v2 predicted 18 past member verdicts from Oct 14–18.
Score: about 10 of 18. Right: Doppelganger, Squatter, Sleepwalker, Lender, Rainmaker, Treasurer, Banker at 8, Stunt Double, Bookkeeper, Penny Pincher (Big Spender, Retinue, Ensemble half). Wrong, and the rule each miss adds:
- **Recluse** (predicted meh; he said "I love this one a lot"). The rubric copied Claude's own Oct 15 audit, which ranked Recluse 9th worst for "paying you for losing the member race". Cory's view is the opposite: a member that makes a *different cult* viable (stay small, win anyway) is the best kind. → Rule: members that open a contrarian plan are prized ("members that make radically different cults"). Never use design/eval/member-feel.md as a proxy for his taste; it is Claude's.
- **Underdog** (predicted dislike as a rescue clause; he liked it, "interactive"). A rescue clause is a fallback for a space you can't use. A member that rewards being behind on a color, and reads rivals' piles, is interaction he likes. → Rule: catch-up keyed to visible rival state is not a rescue clause.
- **Hustler** (predicted like for scaling; he said "bad, nothing players have to remember"). Scaling doesn't rescue a card that asks you to pick and remember a hidden choice. → Rule: memory beats scaling; a chosen color or target must be visible on the card or it's out.
- **Lottery Winner, Mime** (predicted like; he didn't like Lottery Winner, "doesn't love" members that pay for themselves at once). → Rule: a one-shot windfall sized off a rival's pile reads as cheap; he prefers ongoing shapes or one-shots you time (Stunt Double, Tribute Band, Speculator-style).
