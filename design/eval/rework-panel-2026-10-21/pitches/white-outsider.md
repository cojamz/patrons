# White, outsider seat (rework panel, 2026-10-21)

**All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.** No sims, no games: arithmetic from the live rules and the panel's pricing.md. Estimates of follower gaps are guesses, marked (guess).

## Where I start: what the table lacks, and how a White player makes others care

After the reworks every finished color acts *on rivals' stuff*: Red moves their members, Green copies their spaces, Magenta takes their piles, Blue churns the row they buy from. The proposed spread fills in multiplying (Gold), reshaping (Yellow), sharing (Silver) and timing (Purple). Two things are still missing from the table:

1. **Nobody plays the follower track itself.** The base game has one comeback engine (fewest followers pick first, and the trailer takes the best round III anchor). Nothing pays you for *being* behind, and nothing makes a big lead feel unsafe without theft. Cory's two favorite catch-up cards (Recluse "I love this one a lot", Underdog "likes it, interactive") have no home that is about them. His own note #17 says "Recluse not in blue".
2. **Nothing plays the contests from outside.** Five contests on held color, and the only way to touch a rival's lead is to steal from it (Magenta) or outgrow it. A tie halves the prize, and no card uses that.

What White drops: trading followers. Cory found it confusing ("what's a loose follower?"), and selling and buying back is the follower buy in another coat. Under the Oct 20 economy, White keeps the follower buy as it is and bends *when it pays you*.

My two concepts answer the two gaps. Neither is the strategist's lean ("your members work twice"), on purpose: that lean is the anchor seat's ground, and two of the three seats would otherwise sit on the same idea.

One new number both concepts read, shown on each player's seat (a UI need): **how many followers you trail the leader by** (the leader's followers minus yours; 0 if you lead or tie). It's visible state, it changes only on buys and scoring, and nothing carries between rounds.

Rough gaps to the follower leader (guess, 4 players, from pricing.md's ~7/13/23 value per round): end of round I about 0–4, round II about 3–10, round III about 6–15. At 2 players the gap is head-to-head and a little smaller. **Gaps grow by round on their own**, so anything that reads the gap scales without printing "× round".

## Concept A: White catches up (recommended)

**Identity in one mechanic:** White pays you for trailing the leader, and its big payoffs stop when you draw level: you can catch up on White, never pull ahead on it. Distinct from Purple's Catch Up (minions placed this round), from Magenta (nobody loses anything except on one member), and from Blue (White changes the size of one follower buy, never the number of buys).

**The cult it makes:** stay light on followers, hold resources (which also wins contests), keep few members, then spring in round III. Its opposite is the Red body-builder. Cory's "radically different cults" (Recluse against Mob) as a whole color.

### Actions

**Round III, the burst**
- **Last Laugh:** "Your follower buy this turn gives that many again, up to how many you trail the leader by. +1 white."
  - Trailer by 12 who buys a pack of 10: +10 more, +1 white = **11**. Middle player trailing by 5 with a pack of 10: +5 = **6**. Leader: 1.
  - In band (8–12) for whoever is behind, and the trailer picks first in round III, so it's the table's catch-up anchor. Anyone behind wants it, not just the White player, which matters at small tables (strategy-read: anchors must be wanted by more than the specialist).
  - It uses the buy, so it lives with the 10-pack cap and the round III buy squeeze. Green can copy it (a trailing copier gets it too).

**Round II, change what you own or how much you buy**
- **Lean Years:** "+1 follower per member you have fewer than the rival with the most. +1 white."
  - Reads members (Recluse's measure), not followers. Round II member gaps about 1–5 → **2–6**. The member leader gets 1.
  - Changes what you own (followers, no buy spent). Rivals building bodies (Mob, Twins, Panel) feed it.
- **Nest Egg:** "This turn, white you spend buying followers counts double. +1 white."
  - A White player holds about 4–7 white by round II, so a pack of 5 paid in white gives 10: **+5–7**. Changes how much you buy.
  - The tension is the contest: every white spent here leaves the white contest. Hold for the prize, or spend it at double.
  - Flag: same shape as Gold's Penny Pincher ("gold you spend on members counts double") turned toward followers. If Gold keeps Penny Pincher, this slot needs a different round II (see the weakest-card note).

**Round I, equal-ish total, different side effects**
- **Save Up:** "+1 white × round. +1 white." Totals 2 / 3 / 4. The plain anchor, like Gather and Build Up.
- **Sob Story** (reworked from live "+2 white, +1 per rival with more followers"): "+1 white per 2 you trail the leader by (rounded up). +1 white." Round I 1–3, round II 2–6, round III 2–8 for the far trailer. It grows because gaps grow, and it works the same at 2 and 4 players (the live per-rival version tops out at +1 at 2 players).
- **Your Call** (live, kept, now with the +1): "+1 follower × round; take 2 white in place of any. +1 white." Round I 2–3, round III 4–7. The White question in one space: take followers now (and shrink your gap) or white (stay behind, hold for the contest).

Sob Story and Save Up differ in kind: Save Up is the same for everyone, Sob Story is nearly dead for the leader and rich for the trailer. Neither is a strict upgrade of the other.

**Contest:** held white (Cory, Oct 15 default). The specialist earns white everywhere, Nest Egg tempts them to spend it, and Last Laugh wants a big pack. Three pulls on one pile.

**How it touches the table:** the follower leader becomes everyone's reference point, and every White space grows with their lead. A big early lead is no longer safe: it funds whoever is behind. Bodies matter to rivals in a new way, since a big cult feeds Lean Years. Players who hold resources (contests, theft targets) look "behind" on the track, so Magenta's Crime Wave is the natural check on a White sandbagger. No effect counts table colors, and rival counts appear only on Recluse.

### Self-critique
- **Specialist's round:** round I takes Save Up or Your Call for white; buys Shopkeeper or Hermit; avoids follower packs. Round II Nest Egg or Lean Years, Long Shot if it shows. Round III picks first as the trailer, takes Last Laugh for about +11, then cashes out. It plays very differently from a Red cult, which is the point.
- **A rival:** sees White spaces grow with their lead, so leading by 15 has a cost. Leaders can't block Last Laugh (the trailer picks first in round III), but they can buy Untouchable, or keep the gap small. A newcomer reads "trail the leader by", which is one number on the seat.
- **2 players:** the gap is head-to-head, so everything is symmetric and swings hard. Whoever is behind owns White. Last Laugh plus the trailer's first pick could feel like a rubber band. Watch it first at 2 players. Recluse pays at most 2 there (one rival), so it's weak, a known cost of keeping his card as it is.
- **4 players:** gaps are wider, so White is richer, and the middle players get value too.
- **Sandbagging:** holding resources keeps you "behind" while you're rich. Three limits: the 10-pack cap and one buy per turn (you can't cash a hoard out in one go), Crime Wave, and contest prizes, which are followers and close your own gap. I think that's a real strategy with real risk, not an exploit. Unproven.
- **Rubric v3:** opens a contrarian cult (16 ✓), catch-up keyed to visible rival state (17 ✓), Last Laugh passes the walk-away test for trailers, gaps scale by round (5 ✓), nothing to remember (18 ✓). Risks: "too much catch-up on top of the snake", and every space being near-dead for the leader. Prediction: yes on the direction, ~60%; he retunes Last Laugh's cap and Nest Egg.
- **Weakest card:** Nest Egg. It's a Penny Pincher echo, and it's the one White space that doesn't read rivals. I kept it because White needs one space that answers holding against spending on its own pile. If Gold keeps Penny Pincher, replace it with a round II that changes what you own without reading the gap (open; I didn't find one I trust).

## Concept B: White ties the leader (the spoiler)

**Identity in one mechanic:** White raises your pile of a color to *tie* whoever holds the most of it. A tie halves the prize, so White turns other players' contest leads into splits. Magenta makes the leader lose, Gold grows your own stack, Yellow reshapes your own pile. White's reference is always a rival's lead, and it never takes from them. Cory's precedent is Rising Tide ("gain half the silver of the rival with the most": "I like this one a lot"), an action that reads a rival's pile, not a member that pays for itself (Mime and Lottery Winner, both disliked).

**The cult it makes:** hold a little of everything, read the rims, place late in the round so your ties hold at round end. Every contest leader on the table watches the White player.

### Actions
- **III, Dead Heat:** "Choose two colors: raise each to tie the rival who holds the most of it. +1 white."
  - Round III leaders hold about 5–9, you hold 1–3: +4–6 each → **9–13**, plus two spoiled 6-prizes (+3 for you, −3 for each leader, if the tie holds to round end).
  - The tension is timing. Take it early and leaders can top up past you. Wait and someone else takes it.
- **II, Keep Pace:** "Choose a color: raise it to tie the rival who holds the most of it. +1 white." Round II: **4–6**, plus a spoiled 4-prize. Keep Pace to Dead Heat is the copy-one / copy-every pattern he accepted in Green (Encore to Greatest Hits).
- **II, Close Ranks:** "+1 follower per 2 you trail the leader by. +1 white." Round II trailer 4–6, leader 1. The same tie verb on the follower track; it changes what you own.
- **I, Save Up:** "+1 white × round. +1 white." 2 / 3 / 4.
- **I, Tag Along:** "Choose a color: +1 of it per 2 you trail its leader by. +1 white." Round I 2–3, round III 3–5. A small Keep Pace that grows as leads grow.
- **I, Your Call** (live, kept, +1 white): as in A.

**Contest:** held white. The choice is whether to tie the white leader with Keep Pace (spoil your own contest's rival) or tie someone else's color.

**How it touches the table:** every color's leader now needs a margin, not just the most, so the whole table holds more and spends less, which sharpens Cory's core tension for everyone. It works at 2 players (one leader per color is all it needs) and counts no table colors. Bodies don't matter to it. It feeds Magenta (White players end up holding fat, spread piles).

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** holds a spread and reads rims all game. It's a real skill, but it asks more reading than A. A newcomer has to know contest rules cold to see why tying matters.
- **Rival:** the leader of any color feels it. That's good interaction, but the feel can be "I was winning that, then White tied it" at every round end, and that might wear thin.
- **2 players:** strong. One rival leads most colors, so Dead Heat always has targets. **4 players:** more targets, more choice, more reading.
- **Dead Heat's arithmetic is the risk:** 9–13 resources plus two spoiled prizes sits above band, and with Tiebreaker it's a contest sweep. One color plus "+1 white × round" might be the honest size.
- **Rubric v3:** interaction through the board (4 ✓), relative and scaling (5 ✓), a different way to win (16, partly: it's a skill more than a cult). Risks: complexity (8), "Rainmaker / Rising Tide variation" (6–7), Photo Finish too swingy. Prediction: ↔, about 45–50%. He'd like the spoiler idea and cut the deck down.
- **Weakest card:** Odd Lots. It's a good rule-bender but not White's idea. I kept it to show the slot; it's probably a guest.
- The round II pair is thin: Close Ranks borrows A's verb. B's identity carries round III and the members better than round II, which is a sign it's a narrower color than A.

## Recommendation
A, White catches up. It gives the table something no color does (a reason to fear a big lead without theft), gives Cory's favorite member shapes, Recluse and Underdog's "be behind", a whole color, and every number grows on its own because gaps grow by round.
It reads one visible number, it's the same at 2 and 4 players, and it's the opposite cult from Red.
B's tie-the-leader verb is worth keeping in reserve: one B card (Tiebreaker) already sits in A's deck, and Keep Pace could replace Nest Egg if Cory wants White to touch contests too.

## Appendix: seeds (12), and why each was cut or kept
1. **Catch up on the follower track** (tempo/rivals): kept → A.
2. **Tie the contest leader** (rivals/holding): kept → B.
3. **Members work twice** (fire round-start effects again): the strategist's lean, left to the anchor seat. In reworked decks few members have round-start effects (Host, Busker, Connector, Cutpurse), so the round III burst has thin fuel.
4. **Hire a rival's member** (use its effect, its owner gets 1 follower): positive interaction, but the rules for which effects can be borrowed get long, and Cory sent "borrow a recruit" to Red.
5. **Round-end wagers** (a White space pays at round end, read from the board then; your minion shows the bet): fresh and visible, but every version I wrote became All In again (held resources become followers without a buy) or needed if/else text. Cut, complex.
6. **White is wild in contests** (assign your white to a color at round end): the strategist's "judge". A decision for each holder at every round end, and it's hard to show which pile counts where. Cut, too complex; B is its simple form.
7. **Lean cult only** (fewer members pays): folded into A (Lean Years, Hermit, Recluse). Alone it's a contrarian set of members, not a color, and per-rival counts shrink at 2 players.
8. **The follower buy's size** (no 10 cap, any amount): the buy binds only in round III, so it's weak before then (pricing.md §1). It survives as Odd Lots and Windfall.
9. **Sell a member back to the shop for its cost**: Release in another coat, and Blue's "cash in" was "way too strong". Cut.
10. **Follower theft from the leader as the color** (Raider grown up): steps on Magenta's "take", and theft counts twice. Survives only as one mild member (Tall Poppy).
11. **Pacts and positive-sum deals** (pay a rival, both gain): Silver's sharing ground, and anything where the rival decides is "they pick" (rejected Oct 13). Cut.
12. **What if White didn't exist?** The table would miss nothing today: follower trading is a second follower buy. That's why the outsider answer is to give White the one job the table lacks (1), not to repair trading.
