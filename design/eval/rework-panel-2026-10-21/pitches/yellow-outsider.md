# Yellow, actions and members — outsider seat (2026-10-21)

**All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.**

Seat: an experienced designer seeing Yellow fresh. Starting point, as briefed: under held-color contests and colored member costs, the *color* of a resource now decides two things it never did before, which members you can buy and which contest you're in. Today the only ways to change a resource's color are Trade Seeds ("+2 yellow, then swap up to 3") and theft (Magenta keeps the colors it takes). That is the hole on the table.

Constraint kept throughout: nothing scales with the number of colors on the table. Every "each color" / "per color you hold" / "full set" text is gone (that is what makes Yellow weak at 2–3 players, Cory #84).

---

## Concept A — Dye: Yellow decides what color a pile is (recommended)

**Identity in one mechanic:** change the color of resources, your own (to reach any member's colored part, to enter any contest) and, a little, a rival's (to knock them off a lead). No value ever changes hands: dyed resources still buy followers 1-for-1. Red moves members, Green copies, Magenta takes, Blue works the shop; Yellow repaints.

**Contest:** most yellow held (the default). Built-in counterweight: every rival you dye gets yellow, which puts them in *your* contest.

### Round III (the burst)
- **Pour** — "Turn any of your resources into one color, then gain 1 of it per 2 turned. +1 yellow."
  - Arithmetic: a round III pile is about 12–16. Pour 10–12 → +5–6, +1 yellow = **+6–7 resources**, and you now lead that color's contest almost for sure (6 followers) if you hold it to round end. About **9–12** all in, inside the band.
  - Anyone with a pile wants it, not only the yellow player, and the trailer picks first. It works the same at 2 and 4 players: it counts your pile, not the table.
  - It sets up the late contest swing the held-color game lacks. It also builds the biggest single stack on the board, which makes the pourer the obvious Crime Wave target (half a stack) and Fade target.

### Round II (×2, must change what you own, measured against Promote / Shake Down)
- **Fade** — "Up to 3 of a rival's resources of one color turn yellow; gain that many of that color. +1 yellow."
  - Taker: +3 of a color, +1 yellow = **4**. The rival keeps every unit, but drops up to 3 in that color's contest and may lose access to a colored part. The contest swing is 6.
  - It has the same aim as Crime Wave (one rival's stack of one color), but a different result: Crime Wave moves value from them to you, Fade moves only *color*. Flagged as the closest neighbor on the board.
  - "Up to 3" is fixed. It lives in round II–III only; if Cory wants it to scale, "1 + round" (3 / 4).
- **Potluck** (keep, live) — "Each of your members brings you 1 of its color. +1 yellow."
  - Round II, about 4 members → **5**; round III, 6–7 → **7–8**. It counts members, not table colors, and it's the reason a dyer buys bodies. Neutral guests bring nothing (live code).

### Round I (×3, equal-ish gain, different side effect, still worth it in round III)
- **Harvest** (reworded) — "+1 yellow × round. +1 yellow." → **2 / 3 / 4**, all yellow. It's the plain anchor (Gather's and Build Up's shape) and the fuel for the yellow contest.
- **Top Up** (Cory's call: scales by round) — "+1 × round of the color you hold the fewest of. +1 yellow." → **2 / 3 / 4**. The side effect is access: it fills the gap that blocks a member's colored part. On a tie you pick one, so it never pays by color count.
- **Trade Seeds** (live, swaps scaled) — "Swap up to 3 × round of your resources for other colors, one for one. +2 yellow (the +1 included)."
  - **2** raw, plus 3 / 6 / 9 swaps. It's the weak twin in raw gain and the late sniper: 9 swaps just before the round III contests.
  - It keeps the one-at-a-time picker Cory praised (#103).

### How it touches other colors (generic)
- Any color's members open up to anyone who dyes into that color.
- Any color's contest can be entered late (Pour, Trade Seeds) or dented (Fade).
- A hoarder of one color faces a new threat that takes nothing from them.
- Pour makes a theft magnet.
- A Red player re-firing a Dyer's "When recruited" gets a free repaint.

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** round I Harvest / Top Up, buy Gardener or Dyer. Round II Fade the color leader you're chasing, or Potluck. Round III Pour into whichever contest is closest, with Chameleon or Tiebreaker turning the pile into two prizes. It reads cleanly.
- **Rival:** Fade stings without taking anything, so it feels milder than theft. A big single-color hoard is now risky from two directions.
- **Newcomer:** "turn N into that color" is the easiest effect to show on a real table (swap cubes).
- **2 players:** with only two colors, recolor matters less (fewer contests to aim at), but every gain still pays in full, because nothing counts colors. **4 players:** the most value, because there are more contests and more colored parts to reach.
- **Cory (rubric v3):** expect "changed".
  - He'll likely like Top Up scaling, Trade Seeds kept, and Pour's burst.
  - Risk: Fade read as "Crime Wave lite" (rule 7).
  - Risk: Pour near Gold's doubling lean. It's a different verb (one color, +50%, not ×2), but the foreman should pick one of the two for the board.
  - Rainmaker's rewording may draw "why change it". The reason is color scaling, so ask him.
- **Weakest card:** Colorfast. It guards against only Fade and Bleacher, which is narrow. I kept it because the deck shape wants a cheap defense worth 2, and a dyed Fade is the one sting this color adds.

## Concept B — Spotlight: Yellow sets what the contests pay

**Identity in one mechanic:** Yellow puts a marker (the sun) on a color's contest for this round, and that contest pays double at round end. Markers clear at round end, so nothing carries. Nobody else touches the prizes themselves, so the core tension (hold to win a contest, or spend on members) gets sharper exactly where Yellow says.

**Contest:** most yellow held. Spotlighting yellow itself is allowed.

### Round III
- **Headliner** — "Choose a color: it pays double this round, and your yellow counts toward it. +1 yellow."
  - With 6–8 yellow plus a few of that color, you take a 12-follower contest. That's about **10–12** if you hold the lead.
  - Rivals answer by holding instead of buying, and every unit they hold forfeits a follower at game end. It's a real bidding war.

### Round II
- **Match** — "Choose a color: gain enough to tie its leader, up to 2 × round. +1 yellow."
  - Up to **5 / 7**. Catch-up keyed to visible rival state: a rival's lead becomes a shared prize.
- **Potluck** (keep) — as in A, **5 / 7–8**.

### Round I
- **Harvest** — "+1 yellow × round. +1 yellow." → **2 / 3 / 4**.
- **Spotlight** — "Choose a color: its contest pays double this round. +1 yellow."
  - 1 raw, plus about +2 / +4 / +6 to whoever wins that contest. It scales by round on its own, the way Echo does.
- **Top Up** — as in A, **2 / 3 / 4**.

### How it touches other colors
- Every color's contest can get louder, which pulls its leader into holding rather than buying members: a slower shop, a richer theft target.
- Match shares out any color's lead.
- Headliner sends the yellow pile into another color's fight.

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** spotlights the colors they lead, so it leans win-more (the leader gains), with Match as the only brake.
- **Rival:** they suddenly face a contest worth twice as much, which is interesting, but they did nothing to cause it.
- **Newcomer:** "this rim pays double" is clear, but *why* to spotlight which color takes table reading.
- **2 players:** spotlight swings are huge (one rival, 50/50 contests). **4 players:** fine.
- **Cory (rubric v3):**
  - It fails the walk-away test on Spotlight and Barker: they pay at round end, not this turn. That's Dig's trap.
  - Doubling prizes changes the scoring shape (scoring-shape tag, 2✅ 2❌).
  - Headliner's "your yellow counts toward it" is the "which pile counts where" display problem.
  - Expect "interesting, but no" or a trim to one card.
- **Weakest card:** Stagehands, a filler body; the deck runs thin once the verb is "prizes".

---

## Recommendation
Concept A, Dye. It's one verb in every slot, readable on a real table, and it pays the taker this turn in every round. It never counts table colors, gives Yellow a reason to want members (Potluck, Melting Pot, Dyer), and adds a mild new kind of interaction (color, not value).
The two points the critics should hit first are Fade against Crime Wave and Pour against Gold's doubling lean.
From B, keep Tiebreaker and Chameleon (both are in A). Hold Spotlight as a possible round I swap if Cory wants Yellow to touch contests harder.

## Appendix: seeds (12), one line each, and why each was cut or kept
1. **Dye** (recolor yours and a rival's). What the table would miss without Yellow. Developed as A.
2. **Spotlight** (Yellow raises a contest's prize). Holding vs spending. Developed as B.
3. **Wild money** ("yellow can pay any member's colored part" printed on the rim; shop/members). Cut: it overlaps Freelancer (which Cory once said belongs to Yellow) and Gold's anchor 2, and the actions fall back to flat yellow gains.
4. **Filling spaces** (empty yellow spaces collect the colors the table plays; tempo/space). Cut: it scales with player count, crowds the wheel, and sits in Purple's "time as greed".
5. **Plant** (store resources on a space, back with interest at round end; holding). Cut: dead in round III, because they come back after the last buy. Reads as paying to use a space.
6. **Mirror** (gain off rivals' biggest piles). Cut as an identity: it's Rising Tide's shape and Lottery Winner's feel. Its tame form survives as Match in B.
7. **Bleach a color, table-wide** (everyone's X turns yellow; interaction). Cut: a big denial with little for the taker, and feel-bad for the leader.
8. **Two-color members** (yellow members count as a second color too; members). Cut: it only matters where member colors are counted (Mob, Chronicler), and is invisible elsewhere.
9. **Repaint a shop member's colored part to yellow** (shop). Cut: claim-shaped. Cory: "promote and ransom are just straight up way better".
10. **Your members bring their colors** (Potluck ×2 as the burst). The strategist's lean, left to the anchor seat. Potluck is kept as A's round II.
11. **Raise every color to your largest** (old Cornucopia). Cut: it scales with table colors and was measured far too strong.
12. **Trade with a rival** (your yellow for their color). Cut: zero gain for the taker, and it reads like Barter, which Cory turned down ("no, I don't like that").
