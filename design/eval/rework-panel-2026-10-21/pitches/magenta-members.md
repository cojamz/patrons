# Magenta members: a light rework (panel pitch, 2026-10-21)

**All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.** Live card texts are quoted from src/engine/v4/content/thieves.js. Costs are magenta+any, "fN" is printed followers (and bodies). No sims, no games.

Scope: members only. Magenta's six actions and its contest (most magenta held) stay as they are.

## 0. The one finding that drives every price here

**Magenta is the scarcest color in the game, and the live deck prices as if it weren't.**
- Magenta only comes from the +1 on a magenta space (and from stealing someone's magenta). Stolen loot keeps its colors.
- Magenta spaces per round: 3, then 5, then 6. At 4 players about 12 magenta reach the whole table in a game (guess: round I fills exactly, rounds II and III leave a space or so empty). A specialist taking two magenta spaces a round ends with about **6–8 magenta in the whole game**.
- The contest is won on small numbers: 1–3 magenta usually takes a 2/4/6 prize. So every magenta spent on a member is very likely the contest.
- The live deck's magenta parts add up to **35**: six of its ten cards ask 4 magenta, and Extortionist asks 8. A specialist can afford one 4-magenta card a game, and only by giving up a contest. Nobody else can buy any of them, including Bodyguard, the card the *victims* need.
- The flip side: thieves are rich in *other* colors. Help Yourself, Crime Wave and Shake Down fill the any-part easily.

So the rule for this deck: **magenta parts of 1–2 on almost everything, 3 on the color's identity cards, and the rest paid in any color: thieves pay with stolen money.** That makes "spend 1 magenta on this member, or keep it for the contest" a real decision on every buy, which is the hold-vs-spend tension Cory named, instead of a wall. Live total 35 becomes 19 (deck A) and 21 (deck B).

Two other facts from the brief that shape the deck:
- A committed player owns about 3–5 of their color's 10 at 4 players; at 2 players they see all of it. Every card below stands alone; pairs are a bonus.
- On Recruit fires again for whoever takes a member (Shake Down, Poach, Double Agent, Red's moves). Arrival cards in Magenta are theft magnets on purpose.

**Against the shape** (member-shape.md section 4): cheap body (Crew); defense worth 2 (Bodyguard); income on the verb (Cutpurse, Sticky Fingers, Shoplifter); the color's action on arrival (Double Agent, Grifter); plays off rivals (Robin Hood, Desperado); scorer and top card (Kingpin). Missing: a universal rule-bender. Bodyguard half-fills it, and Magenta's guest is gone (Squatter is colorless now).

**Self-critique, deck A**
- Specialist: round I, Help Yourself or Lie Low gives about 2 magenta and 4 loot; buy Cutpurse or Desperado with loot, hold a magenta for the 2-point contest. Round II, Crime Wave then Sticky Fingers or Robin Hood paid in stolen colors. Round III, Poach, then Kingpin if three magenta can be spared. Every buy asks "1 magenta or the contest?", the tension Cory wants.
- Rival: Bodyguard is now buyable after one magenta visit, so the defense is a real choice rather than a card nobody can pay for.
- Newcomer: every card says one thing. Desperado's "ties count" is the only clause to read twice.
- 2 players: Robin Hood and Desperado read one rival, so they flip on and off as the lead changes, which is clear. Shoplifter always hits the same rival. 4 players: steals spread, Bodyguard matters more, and Robin Hood aims the table's thief at the leader.
- Cory (rubric v3): mostly cards he knows, and every reprice goes the way his notes go (quiet cards cheaper, the defense card easier to reach). Shoplifter is his own idea. Kingpin follows his own "based more on members". Risks: Kingpin may read as "Mob again"; Shoplifter sits close to Red's Recruiter ("When you recruit, +1 red") in shape, with theft as the difference.
- Weakest card: **Sticky Fingers**, a bigger number with little decision. Kept because it's live, it's the Impressionist shape he called "great", and its combo with Desperado is a thing he flagged (#23) rather than cut.
- Combo watch: Desperado + Sticky Fingers + Robin Hood on a Crime Wave against the leader can take a whole stack and give as many followers. That needs three cards and last place, about a 5% hand (guess); flagged.

Prices: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 6, 6, 10. Magenta parts total 21. Printed followers 12.

**How Black Market plays.** You pay the full price, so you gain no followers on the deal: what you get is the pick (the best members in the game are the ones rivals chose), the On Recruit again (a rival's Panel or Treasurer fires for you), and the denial (their Mob's count, their Host's income, their combo). They get paid in full and use a buy to turn it back into followers. It's Shake Down turned around: there the victim chooses and the thief gets paid; here the thief chooses and the victim gets paid. Bodyguard is the only block.

**Self-critique, deck B**
- Specialist: Fence early means magenta for the contest every round plus magenta for parts. From round II on, Black Market makes every buy a read of four cults. Round III with Poach plus Black Market is the most aggressive cult in the game.
- Rival: your best member is never fully safe after round II, but you're paid its cost, so the loss is a member's ability or a combo, not followers. Bodyguard (now 1 magenta) is the answer, and it's worth 2.
- Newcomer: Black Market needs the screen to let you click a rival's member and buy it. That's new UI and the hardest card here to show.
- 2 players: Black Market is pure denial against the only rival, so it's sharper there. 4 players: more choice, and the harm is spread.
- Cory (rubric v3): Black Market opens a new way to win and bends one base rule on your own turn (the shape of "you can always buy twice", which he liked). Risk: "not CRAZY bad, like straight up losing a member (unless it's a round-III action)". It's a paid, forced sale, arriving late round II at the earliest. He may call it too harsh, or ask for "once per round". Fence risks "a +1 drip"; the defense is that in Magenta a +1 magenta is a contest point.
- Weakest card: **Fence**, a template (the color's +1 on its verb). Kept because it fixes the scarcity problem from inside the deck instead of by changing the locked actions.

## 4. Recommendation

**Deck A.** It's the light rework Cory asked for: eight cards he knows, reprices that follow his own price notes and magenta's scarcity, Robin Hood and Kingpin moved the way his notes point, and his own "steal when you buy a member" in Extortionist's slot.
Offer deck B's two new cards as single swaps for him to try: **Fence for Sticky Fingers** if magenta feels starved in play, and **Black Market as the top card** if he wants a 10 that opens a new way to win.
The reprices are what matter most either way: without them, most of the deck can't be bought in a real game.
