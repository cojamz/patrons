# Silver, outsider seat (rework panel, 2026-10-21)

**All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.**

Seat: an experienced worker-placement designer seeing Silver fresh. Scope: Silver actions and members. Sources read: brief, strategy-read, member-shape, pricing, deck-visibility, cory-judge (rubric v3), design/strategy.md, pitch-ledger.md, decisions.md (Oct 20 on), live silver.js, spheres/silver.md, and the other pitches already in this folder (for overlaps).

## What the table lacks, after Red, Green, Magenta and Blue

- **Contests are only checked once, at round end.** Five colors score on held resources, so "hold or spend" is the core tension. But a lead only matters at the moment the round ends. Nothing makes a lead matter in the middle of a round. In worker-placement games the best tension is "will it still be there on my turn?" Today that applies to spaces and shop cards, never to contest leads.
- **Nothing gives a held pile a way out.** Once you're holding red to win the red contest, you can't spend red on red members without losing the prize. The colored part of a member's cost taxes its own contest (pricing.md section 4). Every holder stays stuck until round end.
- **Silver's steroids are structural.** Toast 3/6/9 and Hold Court's buy-free followers add resources and followers to the table. A counterweight that only trims numbers leaves Silver as "more stuff". The real counterweight is a Silver that adds nothing new to the table and moves value around in time or toward the table.
- **Rising Tide, the card Cory likes, gets weaker under the +1 economy.** Its harvest is "half the silver of the rival with the most". Under +1 per action, rivals hold 1–3 silver in round II. After its +2 gift that's 3–5, so you gain 2–3 (+1). Meanwhile you handed out 6 at a 4-player table. It was strong in the flood economy, when everyone held 8+ silver. To keep it, it needs a deck that keeps feeding rivals' silver, or a bigger read.

Two concepts follow, as far apart as I could get. A changes **when contests score**. It isn't about giving, and it isn't the strategist's lean. B is the giving color rebuilt so it **can't run away**: its gains match the best-off rival. B is near the strategist's anchor 2 ("inflate, then harvest") and not the lean ("per silver held across the table").

## Concept A: Silver calls the contests early

**Identity in one mechanic:** Silver scores a contest you lead right now, and that contest is then over for the round. Red moves members, Green copies, Magenta steals, Blue works the shop and the number of buys. None of them touch *when* a prize is paid. Gold (in its pitches) grows a stack, and White (in its outsider pitch) ties leaders. Calling is about timing, not size or splits.

**The one new rule:** a contest that has been scored early is marked on its rim with the scorer's face ("won by Ann"), doesn't score at round end, and clears when the next round starts. "You lead" means you hold the most, alone, and at least 1. On a real table you'd flip the rim's prize token to the winner.

**Why it's a counterweight:** it adds no resources and no followers to the game. The rounds' prizes (2/4/6) already exist. Silver pays them sooner, at a discount in round I, and takes the round's suspense with them. Silver goes from "more stuff" to "the clock on the contests".

**Why it sharpens hold vs spend rather than ending it:** the leader gets a third option, "take a sure half now", next to "hold for the full prize" and "spend and give it up". Calling also lifts the colored-part tax for that color for the rest of the round: call the red contest, then spend your red on red members. That interplay runs through every color's deck without naming one.

### Round III (the burst)
- **Last Call:** "Score every contest you lead now; they're over this round. +1 silver."
  - Taker leads 1 contest: 6 + 1 = **7 now**, and the pile it was holding is free to spend on the remaining buys. Holding in round III normally costs the whole pile (it dies at game end), so freeing it is worth about the smaller of the pile and the prize: about +4. Real total **about 11**.
  - Leads 2: **13 now** plus two freed piles, **about 18–20**. That's above band. It's the main risk (see critique). The trim, if needed: "up to two contests" doesn't help, so "score one contest you lead at double" is the fallback (12 + 1, no stacking).
  - Leads 0: dead for that player. Taking a space you can't use is on the player.

### Round II ×2 (change what you own or how much you buy)
- **Crown:** "Score a contest you lead now; it's over this round. +1 silver."
  - Round II: **4 followers + 1 silver = 5 now**, plus that color's pile is free to buy its members this round. Promote is about 3.5 and Shake Down about 5, so it sits with them.
  - Round III (the ring stays open): 6 + 1 + a freed pile, about 9. A strong second pick behind the anchors, like Promote late.
- **Upstage:** "Choose a color: gain enough of it to lead it alone, up to 4. +1 silver."
  - Round II gap is usually 2–4, so **3–5 now**. It changes how much you buy, and it takes a lead off a rival without stealing.
  - It sets up a Crown or a Last Call on a later turn. Between your turns every rival can see it and can retake the lead. "Will my lead still be there?" is the worker-placement tension, moved onto the rims.

### Round I ×3 (equal gain, different side effects, all scale by round)
- **Toast:** "Score half of a contest you lead now; it's over this round. +1 silver." (Half of 2/4/6 is 1/2/3.)
  - **2 / 3 / 4** by round. Side effect: it ends one race early and frees that color for you.
- **Good Deed:** "+1 silver × round, and a rival you choose gains 1 of a color you choose. +1 silver."
  - **2 / 3 / 4** for you, 1 to a rival. Side effect: an aimed gift that tips a race. Make a rival tie a leader (halving that leader's prize), or put a rival ahead of someone who was about to Crown. Silver's gift heritage stays, now as a contest weapon.
- **Rally:** "+1 × round of a color a rival holds more of than you. +1 silver."
  - **2 / 3 / 4**. Side effect: it pushes you into a race you're losing (a setup for a later call, or a block on someone else's).
- Every round I space pays 2 in round I, like Gather, and 4 in round III. Gather is the round I scaling floor Cory accepted.

**Contest:** most silver held, the default. Silver comes from the +1s and Good Deed, so it's a small-number fight, like Magenta's. The Silver specialist usually calls the silver contest first.

**How it touches other colors through the board:**
- Every contest can be called, so any color's leader has to watch the rims all round, not just at round end.
- Theft and lead-gaining moves become setups for a call. A call also beats a thief to the punch.
- Calling frees a color's pile, so it feeds buying that color's members.
- Copy effects that copy a call score the copier's own lead, which is fine and generic.
- Nothing names a color, nothing counts how many colors are at the table, and nothing triggers on a rival's turn.

### Self-critique (A)
- **Specialist's round:** in round I, take Toast or Rally and buy Retinue or Ringleader. In round II, Upstage a contest, then Crown it next turn if it holds. Buy Herald. In round III, take Last Call if leading two, else Crown. It plays nothing like any reworked color.
- **Rival:** every lead you hold is a target for an early call only if Silver gets ahead of you in it, so you defend your lead by staying ahead, not by holding forever. Hecklers is the out. The sting is losing a race early, which is mild.
- **Newcomer:** "score it now, it's over" is easy to grasp. Picking *which* contest to call takes some reading of the rims, about Echo's level.
- **2 players:** two contests. Calling one is half the table's prizes, and the reply is direct (retake the lead before the next call). That's strong but clean. **4 players:** four contests, leads spread thin (about 1.3 each), more targets, fewer double leads.
- **Cory (rubric v3, honestly):**
  - It passes the hard nos, the walk-away test (5 in round II), scaling (the prizes grow 2/4/6, which is Echo's bar), and "a contrarian cult" (Runner-up).
  - Risks: "score half" may read as fiddly. He may ask "where did the gifts go?" (Good Deed and Retinue only). Last Call with two leads is above band. Upstage overlaps White's "tie the leader" in spirit.
  - My guess: ↔, he keeps the shape and trims Last Call.
- **Weakest card:** Patron Saint, kept only for the scorer slot. Swap it for Runner-up if he wants one scorer.

## Concept B: Silver lifts the table, then matches the top

**Identity in one mechanic:** every Silver gain matches, or half-matches, what the best-off rival has, after you've handed the table something. Red, Green, Magenta and Blue all act on rivals' stuff. Silver is the one color that adds to rivals' piles first.

**Why it's a counterweight:** the harvest reads the best-off rival. If you're already the richest in silver, your spaces pay you almost nothing beyond the +1. Silver can keep up with the table but can't run away from it. The gifts also arm rivals in your own silver contest.

### Round III
- **High Tide:** "Everyone else +2 silver. Then gain silver equal to the rival with the most silver. +1 silver."
  - That rival holds about 4–8 by then, so 6–10 after the gift: **7–11 for you**, 2 to each rival. In band. It also usually ties or takes the silver lead for the round's 6.

### Round II ×2
- **Rising Tide** (kept, + the inherent +1): "Everyone else +2 silver. Then gain half the silver of the rival with the most (rounded up). +1 silver."
  - **3–5**. It's thin under the +1 economy unless the deck keeps feeding rivals' silver (Toast, Retinue, Debutante do).
- **Keep Up:** "Choose a color: gain enough of it to tie the rival with the most. +1 silver."
  - Round II **3–6**. It halves a rival's prize if the tie holds. **Overlaps White outsider B's Keep Pace almost word for word.** If White goes that way, B loses this slot.

### Round I ×3
- **Toast:** "+1 silver × round. Everyone else +1 silver. +1 silver."
  - **2 / 3 / 4** to you, 1 to each rival. Toast's steroid (3/6/9) is gone.
- **Good Deed:** "+1 silver × round, and a rival you choose gains 1 × round of a color you choose. +1 silver."
  - **2 / 3 / 4**, plus an aimed gift of 1/2/3.
- **Climb:** "Choose a color: gain half the gap to the rival with the most of it (rounded up). +1 silver."
  - **2–3** in round I, **3–5** in round III.

**Contest:** most silver held. The gifts feed your rivals' count.

**How it touches other colors:**
- Rivals get richer and choose what to do with the gift (a contest or a member).
- The best-off rival becomes the measuring stick, so hoarders feed Silver without being robbed.
- Matching enters any color's race without theft.

### Self-critique (B)
- **Specialist:** Toast and Good Deed early, Rising Tide or Keep Up in round II, High Tide in round III. Its numbers move with rivals' piles, so it plays differently every game. That's good for variety and hard for a newcomer to plan.
- **Rival:** gets gifts every round (pleasant), and the richest one gets matched (fair). Nobody loses anything. Interaction is soft.
- **2 players:** a duel. The one rival is the one you match, and gifts cost only 1×. **4 players:** gifts cost three times as much, and the harvest still reads one rival, so Silver is weaker at big tables. That's the reverse of what "giving" usually does.
- **Cory:** he likes Rising Tide and gifts, which helps. Risks: four of six spaces are half/full matches ("slight variations", "not thinking broadly enough"). Keep Up duplicates White's pitch. Matching a rival's pile is the shape he disliked on members.
- **Weakest card:** Revelers, a bare body count with no Silver idea. Kept because he named it.

---

## Recommendation
A. It's the only one of the two that answers "steroids" structurally (it adds no resources), gives the table something nothing else does (leads matter mid-round), and turns the colored-part tax into play.
B is the safe fallback if Cory wants Silver to stay the giver. It reuses his cards, but it overlaps White's tie pitch and thins out at four players.
If A goes forward, Rising Tide could take Upstage's round II slot, but its harvest is small under the +1 economy. That's his call.

## Appendix: seeds (12), and why each was cut or kept
1. **Score a contest early, then it's over** (holding vs spending, contests): kept, A.
2. **Raise the stakes: a contest pays double this round** (holding): cut. A setup-only space with nothing gained the turn you take it, which fails the walk-away test (Dig, "so weak").
3. **Bounties: put silver on wheel spaces; whoever places there takes it** (space): cut. No real round III burst, it adds resources (steroids), and it's close to Blue's unanswered "sweeten the row".
4. **Silver spaces are never full: anyone may join a taken one, and the host gains** (space, interaction): cut. It's Squatter's rule as a whole color ("maybe TOO strong"), it breaks "round I fills exactly" (core redesign), and it pays on rivals' turns.
5. **A collection plate: Silver spaces feed a public pot, the round III Silver space takes it** (holding): cut. It rewards whoever picks first (the trailer), not the color. It's near the strategist's lean and Gold outsider's pot.
6. **Give one of your members to a rival, gain followers** (members): cut. Close to Release ("needs to go entirely"), and kingmaking.
7. **Dowry: recruit free, a rival you pick gains its cost in silver** (shop): cut. Free recruiting is Red's verb.
8. **Silver pays any member's colored part** (shop, payment): cut. Freelancer already does it, and it's Gold anchor 2.
9. **Silver is second place: runner-up prizes** (contests, contrarian): kept as a member (Runner-up) in A. As a whole color it sits too close to A.
10. **Lift the table, then match the top** (giving, rivals): kept, B.
11. **Spillover followers per rival** (giving): cut. It shrinks to nothing at 2 players (the strategist: "don't").
12. **What if Silver didn't exist?** The table would miss a reason to watch leads mid-round and a positive-sum color. That fed A and B.
