# Gold, anchor seat (rework panel, 2026-10-21)

**All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.** Names are placeholders. Live text is quoted from live-cards.txt.

Seat: design the way Cory does. Pick round III first, work backward to the core, keep what he has kept and liked unless there's a reason. Gold is his own design ("Gold is the money for buying members", "Double your gold"), it sits on every Classic table, and at 2 players it is half the board.

## What I started from

- Under the Oct 20 economy every resource buys followers 1-for-1, and every contest is on held resources. So Gold's old identity (hold the most, gold is money for follower buys) is now the base game. What Gold still owns that no other color has: **doubling** (Double Down) and **being the money for members** (Penny Pincher, Treasurer). Both are Cory's.
- What's broken live: big flat gains (Kickback "+3 gold…", Golden Years "+2 / +4 / +6"), a cost cut (Intro Offer "next member costs 4 less", his #72 "trash or not in Gold"), Magenta's verb (Collect Debts "Take up to 3 gold from rivals"), and Double Down's flat spot: "Double your gold" is worth a lot to the gold leader and almost nothing to anyone else. At 4 players the leader picks last in round III. If three rivals take Poach, Recruit a Member and Spree, Double Down is the anchor left over, and it goes to the gold leader. That's win-more by default.
- The members he liked all play the color's two halves. Treasurer ("really good") turns members into gold. Penny Pincher ("good, could get a little crazy") turns gold into members. Underdog ("interactive") is catch-up on gold. Banker was "too dear" at 8. Lottery Winner and Hustler were disliked.

## Round III first

Candidates I weighed (walk-away for the taker, against the 8–12 band):

| Round III candidate | Specialist | Anyone else | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Double your gold. +1 gold." (his, as is) | 7–12 | 1–5 | Right size, but has the flat spot above |
| "Double one of your colors. +1 gold." | 7–12 | 6–10, plus it can swing that color's contest | Same verb and almost the same text, and every seat wants it |
| "This turn, gold counts double on a member" | about 6 | about 3 | Below the band, and Recruit a Member is free up to 10 |
| "Gain gold per member" (Tithe as the burst) | 6–9 | 6–9 | That's Potluck's shape, which the Yellow seat is likely to claim |
| "One follower pack of any size this turn" | low | low | You already have 5 buys in round III, so it saves about 1 buy. It's also Blue's lever |

Doubling is the anchor. The only real question is "your gold" or "one of your colors". That one question splits the color into the two concepts below. Concept A works backward from doubling gold: gold is the member money, and the round III doubling is the payoff for holding it. Concept B works backward from doubling any pile: Gold is the color that multiplies whatever you hold, and becomes the contest swinger.

They sit on opposite sides of the core tension. A is mostly the spending side (gold into members), with the hoard as its other half. B is the holding side (piles that grow).

## Concept A: "Gold is the money for buying members" (recommended)

**Identity in one mechanic:** gold goes further than any other resource when you spend it on members, and it doubles when you hold it. Every gold coin poses the core tension by itself: spend it on a member now at double, or keep it for the contest and Double Down.
- Red gets members free and moves them. Gold pays for them.
- Blue changes the row and the number of buys. Gold changes how far one buy's money reaches.
- No taking from rivals, no copying.

### Actions

| Ring | Name | Text | Taker walks away with |
|---|---|---|---|
| III | Double Down (kept) | Double your gold. +1 gold. | 7–12 for a player holding 6–11 gold |
| II | Tithe (kept) | +1 gold per member you have. +1 gold. | 4–6 (3–5 members in round II, counting multi-body members) |
| II | Splurge (new) | This turn, gold you spend on a member counts double. +1 gold. | A member worth up to twice your gold. Holding 5 gold, a 10-cost gold member for 5: about +5, +1 |
| I | Golden Years (rescaled) | +1 gold × round. +1 gold. | 2 / 3 / 4 |
| I | Kickback (kept) | +2 gold, and +1 gold to a rival you choose. +1 gold. | 3 every round (and a rival gets 1) |
| I | Half Again (new) | Gain half your gold, rounded up. +1 gold. | 1–3 in round I (it grows as the round goes on), 4 in round II, about 6 in round III |

**Per round:**
- Round I spaces total 2–3 each, with different side effects:
  - Golden Years is plain and scales.
  - Kickback is the best first pick in round I, the weakest in round III, and it hands a rival a gold. That gift can break or make a tie in the gold contest.
  - Half Again pays whoever already holds gold, so it's a later pick in round I and a strong one in round III.
  - All three stay worth taking in round III (4, 3, about 6).
- Round II is the bar Cory set: both spaces change how much you buy or what you own.
  - Tithe is the hold side: gold for the contest and for Double Down.
  - Splurge is the spend side. It lands a member you couldn't afford, at about Promote and Shake Down's level, but it costs your contest stake.
- Round III: Double Down at 7–12 for whoever held gold.

**Contest:** most gold held (unchanged). Splurge and the members pull gold out of the pile, while Tithe, Half Again and Double Down push it back in. The contest is where those meet.

**How it touches the other colors, through the board:**
- On a Splurge turn, gold pays every member's "any" part at double. So a gold player can reach other colors' capstones, but still has to bring their colored part.
- Tithe counts bodies, so worth-3 and worth-5 members from any deck pay gold.
- Treasurer is an On Recruit card, so any color that moves members fires it again for whoever moves it in.
- A doubled stack is the obvious target for half-a-stack theft. Kickback's gift lets you pick who comes up in a contest.

**Round III widening (a pick for Cory, not built into A):** "Double one of your colors" instead of "Double your gold". The specialist still doubles gold. The trailer, picking first, doubles their best stack. It's the same card with a wider door. My default: widen.

Deck checks:
- Ladder: 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 8. Bodies 14 across 10 cards.
- No card counts "gold members", so nothing needs a specific card to show up.
- Each card works alone. The pairs that sing (Midas with Speculator, Splurge with Socialite, Tellers with Tithe) are bonuses.
- Deck visibility: at 2 players all 10 show. At 4 players about 8 show, and a committed player owns 3–5.

Cut from live, and why:
- Intro Offer: a cost cut.
- Collect Debts: Magenta's verb. Flag: on Classic there is then no check on piles at all.
- Tax Collector: under this economy rivals buy packs about 15 times a game, so it's a big drip on their turns.
- Merchant: a rival-turn drip, and it depends on gold members showing up.
- Alchemist: under +1 per action it fires on nearly every non-gold placement, so it's a quiet drip of 8 or more. Midas covers the idea with one decision.
- Penny Pincher's rule moves to the board as Splurge. If Cory would rather keep the standing card, it stays at about 4+3. Splurge is then redundant for its owner, and round II needs another space (see the critique).

### Self-critique (A)
- **Specialist:** Kickback and Half Again in round I, buying Prospector or Miser. In round II, Tithe, then Splurge into a capstone for half its gold. In round III, Double Down, then cash in over 2–3 buys. **Rival:** takes Kickback first in round I, and blocks Splurge in round II when the gold player sits on 6+. **Newcomer:** "gold is money, and it doubles" reads at once.
- **At 2 players** (Gold and Red), gold is half the board, so both players hold gold. Double Down is wanted by both, and Splurge plus free recruits makes a member-heavy game. Kickback's gift always goes to the one rival, so its R1 edge over Golden Years shrinks to about +1. **At 4 players**, unless it's widened, Double Down is narrow. Underdog and the member-money pull help, but don't fix it.
- **Risk 1: Splurge reads as a cost cut.** Cory dropped cost reductions "for now". Penny Pincher survived that and he liked it, so I think a this-turn rule is fine, but it's his call. Fallback for the slot: "Put any of your gold on this space. It comes back doubled after this round's contests. +1 gold." That's his kept Invest, made visible. But it's a second doubling in the color.
- **Risk 2: hoarder runaway.** Midas, then Double Down, then Speculator, compounds. If it runs away, the lever is "Double your gold (at most +10)", the same ceiling as members. Not proposed by default.
- **Rubric v3:** it's his color and his cards, the round II pair are opposite in kind, it scales, and there are no hard nos. Expect "yes, with his numbers" and maybe "this is a light pass". Round III widening is the open question.
- **Weakest card:** Socialite. A threshold trigger is close to a "+1 when…". I kept it because it's the only card that pays the spend-down cult in followers, and it fires on your own turn.

## Concept B: "Gold multiplies whatever you hold"

**Identity in one mechanic:** double, half, match, on any pile. That's Cory's own Gold principle from the start ("open or relative: double, half, match, per X"). Gold doesn't care which color you hold. It grows it, and that makes Gold the color that swings contests. Distinct from theft: Gold grows your own piles and touches nobody's.

### Actions

| Ring | Name | Text | Taker walks away with |
|---|---|---|---|
| III | Double Down (widened) | Double one of your colors. +1 gold. | 7–11, plus that color's contest (worth 6) often flips |
| II | Half Again | Gain half of one of your colors, rounded up. +1 gold. | 4–5 (a stack of 5–7) |
| II | Gold Standard | Raise one of your colors to match your gold. +1 gold. | 4–6, and it can snipe a contest or unlock a colored part |
| I | Golden Years | +1 gold × round. +1 gold. | 2 / 3 / 4 |
| I | Kickback | +2 gold, and +1 gold to a rival you choose. +1 gold. | 3 |
| I | Nest Egg | +1 gold per 3 resources you hold. +1 gold. | 1–3 in round I (a later pick), 5–6 in round III |

- Contest: most gold held.
- Round II changes how much you buy, and through colored parts what you can buy. Gold Standard makes gold the yardstick every other color is raised to.
- Round III is wanted by every seat, which fixes the flat spot outright.

### Deck (10)
- Hoarders 1+1, worth 2: "Worth 2 followers." The cheap body.
- Notary 2+4, worth 2: "You win contest ties." It fits any build. Ties are common in small held contests, so this is about +3 to +6 a game. Universal, so it's priced high and paid mostly in any.
- Interest 2+2: "At round start, +1 gold per 3 gold you hold." His kept Interest, moved to round start. Pays about 2 at the start of round II and about 4 at the start of round III.
- Banker 4+2: "Now and at round start, +3 gold." Kept, repriced.
- Goldsmith 3+2, worth 2: "When recruited: raise one of your colors to match your gold." Gold Standard on arrival, plus bodies.
- Underdog 2+4: kept, as in A.
- Treasurer 4+0: kept, as in A.
- Midas 3+2: kept, as in A. It feeds the one stack you'll double.
- Tycoon 3+2, worth 2: kept, as in A.
- Speculator 5+3: "Once per game, on your turn: double one of your colors." The capstone.

Six of the ten are Cory's keeps and would fit either concept. The difference is on the board and in Notary, Interest and Goldsmith.

### Self-critique (B)
- **Specialist:** Golden Years and Nest Egg in round I. Gold Standard on the color they're one behind in, in round II. Double the fattest stack in round III. **Rivals** see their contest leads flip, so they read the gold player's piles. **Newcomer:** "double", "half" and "match" are each one word, but which pile counts where takes a glance at every seat.
- **At 2 players** only two colors exist, so Gold Standard plus Double Down can take both contests: very strong. At 4 players it's a strong contest reader.
- **Against the economy:** it pushes holding, and every contest already rewards holding. So buying followers only gets stronger at the table that matters most, and members become secondary. That goes against "members are where followers come from".
- **Overlap:** Gold Standard and "double a color" sit next to the likely Yellow directions (match, pour). Gold Standard is close to Rainmaker ("raise each of your colors to match your yellow").
- **Rubric v3:** he'd likely say yes to the round III, but question the whole color as "Yellow-ish" and "steroids" (his word for Silver).
- **Weakest card:** Nest Egg. It's 1 for the first picker in round I. Kept because it's the only round I space that pays for holding anything.

## Recommendation

**A**, with round III widened to "Double one of your colors" as Cory's first pick.
- A keeps his core ("the money for buying members"), his anchor, and every member he praised.
- It pulls gold toward members, which is the Oct 20 direction.
- Widening fixes the one flat spot without giving away the color.

B's best idea, doubling any pile, survives inside A as that widening. The rest of B fights the economy and crowds Yellow.

Decisions for Cory, if this reaches him (my default in brackets):
1. Double Down: "Double your gold" or "Double one of your colors" [widen].
2. Penny Pincher's rule as a round II space, Splurge [yes], or keep the standing card [no].
3. Collect Debts leaves Gold, so Classic has no theft [yes].

## Appendix: seeds (written before developing any)

1. **Holding:** multiply any pile (double, half, match). Became B.
2. **Shop and members:** gold is the member money (counts double on a member, Tithe, Treasurer). Became A.
3. **Space:** a gold bounty on an empty space; whoever takes it gets it and you get 1. Cut: it pays on rivals' turns, coins have to be tracked on the wheel, and it's a lure with no burst.
4. **Tempo:** spend gold to place again or go first. Cut: it spends to use a space, and timing belongs to Purple.
5. **Interaction:** auction a shop member, everyone bids gold. Cut: out-of-turn bidding, and too complex.
6. **Interaction:** buy a rival's member at its cost, paid to them (his Oct 10 bribe, made fair). Cut: Shake Down and Poach already do this, and it's member loss before round III.
7. **Interaction:** keep Collect Debts. Cut: Magenta's verb. Flagged: Classic then has no pile check.
8. **Interaction:** lend gold, repaid double. Cut: a debt (Lender was "a little too complicated").
9. **Holding vs spending:** Invest, a visible deposit that comes back doubled at round end. Cut as a main slot: it's a second doubling, and it needs a rule for timing against the contests. Kept as A's fallback.
10. **The buy:** one follower pack of any size. Cut: there are 5 buys in round III, so it's worth about 1 buy, and the number of buys is Blue's lever.
11. **If Gold didn't exist, what would the table miss?** At 2 players, half the board. Also: money everyone dips into, the hold-to-grow pole, the only round III that rewards not spending, and Underdog's catch-up. That supports keeping gold as everyone's money, which is what A does.
12. **Gold judges the contests** (counts toward any color, decides ties). Cut as the identity: hard to show which pile counts where. Survives as one member (Notary in B, Miser in A).
13. **Members pay you** (payroll as the identity). Cut as the identity: it's Potluck's shape (likely Yellow) and close to White's "members work twice". Kept as one round II space (Tithe).
14. **Catch-up money** (gold flows to whoever has less). Cut as the identity: that's the lean for White's trailer, and Lottery Winner's shape was disliked. Kept as Underdog.
15. **Big-ticket cult** (game end, +1 follower per member costing 6 or more). Cut: Socialite does the same job on your own turn, and a count of cost thresholds is one more thing to read.
