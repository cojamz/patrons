# Purple rework: the outsider's seat (2026-10-21, overnight panel)

All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.

Seat: an experienced worker-placement designer seeing Purple's timing fresh. Scope: Purple actions and members. The purple-leaning guests (Squatter, Freelancer, Extra Hand, Front-runner, Straggler) are out of scope. No sims, no games.

## 0. What "when you place" means on this board (the outsider's read)

Read from the rules (code): N players, N colors, 3/2/1 spaces per color, rings stay open. Round I has 3N spaces for 3N minions, so it **fills exactly**: the last minion of round I has one space and no choice. Rounds II and III each have **N spare spaces**, so the last minion still picks from N+1. Order is a snake (random, then fewest followers first), so the ends double up.

Five things follow. They shaped every seed below.

1. **Priority is worth what it denies, not what it adds.** Moving one of your own turns earlier only swaps the order of your own picks: you still place the same number of minions. A space that moves **one** turn (take your next turn now) is worth only its own gain. Moving **two** turns (Crunch Time) is the smallest move that gets you a contested pick a rival would otherwise have taken. So a timing color can't live on turn moves alone. That's also why the bots read Crunch Time low (13%): their numbers count what you add, and it mostly takes from others.
2. **A hold on a space ("only you can place there until your next turn") is the same trap.** You could have just taken it. A hold only pays when it covers two or more spaces, and then it's Crunch Time without the reordering.
3. **Any "next round" effect on a round I or II space has nothing to do in round III.** Those spaces stay open in round III, and Cory's rule is that round I stays worth taking there. So timing that carries across rounds belongs on members (round start), never on spaces.
4. **Your own clock is fair in the snake; the board's clock isn't.** "Per minion you've placed this round" reaches each count at about the same time for every seat. "Per minion on the board" (Bandwagon) favors whoever comes back to it late, which under fewest-first is the leader. That's the flaw Cory named in Bandwagon, and it's why his Catch Up fix counts only the placer.
5. **In round III the buy binds.** A big late pile of resources can't all be spent (10 per buy, few buys left). Late payoffs should come as followers or contest power, not as a pile that dies.

What the table lacks: no color makes **the moment** matter to everyone. Contests are 12 followers per color per game, and they all pay at one fixed instant, round end. Every holding-vs-spending decision at the table is aimed at that instant. A timing color that moves *that* instant makes every rival care about Purple's timing, without moving a single minion.

## Concept 1: The Count (Purple decides when contests pay)

**Identity in one mechanic:** Purple makes a contest pay out *now*, at this round's prize, to whoever leads it at that moment. It still pays again at round end as usual. Purple can't call its own contest, so the purple player wins calls by leading other colors.
Distinct from Red (moves members), Green (copies actions), Magenta (takes), Blue (the shop and the number of buys). It sits next to the strategist's "White judges the contests" idea: that decides *who* wins a contest; this decides *when* it pays. If White goes that way, one of the two must move.

Rule for calls (one line in the rulebook): "A contest that pays out now pays this round's prize (2/4/6) to its leader, half each on a tie, nothing to zero. It still pays at round end."

### Round III (the burst)
- **The Count:** "Every contest but purple's pays out now. +1 purple."
- Taker (4 players): three contests at 6 each. A purple player who leads one or two of them walks away with **7–13** (in the 8–12 band). Leading none, it's +1 and a gift to rivals, so nobody takes it blind.
- Why it's a burst and not a dominant first pick: the trailer picks first in round III and usually leads little, so they rarely want it at pick 1. The right moment is when you lead the most, often after rivals have started spending. Until someone takes it, every player at the table has a reason not to cash out a color they lead. That's the timing pressure.
- It's wanted by more than the specialist: any player leading two colors in round III wants it.

### Round II (×2)
- **Early Count:** "Choose a contest other than purple's: it pays out now. +1 purple."
  Walk-away: 4 followers + 1 if you lead the color you call (2 + 1 on a tie). About **5**, next to Shake Down (about 5) and Promote (about 3). It changes how much you get without a buy, and it hits rivals through the board: you call the color where a rival just spent down.
  It's the same verb as The Count at one color, the way Open Door is to Recruit a Member.
- **Crunch Time:** "Take your next 2 turns right away. +1 purple." Moved here from the center. This was **Cory's own idea** (Oct 15, 16:54: "Crunch Time should be a round I or round II space… trading a minion for back-to-back turns is a good, interesting trade-off"), before he kept it in the center to judge in play. In round II it takes two of Promote, Shake Down or Buy twice before anyone answers. Ring II stays open in round III, so it can still grab two anchors there. Text unchanged except the inherent +1.

### Round I (×3; each about 2–3; each still worth taking in round III)
- **Catch Up:** "+1 purple per minion you placed before this one this round. +1 purple." This is Cory's own Catch Up idea (Oct 15, 17:53: 1, 2 or 3 for your first, second or third minion; "take 2 now or hope it's still there for 3"). It replaces live Roll Call, which already counts this way. Round I 1/2/3; round III up to 5. *Side effect:* pays for waiting; a rival can take it to deny you.
- **Plan Ahead:** "+1 purple per minion you have left to place this round. +1 purple." The live ring II card, moved to ring I. Round I 3/2/1; round III up to 5. *Side effect:* pays for going early, which under fewest-first means the trailer (catch-up). It's Catch Up's mirror, so the purple player takes Plan Ahead early and Catch Up late, and a rival denies whichever is ripe.
- **Head Count:** "+1 purple per contest you lead right now. +1 purple." Round I 1–3 (few people hold much yet); round III 2–4. *Side effect:* reads rivals' piles; it pays the player who's already holding, which sets up the calls.

Clock In ("+4/+5/+6") and Rush Hour go. A "+1 purple × round" space would just copy Gather and Build Up.

### Contest
Most purple held (default). Calls skip purple's own contest, so purple contests once per round as usual. Purple held still matters for Clockmaker, Timekeeper and purple members' colored parts.

### How it touches the other colors through the board
- Every color with held-resource contests now has a second clock. Spending a color you lead is riskier while The Count or Early Count is open, so the table holds a little longer and cashes out later.
- Takers make calls sharper: steal right before a call to flip the lead. Doubling a stack right before a call does the same. Converters can pour into a color to win it at the moment of the call.
- Shoppers and member-buyers who spent down are what the purple player calls against. That's the counterweight to "members are where followers come from": the call rewards whoever held.
- Crunch Time's priority still touches everyone's best spaces.

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** holds purple for its contest and leads one or two other colors (theft, gifts, plain holding), takes Early Count in round II on the color a rival just spent, and times The Count in round III. It's a hoarder's color that plays by the clock, not a pile.
- **Rival:** feels it every turn The Count is open: "if I cash out my red now, Purple calls it." That's interaction through the board with no harm card. When a call pays a rival who leads, it's a gift, so the caller has to think.
- **Newcomer:** "this contest pays now" uses the round-end animation they already know. The hard part is reading who leads each rim. The game already shows that, but the readout has to be sharp.
- **2 vs 4 players:** at 2 players there's one other contest, so The Count is 7 at best (low). Purple isn't on the 2-player Classic table, so this matters only at custom tables. At 4 players it's strongest.
- **Rubric v3:** no hard nos. Walk-away passes in every round, the effect scales by round (the prize does), it isn't redundant, and it's simple. Risks: "score now" may feel like redesigning when scoring happens (rule 15, "not for now"). Contests gain weight (up to about 20 more followers a game at 4 players), which pulls against "members are where followers come from". Early Count is still open in round III (6 + 1 from a ring II space), which is strong. My honest guess: 55% yes, likely "changed" on numbers. He may cap calls at once per contest per round.
- **Weakest card:** Head Count. It pays the player who's already leading, a second payout on the same pile. I kept it because round I needs one space that points at holding before the calls arrive, and it's the only round I space here that reads rivals.

## Concept 2: Put It Off (wait, then crunch; Crunch Time stays in the center)

**Identity in one mechanic:** Purple pays you for placing late in the round on your own clock (Cory's Catch Up), and its round III is the deadline: Crunch Time, kept exactly as Cory left it. No space adds a minion, and only the center moves turns. This is the kernel Cory said "is there", rebuilt from the five facts in section 0: one clock (your own, fair in the snake), late payoffs in followers (fact 5), and one delayed space (round end) in place of a third counter.

### Round III (the burst)
- **Crunch Time (kept):** "Take your next 2 turns right away. +1 purple." Taken early in round III, it lands two other anchors before anyone answers: about +10 against what those two minions would otherwise get, almost all of it in denial. It also gives three buys in a row off the same row of the shop. Cory: keep it and judge in play. The only change is the inherent +1.

### Round II (×2)
- **Patience:** "+2 followers per minion you placed before this one this round. +1 purple."
  Round II 1/3/5/7 (about 4 on average, 7 on your last minion), next to Shake Down at about 5. Round III up to 9. Followers need no buy, so it works when the buy binds. *Interaction:* everyone's late minions want it, so it's sniped at the right moment, Bandwagon's "dynamic as the round goes on" without the seat bias.
- **Long Shot:** "+2 purple per rival with more followers than you. +1 purple."
  The trailer gets 7 at 4 players, middle seats 3–5, the leader 1. The trailer picks first in round II, so this is Cory's "catch up should be better" taken literally: catch-up keyed to a visible track. It isn't timing, so it rides on the theme's edge ("late bloomer").

### Round I (×3)
- **Catch Up:** Cory's idea, as in Concept 1. 1/2/3; up to 5 in round III. Pays the late minion.
- **Plan Ahead:** moved from ring II, as in Concept 1. 3/2/1; up to 5 in round III. Pays the early minion.
- **Night Shift:** "+1 purple. At round end, +1 purple × round." Round I 2, round II 3, round III 4. *Side effect:* the gain lands after every buy, right before contests. It's pure contest power this round and money next round, and in round III it's a late swing on the purple contest that rivals can see coming. Your minion on the space is the reminder; there's no memory. It shares Gather's numbers but differs in the one thing that matters: the gain can't be spent this round.

### Contest
Most purple held. Night Shift and Clockmaker feed it.

### How it touches the other colors through the board
- Purple's late spaces are open to everyone, and everyone's own late minion makes them ripe. Rivals take them to deny or to profit, so a purple space is a live question each lap.
- Long Shot and Late Riser push the trailer, which fits fewest-first.
- Crunch Time takes rivals' anchors in round III, and that's the sting.
- Night Shift moves the purple contest at round end, and rivals see it coming.

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** places the first minion of a round on Plan Ahead and the last on Catch Up or Patience, holds purple for the contest, and crunches in round III. It's readable and steady.
- **Rival:** cares about Patience and Catch Up only when their own late minion is up, and about Crunch Time once. Less table-wide pressure than Concept 1.
- **Newcomer:** "+1 per minion you placed before this one" is easy to read at a glance.
- **2 vs 4 players:** your own clock doesn't care about table size. Long Shot shrinks at 2 players (0 or 1 rival ahead, so 1 or 3).
- **Rubric v3:** no hard nos. It keeps Cory's two calls (Crunch Time, his Catch Up). The risk: three of its six spaces still count minions (Catch Up, Plan Ahead, Patience), and Cory's #92 set "Catch up, plan ahead, rush hour" side by side as a question. A "these are variations" no is likely on one of them. Long Shot is off-theme. My honest guess: 50%. He keeps Catch Up and Crunch Time and swaps one ring II space.
- **Weakest card:** Night Shift. It's the only non-counter in round I, but it's Gather's numbers with a delay. I kept it because the delay is real timing (the gain can't be spent this round) and it gives rivals something to read at round end.

## Recommendation
**Concept 1, The Count.** It answers the seat's question directly: Purple's timing makes every rival care, because it moves the moment the whole holding-vs-spending game is aimed at, and it needs no extra turns and no reordering beyond Cory's own Crunch Time.
It also keeps both of Cory's calls (his Catch Up idea, and Crunch Time, moved where he first wanted it).
If he wants Purple closer to the kernel, Concept 2's round II (Patience) and members (Latecomer, Rush Job, Night Watch) can drop into the anchor seat's design unchanged.

## Appendix: seeds (12), and why each was cut or kept
1. **Hold a space** (only you can place there until your next turn), tempo/space. Cut: a hold on one space is worth only the gain of the space you stood on (fact 2). It's Claim on the board, and Claim got "promote and ransom are just straight up way better".
2. **Book 2 anchors** in round III. Cut as a concept: it's Crunch Time's power without the reordering, but setup only on the turn you take it. Worth keeping as a fix if #99 (confusing snake turns) comes back.
3. **Fall Back** (your next turn moves to last; paid for it), tempo / Viticulture's wake-up chart. Cut: a fair gain needs a count of turns skipped, it's free on your last minion, and it reorders turns (#99).
4. **Cut In** (take your next turn right away, one turn). Cut: one turn moved adds no contested pick (fact 1), so it's worth only its own gain.
5. **Skip a rival** (the next rival places last this round), interaction. Cut: pure denial, it moves a rival's turn (non-intuitive), and it stings in round I.
6. **Call the order** (choose next round's turn order; the start-player space). Cut: a "next round" effect dies in round III (fact 3), it overlaps Front-runner, and it overrides the fewest-first catch-up (win-more in a leader's hands).
7. **Bank a minion** for next round, holding vs spending. Cut: dies in round III, and it's Overtime's "space for a space".
8. **Accumulating spaces** (an untaken purple space keeps a pile into next round), Agricola's shape. Cut: round I fills exactly, so it only works from round II to round III, and that's small.
9. **Stay late** (a minion stays on its space into next round and acts at round start). Cut: it repeats an action (copying is Green's), and it crosses rounds.
10. **Fast forward** ("your next action counts as one round later"). Cut: +1–2 on scaling spaces only, and you have to know which spaces scale.
11. **Night actions** (purple spaces resolve at round end), what-if-Purple-didn't-exist. Cut as a whole color: round III's end is the game's end, so the gains die (fact 5). One survives as Night Shift in Concept 2.
12. **A contest pays out now**, what the table lacks (a color that moves the moment everyone plays toward). **Kept → Concept 1.**

Also checked against the ❌ tags: no space charges you (Overtime), no out-of-turn minions, no extra placement in round I, no rescue clauses, no cost cuts, no Release, nothing remembered across rounds except what sits on the board.
