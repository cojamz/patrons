# Blue members: two decks (rework panel, 2026-10-21)

**All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.** Scope: Blue's members only. Blue's actions are as settled: round I churn 3 / flip out a color / peek the top 4 and reorder; round II Buy twice; round III Spree (buy 3 times) with +2 blue; every space +1 blue.

## 0. What the members have to work with

- **Blue is the poorest color in its own currency.** No Blue space gives a scaling gain: each gives +1 blue, and Spree gives 3. A Blue specialist takes maybe 5–6 Blue spaces a game, so ends up with about **7–8 blue a game**, against Red's Gather at 2/3/4. So Blue's colored parts have to stay at **1–2, with 3 for the top cards only.** Red-style 6+4 can't be paid. Across a typical haul of 4–5 Blue members, the decks below ask for about 8–9 blue: buying them eats the stack you'd hold for the contest, which is the dilemma we want.
- **Extra buys are cheap until round III** (pricing.md: resources run out first). A Blue member that only adds a buy is close to dead in round I. So each card that adds a buy also pays in another way (bodies, followers, first pick), or is priced for round III.
- **Blue is only on 3- and 4-player Classic tables.** At 3p the deck is 37 cards and nearly all of it shows. At 4p it's 49 cards, about 85% shows, more with churn. A 10-card color has 1.4–1.9 cards in a 7-card row. Every card below stands alone. Cards that count blue members are priced for 3–5 of them, plus multi-body cards (a member worth 2 counts as 2).
- **Cory's steer:** "member synergy", "rearrange the shop and then buy a bunch", "you can always buy twice", Recluse kept, recruiting a peeked card "awkward because they are in different phases", no blue from cards leaving between rounds, no cost cuts, no Release.

**Contest (one line, flagged):** move Blue to **most blue held**, like the other reworked colors. "Most blue members" pays the shopper twice for the same buy, and it hangs on which blue cards show up. Held blue makes every Blue member's colored part a real contest cost.

## 2. Deck A: "Every extra buy pays"

**Thesis:** Blue sells buys. These members make a turn with two, three or four buys the best turn on the board, and make each extra buy worth something even when you're short of money. Nothing in A depends on which colors show up in the row.

**What a round looks like for the specialist.** Round I: a Blue space, Plus-One or Allowance with the buy. Round II: Buy twice with Bulk Buyer is 2 buys and +1 follower. Insider means one of those buys was set up by your own peek. Round III: Spree with Shopaholic is 4 buys, +3 followers from Bulk Buyer and +3 blue. Scalper buries the round III targets you skip.

**Self-critique, Deck A**
- Specialist: a clear plan (buy small and often, then a big round III) that never needs a blue card to show up. Rival: sees the plan on the seat. Scalper and Insider are the only things that touch them, both mild. Newcomer: every card reads in one line.
- 3p: Shopaholic and Doorbuster nearly always show, so whoever is first to the row in round II gets the top bender. 4p: Bulk Buyer and Shopaholic together is a product combo (+1 follower every turn, about +6–8). Each is fine alone, and owning both is about 15–25%.
- Cory, rubric v3: Shopaholic is his idea (yes). Plus-One is the elegant one-shot shape (likely yes). Allowance is steady income (likely yes, he'll retune). Insider is the peek-recruit he liked (likely yes). **Bulk Buyer risks reading as a "+1 when…" drip.** It's on your own turn and on the color's verb, but he may call it small. Doorbuster: 50/50.
- The deck has three ways to buy more (Plus-One, Doorbuster, Shopaholic). They differ in when (arrival, round start, always), but a critic can call it one verb at three prices.
- Weakest card: **Scalper.** It's an optional prompt on every member buy, which costs a little on screen. Kept because it's the only card in A that touches rivals, and it grows with the thesis.

## 3. Deck B: "You set the row, you collect your color"

**Thesis:** Cory's first steer, "Blue is member synergy", made fair. The old Blue cards paid on blue members showing up by luck. Here Blue is the one color with tools to pick what comes up, so collecting blue members is skill. B's members do Blue's round I verbs on arrival with bodies, set the next row, and pay for owning blue.

**Self-critique, Deck B**
- Specialist: a real skill plan (peek, Tastemaker and Gatekeeper line up the blue cards, Plus-One and Spree grab them, Snob and Curator pay). Rival: Tastemaker and Gatekeeper visibly take cards away from them. That's interaction, and it can feel like being controlled. Newcomer: Tastemaker's round-start pick is the one moment that needs explaining.
- 3p: the pile is 37 cards, so Tastemaker sees a third of what's left each time. That's strong. 4p: blue is only 1.4 a row, so the collection leans on the tools. Without them, Snob and Curator are back to luck.
- Cory, rubric v3: "member synergy" is his word. But two of the five cards count blue members, the family the panel's strategy read says he soured on ("luck of the row"). That's a real chance of "this is the old Blue". Tastemaker passes the walk-away test only if he reads choosing the row as power. His "dig… so weak" says he may not. **Gatekeeper and Window Dresser are a near-variation pair** (Blue's two round I actions on cards).
- Weakest card: **Gatekeeper.** Kept because the flip denies a whole color, a different job from the churn's pick of 3. Cut it first.

## 4. Recommendation

**Deck A.** It does exactly "rearrange the shop and then buy a bunch" and "you can always buy twice", and it doesn't depend on which colors show up. Its cards all reward the thing Blue's actions do. B's better pieces (Tastemaker, Window Dresser) are worth showing Cory as swaps into A's open-ish slots (Scalper's, Entourage's) if he wants more row control. Pick the contest first (held blue), because every colored part above is priced for it.

## 5. What happens to the live ten

Keep: Recluse (as is), Snob (5 → 6). Back from the culled list: Talent Scout (B), Curator (B). Cut: Stylist, Connoisseur and Patroness (pay on blue showing up, and Stylist on rivals' turns); Planner, Underbidder and Sommelier (cost cuts); Social Climber (Release); Minimalist (the same condition as Recluse at round start; one lean card per deck is enough).

## 6. How these touch the other colors (generically)

- Blue members are cheap (all but Shopaholic cost 6 or less, most 2–4), so they're ransom targets and fuel for swaps that read cost gaps. Blue's cult is easy to sting, and its multi-body cards are the cheap ones to keep (you pay the cost).
- Arrival cards (Plus-One, Gatekeeper, Window Dresser) fire again when another player moves or free-recruits them (the Oct 18 rule), so they're mild theft magnets.
- Copying Buy twice or Spree gives a copier Bulk Buyer's value. Shopaholic is the best free round III recruit on the table.
- Insider and Tastemaker make the pile visible state. Everyone sees what Blue set up.

## Appendix: seeds (13), and why each was cut or kept

1. **Always buy twice** (shop/members). Kept: Shopaholic.
2. **Buy again on arrival** (members). Kept: Plus-One.
3. **Top of the pile is yours to buy** (shop). Kept: Insider. It fixes the peek-recruit phase problem.
4. **Pick the round's new 7 from the top 10** (shop, interaction). Kept: Tastemaker (B).
5. **Buy first at round start** (tempo). Kept: Doorbuster, flagged.
6. **+1 follower per buy after your first** (members). Kept: Bulk Buyer.
7. **Send a shop member to the bottom with each member you buy** (interaction). Kept: Scalper.
8. **Follower packs of any size** (holding vs spending). Cut: a convenience, not a decision, and it feeds the buy-followers-only seat (the guest designer cut it too).
9. **+1 blue per card you send to the bottom** (holding). Cut: it's the scaling-churn pitch still waiting on Cory for the actions, and a cousin of "blue when cards leave", which he called too strong.
10. **Leftover resources become followers at game end** (holding vs spending). Cut: you'd keep everything for round III's contests and still score it, which kills round III's hold-or-spend choice. Hoard territory, not the shop.
11. **Last Call: once per game, buy as many times as you like** (shop). Held as A's alternate if Recluse leaves. It's Spree on a card, and two buy-benders at the top of one deck is a variation.
12. **Mark a shop member only you can buy** (shop). Cut: Claim, ❌ "promote and ransom are just straight up way better".
13. **What if Blue didn't exist?** The table would lose the one big shopping turn and any say over what comes up. Both decks answer it, A with buys and B with the row. Also cut: buy before you place (fiddly, small); buy twice while you have the fewest members (a variation of Shopaholic, and Recluse already covers lean); +1 blue when a rival buys twice (a drip on rivals' turns); all your members count as blue (does nothing alone).
