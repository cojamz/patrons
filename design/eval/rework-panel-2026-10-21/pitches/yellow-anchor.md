# Yellow, anchor seat (actions and members)

All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.

Seat: anchor. Cory's way: round III first, then work back, keeping a Yellow you'd recognise (Top Up, Trade Seeds, Potluck, Rainmaker, Chefs). Sources: generator brief, brief.md, strategy-read.md, member-shape.md, pricing.md, deck-visibility.md, cory-judge.md rubric v3, live yellow.js, design/spheres/yellow.md.

## What has to change, and what has to stay

- **Why Yellow is weak at 2 players (#84).** Every big yellow gain is "+1 of each color" or "per full set". At 2 players that's 1 color, at 4 it's 3. Harvest, Top Up's ties, Branch Out, Bumper Crop, Peddler, Pilgrim, Gardener, Dyer, Weaver, Late Bloomers and Feastmaker all scale with the colors on the table. The fix: count something that doesn't depend on table size (your members, the round number, your own largest stack), and let the player choose where the color goes.
- **Why Yellow doesn't want members (#96).** No yellow space reads your cult except Potluck. Members are where followers come from now, so the color has to pay you for having them.
- **Top Up (#97)** grows by round. **Chefs** count as 2 members and cost a little less than Feastmaker's 10. **Rainmaker** costs more. Full sets go, and the contest is the default, **most yellow held**.
- **What Yellow is for in this economy.** Colored parts gate members, and held color scores contests. The color of your pile now matters, and Yellow is the one color that gets you colors you didn't take spaces for. That's the kernel I keep: Yellow gets you the colors you lack. Two ways to build it, below.

## Concept A: Your cult is your harvest

**Identity in one mechanic:** each of your members brings you resources of its own color. Yellow is the color of the mixed cult. Red moves members, Green copies spaces, Magenta takes, Blue works the shop. Yellow reads what you've already recruited and pays out in those colors, which gets you the colored parts for more members and a stake in those colors' contests.

### Actions

| Round | Space | Text | Taker walks away with |
|---|---|---|---|
| III | **Bumper Crop** | Each of your members brings you 2 of its color. +1 yellow. | 5–6 bodies: **11–13**, spread over the colors of your cult |
| II | **Potluck** (kept) | Each of your members brings you 1 of its color. +1 yellow. | 3–4 bodies in round II: **4–5**; in round III: 6–7 |
| II | **Feast** | +1 follower per member you have of another color. +1 yellow. | Round II: **4–5** (3–4 followers, no buy needed); round III: 6–7 |
| I | **Top Up** | +1 × round, one at a time, each to your fewest color. +1 yellow. | **2 / 3 / 4** |
| I | **Trade Seeds** | +2 yellow, then swap up to 2 × round of your resources for other colors. | **2**, plus a swap of 2 / 4 / 6 |
| I | **Harvest** | +1 yellow per 2 members you have (rounded up). +1 yellow. | Round I: **2** (1–2 members); round II: 3; round III: 4 |

Notes:
- "Members" counts bodies (a member worth 2 counts as 2), as everywhere. Colorless guests bring yellow (rule note, my fill-in).
- Trade Seeds keeps Cory's "+2 yellow, then swap" with the +1 inside the +2, as Gather's is. Now the swap grows instead of the gain, so it differs from Top Up in kind (Top Up adds new resources, Trade Seeds reshapes your pile) rather than out-gaining it.
- Round I siblings: Top Up fills your gaps (colored parts), Trade Seeds reshapes (contests), Harvest grows yellow with your cult (yellow contest). Similar totals, different side effects, and each still worth 4ish in round III.
- Round II against the bar: Promote is about 3.5 of value, Shake Down 3–5 plus the +1. Potluck and Feast walk away with 4–5. Feast changes what you own directly (followers that can't be stolen and don't use your buy). Potluck changes what you can buy next (colored parts in your cult's colors). Neither one is access or information.
- Bumper Crop is wanted beyond the specialist: any body-heavy cult (multi-body members from any deck) wants it, and the trailer picks first.

**Contest:** most yellow held. Yellow comes from the +1s, Harvest, and your own yellow members on Potluck and Bumper Crop. So a yellow cult with yellow members holds the contest, and a mixed cult spends its yellow. That's the hold-or-spend split.

**How it touches other colors (generically):** resources arrive in your members' colors, so a Yellow player turns up in other colors' contests and can afford their members' colored parts. Multi-body members from any deck are Bumper Crop fuel, which makes Yellow the natural partner or rival of a body-counting cult. Copiers copying Potluck or Bumper Crop get paid in their own members' colors. Member theft (take or ransom a member) hits a Yellow engine where it lives, so defense members from other decks matter to it. A Yellow player fed by its members spreads its pile, so half-a-stack theft takes less from it.

Every member that counts here counts **all** your members, not yellow ones. So no card needs another yellow card to show up, which fits deck visibility (a committed player owns about 3–5 of a color at 4 players).

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** buys two or three cheap mixed members in round I (Sprouts, any cheap body), takes Potluck or Feast in round II, and Bumper Crop pays 11–13 in round III. It plays as an engine, and Yellow finally wants members.
- **Rival:** a body-heavy cult will grab Bumper Crop as the trailer. Its owner's Mob-style cards make it 15–20, the top of the band or over it. Flag: if it runs hot, the fix is "1 + 1 per member", not a cap.
- **Newcomer:** "each of your members brings you 2 of its color" reads in one look. Feast is one line.
- **2 vs 4 players:** the same arithmetic at both, since it counts members. This fixes #84 at the root.
- **Cory, rubric v3:** passes the hard nos. The color fits: the mechanic is Yellow's live Potluck promoted. Walk-away is in band, scales by round (bodies grow), opens a cult that plays differently (wide and mixed, the opposite of a one-color counting cult). Likely objections: round II is two gains, not an ownership change ("Potluck is just resources"). Bumper Crop with a big Red-style cult is "too strong". It sits near a White pitch built on "your members pay you".
- **Weakest card:** Fieldworker. It's a known template on a new verb, so it's the first cut. I kept it because the specialist needs one follower stream that isn't a scorer.

## Concept B: Even out

**Identity in one mechanic:** Yellow copies the height of your tallest stack into your other colors. All game you choose between going tall (one stack, a contest lead, a theft target) and going wide (colored parts, safety). In round III Yellow turns tall into two-tall. The verb is Rainmaker's, on the board, rewritten so it never counts table colors.

### Actions

| Round | Space | Text | Taker walks away with |
|---|---|---|---|
| III | **Even Out** | Raise one of your colors to match your largest. +1 yellow. | Largest 8–10, target 0–2: **9–11**, plus a likely second contest lead (worth up to 6) |
| II | **Potluck** (kept) | Each of your members brings you 1 of its color. +1 yellow. | **4–5** |
| II | **Branch Out** (new text) | Gain half your largest color (rounded up), in any other colors. +1 yellow. | Largest 5: **4**; in round III, largest 10: 6 |
| I | **Top Up** | +1 × round, one at a time, each to your fewest color. +1 yellow. | **2 / 3 / 4** (wide) |
| I | **Harvest** | +1 × round to your largest color. +1 yellow. | **2 / 3 / 4** (tall) |
| I | **Trade Seeds** | +2 yellow, then swap up to 2 × round of your resources for other colors. | **2**, plus a swap of 2 / 4 / 6 |

Notes:
- The round I pair is the color's question in two spaces: Top Up goes wide, Harvest goes tall. They're mirror images rather than one beating the other. The risk is that they read as "variations" (flagged below).
- Branch Out spreads, Even Out concentrates. Both read your own largest stack, which is visible to everyone at any table size.
- At 2 players, "one of your colors" and "any other colors" give the same totals as at 4.
- With the old Gold-style hoards of 20, Even Out is +20. The old spec's Cornucopia ("raise every color to your largest") was "far too strong with Gold piles". This is one color, and it's pitched for the reworked economy, where the largest stack in round III is about 6–12.

**Contest:** most yellow held.

**How it touches other colors:** any hoarder wants Even Out, and hoarders trail on followers (their resources aren't converted yet), so they pick first in round III. It's catch-up aimed at holders. A matched second stack can snipe another color's contest, and anyone can see which one by looking at your pile. Two tall stacks are two targets for half-a-stack theft. Copying Even Out is strong for any player with a hoard.

### Deck sketch (10)
The seven core cards from A (Sprouts, Go-Between, Gossip, Diplomat, Chefs, Granary, Rainmaker), plus:

| Card | Cost | Worth | Text | Why this price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explorer (kept text) | 2+2 | 1 | Whenever you take a space outside Yellow, +1 yellow. | Was 8, which is too dear for a +1 drip. At 4 it's about 1.3 bought in round I. On your own turn, and it rewards placing everywhere |
| Late Bloomers (reworked) | 2+2 | 2 | At round start, if no one has fewer followers, +3 of your fewest color. | Catch-up keyed to visible state. Fires about once, so 3 + 2 followers for 4. No longer "+1 of each color" |

Granary is B's natural scorer: Even Out makes your second-largest equal your largest, so it pays 8–10 to a committed player and 4–6 to anyone else.

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** builds one tall stack with Harvest and holds it for that contest, widens with Top Up or Branch Out to pay colored parts, and in round III matches the tall stack into a second color. Two contests and a big final buy.
- **Rival:** every hoarder wants Even Out, so it's never dead. Reading a rival's largest stack tells you what it's worth to them.
- **Newcomer:** "raise one color to match your largest" is one look. The tall-or-wide question is easy to see on your own pile.
- **2 vs 4 players:** the same at both.
- **Cory, rubric v3:** passes the hard nos. It's in band, relative ("match" is his "open or relative" family), and he likes Rainmaker. Likely objections: it's close to any "double one stack" round III a reworked Gold might take, since the arithmetic is the same when the raised color is empty. Members matter less than in A: only Potluck, Chefs and Go-Between say "members", so #96 is only half answered. Top Up and Harvest may read as one template pointed two ways.
- **Weakest card:** Scarecrow, a narrow cousin of a full theft block. It's the first cut. I kept it because a tall-stack color needs a defense, and Rainmaker isn't one.

## Recommendation: Concept A
1. It answers all four of Cory's Yellow notes with one mechanic: it wants members, it plays the same at 2 and 4 players, Top Up scales, and Chefs' 2 bodies actually pay.
2. Its identity (your members pay out their colors) clashes with no locked color. B's round III risks colliding with Gold's likely "grow a stack" direction.
3. A keeps Yellow's recognisable spaces (Potluck, Top Up, Trade Seeds, Harvest), and Bumper Crop is Potluck at double, so the burst is what the color builds toward. If Cory wants B's tall-or-wide question too, Branch Out's new text can stand in for Feast.

## Appendix: seeds (12), one line each, and why each was cut or kept
1. **Members bring their colors, ×2 in round III** (shop and members): developed as A.
2. **Raise one color to your largest** (holding vs spending): developed as B.
3. **Pour: move everything into one color, plus half again** (holding vs spending, contest snipe): cut. Conversion overlaps Trade Seeds and the guests' Launderer, and the walk-away is mostly position.
4. **Rainmaker as the round III space** (raise every color to your yellow): cut. It scales with table colors (1 at 2 players, 3 at 4).
5. **Keep full sets, fix the yields by round:** cut. Sets stay table-dependent and opaque, and variety scoring was a no on Oct 9.
6. **Tempo: yellow spaces pay double once every color on the wheel has a minion:** cut. It depends on table size and asks you to track the board.
7. **Space: bonus for placing on a color you hold none of:** cut. It's rescue-shaped and counts colors. Kept in spirit as Explorer, a member.
8. **Shop: recruit a member of a color you have none of, paying in any colors:** cut. It's your normal buy plus the guests' Freelancer perk, near-zero walk-away (the "claim" shape Cory rejected).
9. **Rivals: each rival gives you 1 of their largest color:** cut. That's theft, Magenta's job, and it scales with player count.
10. **Rivals, giving: gain 2 × round of a color, and rivals with none get 1:** cut. That's giving territory, and "everyone else" costs grow with players.
11. **Hold vs spend: your smallest color scores as followers at round end:** cut. "Smallest of all colors" is easier at 2 players (table scaling inverted), and as opaque as full sets.
12. **What would the table miss without Yellow?** The only color that gets you colors you didn't take spaces for: a way into a contest or a colored part you weren't set up for. Seed: "contest ties pay you in full". Too small for an identity, so it's kept as a member (Diplomat).

Also considered and dropped while filtering: "return a member to the shop for its cost in any colors" for round II (Release in disguise), and "recruit free a member costing up to the colors you hold" (Red's verb, and it scales with the table).
