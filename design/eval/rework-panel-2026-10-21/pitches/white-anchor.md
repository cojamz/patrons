# White, anchor seat (rework panel, 2026-10-21)

**All card texts and numbers are Claude's fill-ins, not approved.** No sims, no games, no code. Arithmetic from the live rules (contests 2/4/6 on held color, half on a tie, zero never wins; 3/4/5 minions; 1-for-1 follower packs of 1/5/10, one buy a turn) and the panel's pricing.md. Estimates are marked (guess).

Seat: design the way Cory does. Round III first, then work back. A new identity is expected.

## Where I started

- **What dies.** Every live White space trades or prints followers: Cheap Seats and Your Call hand followers from a space, Sell High and Dividend need "followers you bought or won" (Cory: "what's a loose follower?"), All In is the follower buy without the buy. The loose-follower rule goes away in both concepts below, and so do Sell High, Dividend, Your Call, Cheap Seats, All In, Dealer, Pawnbroker, Liquidator, Reinvestor (a cost cut), Lender ("a little too complicated") and Broker.
- **What's open for White.** Under the Oct 20 frame the core tension is "hold or spend": what you hold at round end scores that color's contest, what you spend becomes members and followers. Five contests now run on held color, and no color plays the contests themselves except by stealing from a pile (Magenta). That is the biggest unclaimed lever left. The other one is the members themselves as a place followers sit.
- **What the rest of the panel already holds** (read before choosing, to avoid three seats on one idea): White outsider takes the follower gap (catch up to the leader) and tying a leader; Purple anchor takes "your members' round-start effects happen now" (the strategist's lean for White); Yellow takes members bringing resources and recoloring piles; the guests take Tiebreaker, Spendthrift and Cory's Launderer; Gold takes doubling. So I took the two grounds nobody holds: **taking a contest's prize early**, and **followers that sit on your members**.

## Two words used below (rules, not card names)
- **You lead a contest** when you hold more of that color than every rival. A tie isn't a lead. Holding none never leads. (The rims already show this.)
- **Take a prize early**: you take that contest's prize now (2 / 4 / 6 by round), and that contest won't score at this round's end. Next round it's a normal contest again. On screen the rim greys out with your mark; on a table, a token on the rim.

## Concept A: White takes the prize early (recommended)

**Identity in one mechanic:** White lets you take a contest's prize the moment you lead it, then spend the stack you were holding for it. Every other color pays you to hold to round end; White lets you have the prize and the spending both, paid for in minions. Red moves members, Green copies, Magenta takes, Blue works the shop and the number of buys; White settles the contests.

**The cult it makes:** build a lead early in the round, cash it, spend the freed stack on members the same round. Its opposite is the patient hoarder who sits on piles to round end.

**The table's new question:** "Can I hold this lead until someone with a White minion takes it?" A narrow lead is no longer safe until round end, and a narrow trailer can lose a prize they were about to win. Nothing is ever taken from anyone.

### Actions (every space ends +1 white)

**Round III, the burst**
- **"Take now the prize of every contest you lead. They won't score at round end. +1 white."**
- Specialist: leads white (every White space gives +1 white) and usually one more color at their first round III turn: 6–12, +1. The real swing is bigger: in round III, winning a contest normally costs the stack you hold to the end (dead at game end). Here you take the 6 and still spend the stack on your next buys. Marginal gain over not taking it is about the smaller of the prize and the stack, per contest: **about 8–13 all in** (guess). In band.
- Anyone else: a hoarder leading two piles gets the same. A player leading nothing gets 1 and the rim told them so before they placed. Holders tend to trail on followers, and the trailer picks first in round III, so this anchor goes to whoever held, not to whoever bought followers. That's the catch-up working with the core tension, not against it.
- Prize pool stays fixed: an early prize replaces that contest's round-end prize. No new followers enter the game, which keeps the arithmetic honest at every table size.

**Round II, the bridge (×2; must change what you own or how much you buy)**
- **Cash a lead:** "Take now the prize of one contest you lead. It won't score at round end. +1 white."
  - Round II: 4 followers + 1 white = **5**, plus the freed stack (a member bought this round instead of a pile held to round end). That beats Promote's free upgrade of up to 2 cost (about 3–4 with its +1) and sits with Shake Down (a member of cost ≤5 or its cost, about 4–6). Round III: **7**, still a strong pick for the second leader after the anchor goes. It's the one-versus-every pattern he accepted in Open Door → Recruit a Member and Encore → Greatest Hits.
- **Take the lead:** "Choose a color you don't lead: gain enough to lead it by 1 (at most 5). +1 white."
  - Round II leaders hold about 3–5, you 0–2: you gain 2–4, +1 white, and you're now the leader of a 4-prize: **about 5–6** (guess). Round III: 4–5 resources and a lead on a 6-prize: 7–9. Relative to the board, so it grows by itself.
  - The two round II spaces make a two-turn plan with risk in between: take a lead, then cash it next turn (or with Cash a lead's twin in round III). A rival can top up past you, or take the cash space first.

**Round I (×3; equal-ish gain, different side effects, all still worth taking in round III)**
- **Pick a fight:** "Gain 1 × round of one color you choose. +1 white." 2 / 3 / 4. Side effect: builds a lead anywhere, or pays a member's colored part. The flexible one.
- **Even odds:** "+1 white × round. This round, contests you tie for the lead pay you in full. +1 white." 2 / 3 / 4, plus about +1 / +2 / +3 when a tie happens (ties are common in small held piles). The minion on the space shows the effect, like Lie Low's "this round". Side effect: you can hold to *match* a leader instead of beating them.
- **Sit tight:** "+1 white per 3 resources you hold. +1 white." Round I (holding 3–5) **2**; round II (6–8) 3; round III (9–15) 4–6. Side effect: pays the holder before the cash-in. Flag: Gold's anchor seat has "Gain half your gold" as a hold payoff; mine counts every color. If Gold keeps its version, swap this for "+1 white per contest you lead. +1 white." (on White's verb, but scales less).

| Round | Space | Taker walks away with (guess) |
|---|---|---|
| I | Pick a fight / Even odds / Sit tight | 2 / 2–3 / 2 (round III: 4 / 4–7 / 4–6) |
| II | Cash a lead / Take the lead | 5 + a freed stack / 5–6 |
| III | Every lead now | 8–13 |

**Contest:** most white held (the Oct 15 default; live is still "most white members"). White's own contest is the easiest one for the White player to lead and cash, which is the point: it teaches the verb on the color itself.

**How it touches the other colors, through the board**
- Every color's specialist can use White: lead your own color, take its prize early on a White space, then spend that color on its members' colored parts the same round. So White's round II and III spaces are wanted beyond the White player, which the strategy read asks of every anchor.
- Leaders need a margin, not just the most, or a White minion settles their contest in someone else's favor. The whole table reads the rims more.
- Theft: a stack cashed early is safe from a half-a-stack steal before round end; a steal before the cash breaks the lead. Copying: copying "Cash a lead" or the anchor pays the copier's own leads (designed for two players, as every round II/III space must be). Member movers: an arrival card that takes a prize (Magistrate, Crown below) fires again for whoever moves or takes it. Extra buys: a freed stack is fuel for buying twice or three times.
- 2 players: two contests, so one early prize is half a round's prizes. Swingy but plain to read. 4 players: four contests, about one lead per player on average (contests per player is constant, because colors = players), so the anchor holds its value at every size.

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** round I, Pick a fight into a second color while every White space builds white; buy Bailiff or Clerks. Round II, Take the lead, then Cash a lead on white or the new color and spend the stack on Magistrate. Round III, trailing because they held, they pick first and take the anchor for two prizes, then spend everything. It reads as a plan and every step is visible.
- **Rival:** loses a narrow lead's prize once or twice a game, and learns to lead by 2 or buy Holdout. A Red or Gold specialist gets a new tool: lock your own contest and spend down. That's interaction through the board with no theft.
- **Newcomer:** one new rule ("take a prize early") on top of contests they already see on the rims. The anchor's text is plain.
- **2 players:** sharp. Two contests, so the anchor is up to 13 and a round II cash is half the round. At 4: steadier, one lead each on average.
- **Rubric v3:** hard nos clear (nothing to pay, all on your turn, no rescue, nothing carried between rounds except a mark on a rim that clears at round end). Walk-away passes in every round (followers now). Scales with the round through the 2/4/6 prize itself. A new way to win (16): the spend-early cult against the hoarder. Risks: he may want the round-end contest to still score (I chose "won't score" so no followers are added and the stack's freedom is the price; "still scores" would add up to 6 per contest and pay leaders twice). He may also call Take the lead a cousin of the outsider's tie spaces. Prediction: ↔, shape kept, numbers his, about 55% (guess).
- **Weakest card: Arbiter.** "Which pile counts where" is the hard-to-show worry the strategy read raised. I kept it because it's one choice made at a visible moment (round end), and it gives the White pile a reason to exist after you've cashed your leads. If he finds it fiddly, Clerks takes the slot and a second plain body costs nothing.
- **Engine and screen cost (for the foreman, not Cory):** a "taken early" flag per contest per round, and a rim state for it. No other base rule changes.

## Concept B: White puts followers on your members

**Identity in one mechanic:** White's spaces put followers onto your member cards. They stay on the card and go wherever it goes: swapped, taken, ransomed, or swapped into the shop, where whoever recruits it gets them. "Members are where followers come from" (Oct 20) made literal, with risk attached. Followers on a card are followers, not bodies (they don't make a member count as more members), so they don't multiply member-counting cards.

**The cult it makes:** a few members you protect, piled with followers. It's tall where Red is wide, and it gives every member mover on the table a target worth reaching for.

### Actions (every space ends +1 white)
- **Round III:** "Put 2 followers on each of your members. +1 white." 4–6 member cards: **8–12, +1**. A wide cult with 8 cards gets 16, but a wide cult usually leads on followers and picks last in round III, so the anchor is gone by then (snake order is the check).
- **Round II, spread:** "Put 1 follower on each of your members. +1 white." 3–4 cards: **4–5**; round III 5–7.
- **Round II, pile on:** "Choose a member of yours: put on it as many followers as it's worth. +1 white." A worth-1 card gets 1, Shareholder 3, a worth-5 capstone 5: **2–6**. Rewards the multi-body members Cory keeps pushing up. The weak one of the pair.
- **Round I:** "Put 1 follower × round on one of your members. +1 white." (2 / 3 / 4, needs a member) · "+1 white × round. +1 white." (2 / 3 / 4, the plain one) · "+1 white, and +1 per member of yours with followers on it." (round I 1–2, round III 4–6; grows with how you've played).

**Contest:** most white held. **Touches the board:** member movers and takers get juicier targets; a defense card matters more; copying the spread or the anchor pays the copier's own cult. Works the same at 2 and 4 players (it counts your own cards).

### Self-critique
- **Specialist:** plays well from round II, poorly in round I: the first round I placement has no member to put followers on (Open Door's and Even Swap's problem too; "taking a space you can't use is on you", but round I fills by rule).
- **Rival:** a Red or Magenta player loves this White player. That's interaction, but it can feel like White builds for everyone else.
- **Rubric v3:** walk-away passes (followers now); simple to say and to show (a number on the card). But it's "+N followers per member" in a new coat: kin to the member-counting scorers and Yellow's members-as-income, and three of its spaces are "put followers on members" at different sizes ("not thinking broadly enough", Oct 18). Prediction: ❌ or ↔, about 35% (guess).
- **Weakest card:** Sanctuary, a conditional copy of Loyalists. Kept to show the defense slot this cult needs.

## Recommendation
**A, White takes the prize early.** It's the one lever left unclaimed in the Oct 20 economy: it lives on the hold-or-spend tension instead of beside it, it touches every color through the rims without taking anything, and it scales by round through the contest prize itself.
B is the honest alternative on the member side, but it reads as "+N followers" and is thin in round I.
If Cory wants White to also catch up or tie (the outsider's concepts), Take the lead is the space to swap.

## Appendix: seeds (12) and why each was cut or kept
1. **Take a contest's prize early** (holding vs spending): kept, A.
2. **White counts in every contest** (holding): as a space it pays only at round end, so it fails the walk-away test in round III; survives as one card (Arbiter, one contest only).
3. **Score every contest now, for the whole table** (rivals): pays rivals too and makes the taker a kingmaker. The "every contest you lead" form is A's anchor.
4. **Half your gap to the follower leader** (rivals, catch-up): the outsider's concept A, and "fewest followers" already sits on Underdog, Desperado, Late Bloomers and Late Riser. Left to that seat.
5. **Your members' round-start effects happen again** (members): the strategist's lean; Purple's anchor seat built it (Fast Forward, Wake Up). Thin fuel in reworked decks anyway.
6. **Followers on your members** (members): kept, B.
7. **Each member worth its cost** (members): sized by your priciest member, like Show Off ("trash", Oct 17) and Mime ("doesn't love"). Cut.
8. **A vault: set resources aside to grow** (holding): doubling is Gold's ground, and a stored pile is tracking. Cut.
9. **Your follower buy counts double this turn** (shop/buy): Penny Pincher's and Double Down's shape, and a follower-price cut. Cut.
10. **Pick first next round** (tempo): carried between rounds, and Front-runner owns it. Cut.
11. **Spaces that protect** (space): Lie Low, Loyalists, Agents and Bodyguard exist; nothing to walk away with. Cut.
12. **What if White didn't exist?** Today the table would miss nothing: follower trading is a second follower buy. What it lacks is a way out of the hold-or-spend squeeze that costs a minion, not a theft. That's A.
