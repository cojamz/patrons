# Lessons: design (see README.md for the format)

- 2026-10-20 · Cory found Claude's pitches "not good and not thoughtful", from someone who doesn't understand the heart of the game · ground every pitch in strategy.md and the pitch ledger, go wide then cut to the 2-3 furthest apart, filter against his past no's, critique by how a round feels · /brainstorm
- 2026-10-21 · Overnight rework panel: designers all reached for contest timing because the brief stressed contests, and a "deck of 10" slot template made seven decks look alike; the first judge test leaked its answers · name the brief's emphasis and police herding with an architect critic; give principles not slots; blind-test judges with answers kept out of the prompt · /rework-panel
- 2026-10-21: Cory on the overnight color picks: "i don't think you have the hang of this quite yet. though tiebreaker was a good idea". Magenta picks "look awful"; guest prices too high and too flat. Fix: member-principles.md now requires a wide price spread and a cheaper starting point; the panel's picks are pitches, not near-final.
- 2026-10-21: Pricing model priced every card at its peak round with one value ratio, so decks came out high and flat (Cory: "everything is roughly the same cost"). Fix: Pricing v2 shapes each deck's price curve first (1-3 / 4-6 / 7-10), prices for the round you want it bought in, reads his "too cheap" as one step, no neutral surcharge.
- 2026-10-21: Cory: "the color rework process didn't come up with good enough examples… needs a better process that understands the strategy and balancing more." Panel retired; the 'Better design process' thread owns it. Index of what exists: design/eval/rework-panel-2026-10-21/README.md.
- 2026-10-21 · Cory: his own members "aren't priced the best", so a model fitted to his prices (and the panel's value model before it) can't be the standard · concepts first, strong to weak with counters, his cut, then sim sweeps at 2/3/4 seats set the prices · design/member-process.md, /rework-members
- 2026-10-22: A round II "smaller Crunch Time" pitch drew "do you understand this game at all?". Fix: never pitch a weaker copy of a color's round III action for an earlier round; earlier spaces build toward the burst, they don't preview it.
- 2026-10-22: Five White round III shapes went out without a turn-by-turn check; two failed on inspection (a doubled follower recruit is below Spree and feeds buy-followers-only; a flat gain for everyone isn't catch-up). Fix: before pitching, play each idea through one real turn at 2 and 4 players against just placing that minion elsewhere, and cut what fails.
- 2026-10-22: Claude called White's "exchange all white for followers" no better than a recruit at 1 for 1 and pushed 2 per white; Cory: "2 is way too many. the idea is you can have a lot of white and go above 10". The follower recruit caps at 10 and uses the turn's one recruit, so an uncapped, recruit-free cash-in is the value. Fix: when judging any exchange, check it against all three limits on the follower recruit (1 for 1, 10 at a time, one recruit a turn) before changing a rate.
- 2026-10-22: Claude pitched "this turn your follower recruit has no limit"; Cory: the follower recruits are the 1, 5 and 10 cards in the shop, set by design, with no mechanism for another size. Fix: a space may change how often or with what you recruit followers, never the follower cards themselves; picture the actual card on screen before pitching anything about follower buys.
- 2026-10-22: Cory on the new /brainstorm: "the skill is missing like the overarching strategy/balance. just saying what NOT to do isn't enough to spur thoughtful creative design". Fix: the skill's design-core.md (the arcs, each color's fantasy and rivals, why his favorite spaces landed, generative shapes and prompts) is read with the kill list, and ideas start from the shapes, not from the don'ts.
- 2026-10-23: Cory: "why are you doing these so piecemeal?" after one-idea pitches for a single slot. Fix: every open slot gets a /brainstorm batch of about five, strong to weak, theme line first, never one idea at a time.
