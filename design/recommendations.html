Rebalance, don't revamp

The core is solid. Most of the confusion comes from scoring, plus about five rules that take more to learn than they give back.

1. Scoring: every point printed, only goes up

Today you can earn Favor about 20 ways, at 4 different times, and lose it about 10 ways. Nobody can look at the board and say "I'm 4 behind, here's how I catch up."

Recommend

Every point comes from a number you can see, like Splendor. Power cards show 0 to 3 Favor. Each god's VP shop becomes one visible offering. Favor never goes down.

Aurum offering: 4 gold → 3 Favor · Card: "Gilded Ledger", +1 gold each round · 2 Favor

  • Cut: the 4 hidden Favor conditions, Favor-stealing, negative Favor, and score multipliers.
  • Keep: the engine. Cards still power your drafting. They also show their worth.
  • Optional: one bonus per god, like "own 2 of my cards = +3".

2. Gold is the only real strategy

I re-ran 300 games with the strong AI today. The Gold god produces 71% of all Favor at 2 players and 45% at 4. The winner is usually just whoever hoarded the most gold. Printed scoring fixes most of this on its own, because gold stops being a scoring engine.

Also lopsided: Green-heavy players win 68% at 2 players. Yellow-heavy players won 0 of 7 games, and Black-heavy players won 1 of 28 at 4 players.

3. Black loses its point, so give it a new one

Without Favor-stealing, Black needs a clear job. I'd make it the god of taking resources: one clean "take 2" action and a shop that blocks or bumps a rival's worker. That keeps the conflict and avoids score swings.

4. Cut the hidden-state rules

  • Champions. Six different passives, some hidden. Replace them with "pick 2 starting resources".
  • Repeat family. Seven Green variants. Merge them into one Echo: copy any action on the board.
  • "Next time" buffs (double next gain, next repeat twice, Haggle). Make them happen right away, or cut them.
  • Cards that fire on other players' turns. They're surprises you can't plan around. Cut them.

5. The game is decided in one late burst

Round 3 hands out 60–65% of all Favor. The player in last place after round 1 wins only 13% of 4-player games, where a fair share would be 25%. Rounds 1 and 2 feel like they don't matter, and then the score explodes.

Recommend

Spread scoring evenly across rounds, using offerings that pay steadily rather than round-3 mega-actions. Keep turn order as the comeback: the player in last place picks first.

Where that lands

Same loop on the wheel: draft actions, gain resources, buy at a god you visited. A new player learns about 10 things instead of 26, and can always see who's winning and why.
